- Every LLM generation batch logs token spend under **Generation cost** (SPEC.md section 8).

| 2026-10-02 | Rout spreads panic to friendly armies in the same town, in the same tick | COMBAT.md section 6 says routing troops "spread panic", and that is the part of rout that makes it matter: one army breaking should be able to break the army beside it. The three options were a direct hit on every other army of the same side in the town (chosen), a persisted flag with the effect applied next tick, or no propagation at all. The same-tick version was chosen because the cost is bounded by the number of armies in one town, which `run` has already enumerated, and because the effect is a cause-log row citing the rout, so the Why panel walks from a broken army to the army that broke beside it. The next-tick version is a state machine with its own flag, its own recovery rule, and its own way to be got wrong, in exchange for a one-day delay nobody would notice. The no-propagation version drops the mechanic the document names. The rout flag itself is derived from morale rather than latched, so an army that is fed and rested back above the threshold fights again and nothing has to clear anything. | `services/simulation/internal/systems/battle/battle.go` (`broken`, `markRouted`, `fleeMen`, `panicSpread`), `internal/model/entities.go` (`Party.Routed`), `internal/model/fields.go`, `internal/model/access.go`, `config/balance.toml` (`[battle]` rout keys) |
| 2026-10-02 | A defeat breaks an army, not a broken army losing a fight | The rout threshold is compared against the loser's morale *after* the defeat's morale hit. A party already at or below the threshold is excluded from the fight entirely, so it never lost anything and cannot break from losing it, and under the alternative reading rout would be unreachable in the whole simulation. What breaks an army is the defeat taking it the last step over the line, which is also the reading COMBAT.md section 6 describes. The consequence is that an ordinary defeat does not rout, and that is the intent. | `services/simulation/internal/systems/battle/battle.go` (`run`), `config/balance.toml` (`battle.rout_morale_threshold`) |
| 2026-10-02 | The formation matchup table is antisymmetric; the terrain table is not | The matchup is indexed attacker then defender and the cells of an unordered pair multiply to 1, because a matchup is a relative advantage and exactly one side of a pairing can be favoured. A directional table would let both cells read above 1, and then meeting would strengthen both armies and the table could not change the outcome between equal armies. The terrain table has no such structure and must not be given one: the ground does not take sides, both armies stand on it, and the same cell is read for each. One consequence is arithmetic rather than a design choice and is pinned by a test: between two armies of the same template the terrain term cancels in the ratio, so ground decides fights between unlike armies and cannot decide a fight between like ones. | `services/simulation/internal/config/validate.go`, `internal/config/config.go` (`Battle.FormationBonus`, `TerrainCombat`), `config/balance.toml` (`[battle]`, `[terrain_combat]`), `services/simulation/internal/systems/battle/battle.go` (`matchupBonus`, `terrainBonus`, `situational`) |
| 2026-10-02 | A battle's ground is the town's own terrain, not the terrain of the nearest road | `model.Town` has carried a `Terrain` field since the entity was written and the world generator decides one for every settlement, but nothing copied it onto the town and no registered field or accessor could reach it, so the number was generated and discarded. Using it is one lookup, adds no fourth copy of the nearest-route scan the template, march, and attrition systems each keep (whose "three copies are deliberately identical" comment would otherwise become a lie), and it answers the question the scan gets wrong: a town on a river plain with one mountain road to it would otherwise fight as mountain ground. | `services/simulation/internal/worldgen/worldgen.go`, `internal/model/fields.go` (`town_terrain`), `internal/model/access.go`, `services/simulation/internal/systems/battle/battle.go` (`terrainOf`) |
| 2026-10-02 | A landed lord may deal in coin but not in goods | A `Leader` holds `Money` and `Gold` and no cargo (`internal/model/entities.go`), so the only holds a barter line can touch are the party's cargo and the town's stock — which are the two ends of the same line. Coin crosses between two purses and needs no carrier, so a lord with no party in the field can still deal; goods cannot, in either direction, because the party is where they land. Allowing the arrival into the town's stock is not a smaller rule, it is a broken one: it makes the town both ends of the line, and `mergeWrites` sums the two writes into no change, so the player pays coin for goods that never move. | `services/simulation/internal/systems/barter/barter.go` (`Validate`, `applyOrder`, `stageLine`) |
