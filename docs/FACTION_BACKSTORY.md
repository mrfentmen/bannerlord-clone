# Faction Backstory: The Fallen Republic

## The Premise

Ten years ago, the Federal government collapsed. Not in a single dramatic
moment — it rotted from the inside, then broke all at once. The President
was assassinated, Congress dissolved into bickering regional blocs, and
within eighteen months there was no functioning Federal authority west
of the Mississippi or south of the Mason-Dixon.

What remained of the Federal military split into three rival commands,
each claiming to be the legitimate successor. They've been fighting each
other — and everyone else — ever since.

## The Three Federal Successors (the "Empire")

These are the strongest factions on paper. They have the best equipment,
the most training, the old Federal stockpiles. They're also the most hated.
Everyone remembers what the old regime did — the drafts, the seizures,
the drone strikes on American cities. Nobody wants the Feds back in charge.

### Atlantic Corridor — Eastern Federal Command
**Leader:** General Elena Vasquez
**Claim:** "We hold the capital. We ARE the government."
**Based on:** Southern Empire (Rhagaea) — the legitimists

Vasquez controls DC and the Eastern seaboard. She claims continuity of
government — same flag, same constitution, same chain of command. The
problem: nobody elected her, and her "emergency powers" look a lot like
martial law. She's got the best navy and the deepest bunkers, but she's
surrounded by enemies and her supply lines are stretched thin.

**Why they're hated:** They taxed the breadbasket states to feed DC during
the collapse. The Midwest hasn't forgotten.

### Great Lakes Union — Northern Federal Command
**Leader:** General Marcus Thorne
**Claim:** "The Senate will choose the next President. Not generals."
**Based on:** Northern Empire (Lucon) — the institutionalists

Thorne holds the industrial heartland — Chicago, Detroit, the factories.
He's a constitutionalist who insists civilian rule must be restored through
a rump Congress. His territory is the richest in manufacturing, but he's
fighting on three fronts and his "allies" in the Senate undercut him
constantly.

**Why they're hated:** They seized the food distribution network "for the
war effort" and never gave it back. People starved while warehouses filled.

### Pacific Compact — Western Federal Command
**Leader:** General David Reyes
**Claim:** "The Army keeps the peace. The Army decides."
**Based on:** Western Empire (Garios) — the military populists

Reyes commands the West Coast and the Pacific fleet. He's a soldier's
soldier — popular with the rank and file, terrifying to civilians. He
promises order through strength and has the firepower to back it up.
His territory is wealthy (tech, ports, agriculture) but he's locked in
a grinding war with the mountain factions.

**Why they're hated:** They ran the internment camps during the collapse.
Whole towns disappeared into "processing centers."

## The Three Regional Powers (the "Barbarians")

These rose from the wreckage. They're not as well-equipped as the Feds,
but they know their terrain, they fight dirty, and they have nothing
to lose.

### Lone Star Frontier
**Leader:** Governor Caleb Hutchins
**Claim:** "Texas was a republic before. It'll be one again."
**Based on:** Vlandia — arrived as allies, stayed as conquerors

Texas seceded during the collapse, taking its oil, its guns, and its
attitude with it. They started as Federal contractors — security,
logistics — then realized they didn't need the Feds at all. Now they're
expanding, and their heavy cavalry (armored vehicle columns) are the
terror of the plains.

**Grievance against Feds:** The Feds nationalized Texas oil "for the
national emergency." Texas remembers.

### Southern Compact
**Leader:** Colonel Ada Beaumont
**Claim:** "We were here first. We'll be here last."
**Based on:** Battania — guerrilla forest fighters

A coalition of Southern militias, National Guard units that went rogue,
and civilian defense groups. They own the woods, the swamps, the back
roads. The Feds can't find them, can't catch them, and bleed every time
they try. Masters of ambush and asymmetric warfare.

**Grievance against Feds:** Federal drone strikes hit three wedding
parties in one month. The South will never forgive.

### Mountain Alliance
**Leader:** Chief Jonas Kessler
**Claim:** "The mountains provide. The valleys obey."
**Based on:** Sturgia — hard northern infantry

Survivalists, miners, loggers, and deserters who fortified the Rockies
and Appalachians. They've got the high ground — literally — and the
toughest infantry in the game. They don't want to rule America. They
just want everyone else to leave them alone. Good luck with that.

**Grievance against Feds:** The Feds strip-mined their mountains for
"strategic resources" and left poisoned rivers behind.

## The Dynamic

Like Bannerlord's Calradia:

1. **The three Federal commands are the strongest individually** but
   divided against each other. They'd win if united. They never will.

2. **Everyone hates the Feds** but for different reasons. This is the
   glue that prevents any single Federal command from winning — the
   moment one gets too strong, the others (plus regionals) gang up.

3. **The regionals are grabbing former Federal territory** piece by piece.
   Every Federal base that falls, every depot that's raided, shifts the
   balance.

4. **The player starts as nobody** — a mercenary captain with a sword
   (or rifle) and a dream. Pick a side, or play them all against each
   other, or build your own faction from nothing.

## Why This Works for Gameplay

- **Clear antagonist:** The Feds are the obvious "big bad" — well-armed,
  well-organized, universally resented. Players love toppling giants.
- **Moral grayness:** Each Federal command has a sympathetic angle
  (order, democracy, security). Each regional has a dark side too.
- **Natural conflict:** The civil war between Federal commands + regional
  expansion + player ambition = endless war, just like Bannerlord.
- **American resonance:** Every player knows these regional identities.
  Texas pride, Southern defiance, mountain independence — it's built in.
