# FACTION RANKS AND PROGRESSION

What happens when the player joins one of the six sections, works up through its
ranks, and gets put out of it. One block per section, plus rival reactions and
the Wanderer path.

Read content/characters/faction-leaders.md and content/lore/factions-regions.md
first. Every rank ladder below is built out of the five values and the one "what
they are wrong about" line already published for that section, so a promotion
always argues with something that section is actually arguing with.

## Rules for anything added here later

1. Real geography, fictional people. No real person, company, or event appears
   here. Per CONSTITUTION.md section 6.
2. The six leaders appear, in their top promotion and in their betrayal scene.
   They must not repeat their first meeting conversation. If a line could have
   been said in a first meeting, it does not belong in this file.
3. Plain ASCII. No em dashes, no en dashes, no curly quotes, no ellipsis
   characters. Same rule as content/dialogue/quests.md.
4. One thought per line. Two ideas from one speaker is two lines.
5. No speeches. A promotion is thirty seconds of talking and a room changing.
6. Every promotion names who gives it and who is in the room. A promotion with
   no audience is a briefing, and briefings are a bug.
7. No marketing language. Nobody is proud to belong to something. They are glad
   it pays on time, which is a different sentence.

## THE SIX LADDERS

Rank names are local, not universal. The simulation only knows the tiers in
RULERS.md section 2, so every name below is content mapped onto a tier.

| Section | Joining rank | First promotion | Second promotion | Top rank | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pacific Compact | Carrier | Route Captain | Yardmaster | Freight Signatory | dock, road, port, section |
| Mountain Alliance | Tender | Crew Chief | Superintendent | Mutual Aid Commissioner | shaft, mine, road and water, section |
| Great Lakes Union | Member | Local Captain | Elevator Agent | Union Steward | county, county, rail head, section |
| Southern Compact | Rifleman | Line Captain | Field Commander | Muster Officer | camp, company, field, call |
| Lone Star Frontier | Hand | Trail Boss | Land Partner | Frontier Signatory | gate, trail, ranch, section |
| Atlantic Corridor | Filing Clerk | District Agent | Portfolio Manager | Authorizing Officer | office, district, portfolio, signature |

Four rules that hold across all six ladders:

- Nobody is promoted for one good battle. The second promotion in every section
  requires the player to have been dependable in a specific unglamorous way,
  because that is the trait the section actually runs on.
- The top rank in every section is the dangerous one. It is the rank where the
  player stops being a good soldier for somebody and starts being load bearing,
  which is what makes each betrayal scene work.
- A rejection is never a judgment on the player's worth. Every section has a
  real stated reason, and three of the six reasons are a shortage rather than a
  suspicion.
- The recruiter is not the leader. Six recruiters appear here and none of them
  is a faction leader, because the first conversation about joining should not
  be the conversation about being important.

## 1. PACIFIC COMPACT

The section is a shipping agreement with no flag moment, and the Compact is
embarrassed by the suggestion that there was one. So there is no oath ceremony
either. There is a manifest, a signature chain, and a woman at a window who
reads your first line before you finish signing it. The first promotion is given
by a man on the docks mid shift. The second is given by an inland county
scheduler, which is a promotion and a political statement at once. The top rank
is given by Vega in a room with a whiteboard, and the promotion is a tablet.

### 1.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Ruben Ostrander |
| Age | 58 |
| Home | Signal Hill, California. Twenty-five years at the Long Beach terminal |
| Role | Terminal clerk, grade three. He cannot promote anybody and mentions it |
| Talks like | Days of supply. He measures people in how often they turn up |
| Wants | Somebody who is on schedule when nothing is wrong, because that is the only time it matters |

*He is at a desk on the terminal floor with a monitor he does not look at. There
is a folding chair for you and it is already there, which means somebody told
him you were coming and he is not going to pretend otherwise.*

**Ruben Ostrander:** Sit. You want the short version or the one with the numbers
in it.

**Player:** The short version.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Then the short version is, I do not hire, I schedule.
There are eleven men on the inland run and one of them quit on Tuesday and one
of them is on light duty, so there is a hole Tuesday through Thursday until the
end of the month, roughly. You are the hole.

**Player:** And the long version.

**Ruben Ostrander:** The long version is that the Compact does not pay you to be
brave. It pays you to be at gate six at four in the morning on a Tuesday in
February with the truck loaded and the paper already stamped, and then to do
that again next Tuesday, about forty of those in a row, and then a man in
Bakersfield starts planning around you. That is the whole job. Nobody is going
to thank you and nobody is going to notice, and that is also the job.

**Player:** What happened to the man who quit.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Nothing dramatic. He got four hours of sleep, a kid with a
fever, and a decision to make at four in the morning on a Tuesday, and he made
the wrong one for him. Do not hold it against him. Just be the man who is there
on the days he was not.

**Player:** I have never run a terminal.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Good. Neither have half of us, and the ones who have are
the ones who tell you how simple it is. You know trucks, or you can learn trucks
in a week. I will teach you the paper. I am not going to teach you to drive,
everybody thinks that is the interesting part.

**Player:** What is the interesting part.

**Ruben Ostrander:** The chain. Every load gets stamped and every stamp has a
name on it, and if a load is wrong the name is the only thing that tells
anybody where to start. So the interesting part is that people read your name
eleven times before you ever throw a punch. Be boring in writing.

**Player:** What are you looking for.

**Ruben Ostrander:** A man who asks what the job is before he asks what it
pays. You have not asked what it pays. That is not loyalty, that is just
somebody who is going to still be here in March, and March is what I am hiring
for.

**Player:** What does it pay.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Every second week, in person, from the woman at the window
who has never once been wrong about the day. That is not a small thing right
now and I will not dress it up.

**Player:** I will take it.

**Ruben Ostrander:** You have not met Vega.

**Player:** I do not need the whole section. I need to be somewhere on a
schedule, repeatedly.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Write that down somewhere, because in about four months
somebody with a title is going to offer you something that is not that, and I
would like you to be able to read your own handwriting.

### 1.2 The oath

*There is no oath. There is a desk, a form on a clipboard, and a woman at a
window who has done this for nineteen years and reads the first line before the
man has finished signing it.*

**Terminal Clerk:** Line one is your legal name as it appears on your papers, and
if the two do not match I cannot stamp you, and if I cannot stamp you you are
standing at gate six holding somebody else's load. Everybody gets this wrong on
day one and everybody thinks the day is going badly.

**Player:** Understood.

**Terminal Clerk:** Line nine says the section does not stop for armed men.
Line ten says the section stops for everybody else. Read them both out loud,
both of them, because in six months somebody is going to tell you they are the
same thing and you are going to have to know what you signed.

**Player:** The section does not stop for armed men. The section stops for
everybody else.

**Terminal Clerk:** Now the part that is actually the oath. If a load comes back
wrong, you say so. In writing. Same day. Even if you are the reason it is
wrong, and especially if you are the reason it is wrong, because the day the
chain stops meaning anything is the day this yard is a warehouse with guns in
it instead of a supply line. Sign there. That is all of it.

*Behind her, a load goes out. Nobody in the room looks up, which is the point.*

### 1.3 If you are turned away

Picked when the terminal has no hole, which is most of the time, and never as a
judgment on the player.

**Ruben Ostrander:** I have nothing for you this week and I am not going to
write you down for later, because I have twelve men on a list who are all
waiting for a hole and I would be stealing from them to make you feel better.

**Ruben Ostrander:** I am going to say this once and then we are done. You want
a section with a flag and a ceremony and a man to tell you it matters. This is
a yard where we argue about a loading dock. If that is not enough, you will spend
three years here being the man nobody noticed.

**Ruben Ostrander:** Take the ride to Oakland. The port side pays more and
hates you slower. I am being honest, I like you, I do not have a chair.

**Ruben Ostrander:** You are not too young. You are too available. If I take
you today I am not taking you in March, and March is the part I am hiring for.

**Ruben Ostrander:** The Compact is a schedule, not a church. Nobody is going
to be in trouble if they do not join. Go be a Wanderer for a year. Come back
when you can tell me what a manifest is.
### 1.4 Promotion to Route Captain

*Given by Curtis Mabry, dock superintendent, on the floor, mid shift, with a
forklift going past behind him. He does not stop working to do it. There is no
announcement. Four men on the inland run hear about it in a week and one of them
is irritated for about a day.*

**Curtis Mabry:** Ruben says you did not miss a gate in eleven weeks. I did not
believe him so I checked the log myself, which I do not do.

**Player:** Thank you.

**Curtis Mabry:** Do not thank me, that is not what this is. What this is, you
are the second signature on forty loads now, and when one of them is wrong I am
going to be standing in a room asking two men which one of them signed, and I
picked you because you have not done anything interesting enough to be the one I
ask first.

**Player:** That is a promotion by insult.

**Curtis Mabry:** It is a promotion by Tuesday morning. In four months somebody
will break a seal in a shed out past Fontana, and there will be two names on that
paper, and I want both of them to be men who check things. Congratulations.

*How the room reacts: nobody. One of the drivers says "nice" without looking up
and goes back to loading. The promotion is a bigger deal to the player than to
the room, and the game should let that land without a popup.*

### 1.5 Promotion to Yardmaster

*Given by Ana Delgado, who runs a schedule out of Kern County and drove ninety
miles to do it in person, which she mentions in the first sentence. She is not
from the coast. She is from the water end of the argument, and she is putting a
coastal man on top of a coastal yard in a section that thinks the coast took
their water, and she has decided to do it anyway. The room is eleven port people
and one county woman with a whiteboard.*

**Ana Delgado:** I drove ninety miles in a truck with a heater that works on
principle, and I would like it noted that the Compact made me do that.

**Player:** It is noted.

**Ana Delgado:** Here is the thing nobody in this yard wants to hear. You just
got the highest job on this dock, and I am the person giving it to you, and I am
not from Long Beach. I run grain scheduling out of Bakersfield. This is not a
mistake and it is not a favor, and if either of you tells anybody it was a
favor I will hear about it and I will be unpleasant.

**Player:** Understood.

**Ana Delgado:** A yardmaster is the last person in the chain before a county
gets fed or does not. Eleven thousand people in that valley do not know your
name and will not, and if the road stops, the first voice they hear is somebody
in a truck who is fed up and says so, and that is a person with a yardmaster
stamp on him. That is why I came myself instead of sending a form.

**Player:** And the last stamp in the chain.

**Ana Delgado:** Which is exactly what makes this a promotion. You are now the
man who stamps a thing that cannot be unstamped. A route captain makes a
decision. You make it permanent, and every yardmaster I have ever known worked
that out on a bad day, usually in October, usually in a language they did not
speak at home.

*How the room reacts: the port people go quiet, because they all know what a
yardmaster is and none of them expected the job to come from inland. Delgado does
not soften it. She leaves before anybody can thank her, and the last thing she
says over her shoulder is about the building.*

**Ana Delgado:** My heater is fine. The heat in this building is a problem.
Tell Vega a hundred and ten percent. She will know what I mean.

### 1.6 Promotion to Freight Signatory

*Given by Marisol Vega, in the Long Beach office, at seven in the morning,
standing up, in front of six port captains and a state official who came to
watch and did not expect to be here. This is the only promotion in the section
where the room applauds, and it applauds for about a second and a half and then
gets back to work, which is the most Pacific Compact thing that happens all day.*

*She hands over a tablet instead of a document.*

**Marisol Vega:** I have read eleven weeks of logs on a man who was signing
loads, and I am not going to pretend that is a big deal, because on this coast
eleven weeks of logs is a normal Tuesday.

**Player:** Then why am I standing here.

**Marisol Vega:** Because of the Fontana seal, which you reported the same day
you broke it, before anybody asked you about it. That is the whole reason. Every
man I have put in that chair could load a truck. The difference between them is
whether I find out from them or from a count.

**Player:** What can I actually do.

**Marisol Vega:** Refuse a load. That is the whole power of the job and it is
more than anybody outside that building has. When the road is short and a county
is yelling, somebody has to decide which one does not eat, and right now that
somebody is a terminal and not a governor, and the governors are extremely
comfortable with that arrangement, and I would like to take it away from them.

**Player:** Who decides when I have to pick.

**Marisol Vega:** You do. I will give you the map and I will take the blame in
whatever room complains, and I want to be clear this is not a favor. It is an
accounting. A freight signatory is a promise that a specific place goes short on
a specific day, and a person who can make that promise out loud is a person the
whole system can run on.

*How the room reacts: one port captain says he has wanted that chair since
before the player was born, which is not entirely a compliment. The state
official asks whether the Compact intends to consult the counties. Vega answers
with a number.*

**Marisol Vega:** A hundred and ten percent this quarter. Ana Delgado can give
you the address. Anybody else can take it up with me, and I have got seven in
the morning tomorrow, and I like this one.

### 1.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*The scene is a Tuesday morning in the same room with the same whiteboard. She
does not raise her voice and she is not disappointed, and the second of those is
much harder to survive than the first.*

**Marisol Vega:** Sit down. I am going to do this in front of these people
because in six months somebody is going to tell them a story about you and I
would rather they heard the version with my face on it.

**Player:** Vega.

**Marisol Vega:** Four counties did not get loaded in August. Not three. Four,
and two of them were inland, and one of those two was the county Ana Delgado
schedules out of, and she drove ninety miles to hand you a stamp in March, and
she drove ninety miles again in September to ask me what happened, and I could
not tell her anything, because the answer was a man at a desk making a call every
morning with nobody above him.

**Player:** I was the only one with the picture.

**Marisol Vega:** You were the only one with the picture, and that is the whole
accusation, so do not make me say it twice. I did not put a counter in front of
you. I put a pen in front of you. I told you your name is the only thing that
tells anybody where to start, and you used it, and you used it to decide which
counties were nobody.

**Player:** If I had waited, four counties would be nine.

**Marisol Vega:** Probably. And eleven is a number a person can say out loud in
a room, and a promise that four counties were a nobody is not a number, it is a
lie with a schedule on it. You chose who starved and you did it in a tone of
voice nobody in that room would have argued with. I have been doing this eleven
years and that is the first time I have heard that trick used by somebody I
promoted myself.

*The room reacts badly and then not at all, which is worse. One port captain
will not look at the player. The state official is writing, and stops.*

**Marisol Vega:** You are off the signatory list today and you are out of my
office today. The yard will still have a carrier in it if you want one, because
I am not going to make you unemployed. I am going to make you nobody, and the
difference is that nobody does not get a better offer next month.

**Player:** Thank you for doing it in front of them.

**Marisol Vega:** Do not thank me. If I had done it in a hall this would have
been a story by dinner and a resignation by Friday, and you are not going to get
to be a man who left. You are going to be a man who was put out, and you are
going to have to say that sentence yourself, to somebody, out loud, and I am
sorry about that. Sign nothing. Take your card. It is dead, not paper.

## 2. MOUNTAIN ALLIANCE

The section has no authority and everybody in it says so within ten minutes of
your arrival, usually with pride. So there is no oath room and nobody who can
swear you in. There is a call list, a hard hat, and a man who writes your name
on a sheet and tells you he is going to use it. The first promotion happens
underground at a face you never see. The second is given on a gravel turnaround
by a man who counts things for a living. The top rank is given by Grant, who has
to admit out loud that he cannot give it to anybody, and gives it anyway.

### 2.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Cletus Buhm |
| Age | 61 |
| Home | Butte, Montana. Thirty-four years underground, eleven of them as a shift foreman |
| Role | Mine foreman. The only man in the section with authority to send anybody into a hole |
| Talks like | Slow and literal. He does not use metaphors, on purpose |
| Wants | Somebody who will go down when the roof is bad, which is a smaller ask than it sounds |

*The meeting is in a man cave outside Butte with a coffee urn that has been on
since five. He is reading a clipboard. He does not offer you a seat until he
finishes the line he is on, and that is the first thing he is testing.*

**Cletus Buhm:** Sit down. I am on a line. Okay. Now. Where are you from.

**Player:** Flat country.

**Cletus Buhm:** That was not an answer, that was a category, and I asked a
question, and I am going to ask it again, because I have found the first answer
is usually the one the person prepared.

**Player:** Ohio originally. Four years in the Lakes, a year in Texas.

**Cletus Buhm:** So you have seen flat, and you have seen nothing at all, and
those are very different problems. The difference is this. Out here the country
is trying to kill you on a schedule. It has a schedule. My pit has a schedule.
Texas has a hundred miles between you and the man who owes you help. You are
none of those, are you. You are a man with a rifle and a reputation and no idea
what a rock face does to a plan.

**Player:** That is fair.

**Cletus Buhm:** I did not ask for fair. Fair is a word people use when they
want the conversation to stop. Here is the job. Roof bolts go in on a cycle, not
on a feeling, and I need a man on the surface who can tell the difference
between the sound that is normal and the sound that is a bad afternoon, and who
will take four men down and get them up on his own authority without asking me,
because I am two hours away and there is no cell service in the shaft.

**Player:** And if it is not bad.

**Cletus Buhm:** Then you wasted a shift, a truck, and a man who hates going
underground after dark. I will say so in front of your crew. You will have
earned it. And everybody up here will learn in a year that you went down four
times for nothing, which is how a crew decides what you are.

**Player:** What do you want me to say when I do go down.

**Cletus Buhm:** The time. Your name. How many are with you. That is the whole
call and it takes four seconds. Every man who died under this mountain that I
know of had somebody upstairs who could not say four words in the right order.

**Player:** You have been doing this thirty-four years.

**Cletus Buhm:** Thirty-four. And I will tell you the one that stays with me. A
man came up on shift nineteen and told me the roof was fine, and he was right,
and I sent him home, and he was holding his wife's coffee the whole time, and
when I asked him about it three days later he said he had wanted to take his
time, and that was the whole reason he did not run. I was fifty-eight, and it
took me a year to understand that a slow man on my surface is not a slow man. He
is a man who noticed something nobody asked him to notice, and I sent him home,
and I was wrong to, and he never worked underground again and I never got it
back.

**Player:** I would have gone down.

**Cletus Buhm:** I know you would. That is not why I am telling it. I am telling
it because in four years you are going to be a shift boss yourself, and somebody
is going to tell you the roof is fine, and I want you to remember that sometimes
they are right.

**Player:** What is the pay.

**Cletus Buhm:** Better than the valley, worse than Texas, and it is the first
of the month, and I have never missed one in eleven years, which I am told is
unusual, and I am not going to pretend I do not know what that is worth, and I
am also not going to make a speech about it.

**Player:** I am in.

**Cletus Buhm:** I know. You said Ohio and flat country, which is the answer I
would have given. Here is what you do not do. You do not tell people in Denver
you got hired by Grant. You did not. You got hired by a man in a man cave, and
Denver does not need to know, because the day Denver knows is the day there is a
form.

### 2.2 The oath

*There is nothing to sign. There is a hard hat, a call list on a clipboard, and
Cletus writing your name on a line between two other names, one of which is
crossed out with a single stroke and no explanation.*

**Cletus Buhm:** Fourteen names on this sheet. Thirteen of us are getting paid,
and the one with the line through him is not, and I am not going to tell you
which one that is or why. If you figure it out, you keep it, because this
section runs on two things and one of them is that nobody in the country knows
what anybody else in the country said.

**Player:** And the other thing.

**Cletus Buhm:** That I do not know how to say this without it being a lie. In
a flag you are loyal to a man. In a city you are loyal to a payroll. This is
neither. I am putting your name on a sheet because there is a percentage chance
a person is under this mountain in a bad week, and he needs a name he can send
somebody to. You do not owe me anything. You owe a list.

**Player:** I will be on that list.

**Cletus Buhm:** You will be on that list, and the day you find out what the
crossed out name cost, you come to me before you go to Denver, and I will not be
able to help you and I will not pretend to. That is the whole speech, and it
took me a year of the job to learn, and I am not doing it again until the next
one.

*He hands you the hard hat. It is used and it fits.*

### 2.3 If you are turned away

**Cletus Buhm:** I have no crew, and I am not sending a man I do not have into a
shaft under a roof I do not like. Go east. The Corridor pays in thirty days
instead of thirty-one, and the difference will cost you a truck.

**Cletus Buhm:** You are too comfortable, and I say that with respect, because
comfortable is fine until the emergency. I have watched comfortable men freeze
and I have watched frightened men move, and they are not the same problem, and the
comfortable one is worse because he does not know it yet.

**Cletus Buhm:** I do not care about your reputation. I have four men in this
outfit with reputations and two of them are the reason the safety board came
out twice. I care that you come back up.

**Cletus Buhm:** The Alliance is not a section you join, it is a call sheet, and
if your whole reason for being out here is that you want somebody to believe you
matter, take the drive south and get it over with, because up here it is a
roster and your name is on it in pencil until somebody writes in pen.

**Cletus Buhm:** I am not going to promise you there is work in a good year.
There is not. In a good year you are a man with a hard hat and no hours, and
you will be tempted to go work for Ashby, and everybody does, and nobody who
came back is a traitor, they were just hungry. I would rather tell you that now
than have you hate me in October.
### 2.4 Promotion to Crew Chief

*Given underground, at a face the player does not see, forty feet down, with a
lamp behind the foreman's shoulder. It takes about nine seconds. Nobody shakes
your hand because nobody has a free hand, and it is the only promotion in the
game that happens without eye contact.*

**Cletus Buhm:** You are crew chief. Say it back.

**Player:** I am crew chief.

**Cletus Buhm:** Say what that means.

**Player:** I am responsible for the men who go down after me.

**Cletus Buhm:** Say what happens if you send a man into a bad roof.

**Player:** I go down myself and I do not have to be told.

**Cletus Buhm:** That is what I wanted. I have promoted four men to crew chief
and two of them said something about being ready, and both of them are dead, and
I want to say clearly that I did not ask them the question they answered.

*How the room reacts: the crew does not cheer. One of them, an old man named
Kowalczyk who has been down here longer than any shift, taps the lamp twice with
two fingers, which is apparently a thing they do, and then the shift continues,
and that is the player's entire career so far.*

### 2.5 Promotion to Superintendent

*Given by Harlan Duff, who runs road and water maintenance for three districts,
standing on a gravel turnaround at six in the morning next to a water truck. He
is the only man in the section with a title that means he can be blamed for a
river, and he has the tired voice of somebody who has been blamed for two
already.*

**Harlan Duff:** Two of my districts have water problems in the same week, which
never happens, and the reason it is happening this week is that I put the wrong
crew on the wrong one in May. That is the whole speech. I have said it before
and I will say it again, and I do not know why I am saying it to you instead of
to the water board.

**Player:** I am not the water board.

**Harlan Duff:** No. And that is why I am telling you. The water board would
write it down and it would become a reason not to give you the district, and I
would rather you have the district and hear it from a man standing next to a
truck.

**Player:** What is in the district.

**Harlan Duff:** Two hundred miles of road, four water pumps, and a town of nine
hundred people who own a mine that shut in March. Ninety percent of the job is
the road and ten percent is the water, and the water is the part that ends
somebody. A superintendent does not fix a road. A superintendent fixes the order
things get fixed in, and the order is going to be wrong this year, and then you
spend the summer explaining it.

**Player:** Why me.

**Harlan Duff:** Because you went down four times for nothing and told me about
it, and because you said my heater was a problem and not my truck, which is a
stupid thing to remember and I have remembered it for a year.

**Player:** I do not know how to schedule water.

**Harlan Duff:** Neither do I. That is the job, and everybody who tells you
otherwise is selling something. You learn the pumps and you learn the men, and
you decide in March what goes unfixed, and then you explain it all summer.

*How the room reacts: six men from three districts came to watch, and they are
quiet. One of them, an irrigation man from outside the section entirely, asks the
player whether the mine town gets water before the fields do. The player has to
answer. Whichever way it goes, one of the six does not like it, and that is the
intended cost.*

### 2.6 Promotion to Mutual Aid Commissioner

*Given by Hollis Grant, in the Denver warehouse that used to hold something
heavy. Two of his own governors are in the room arguing about a grain shipment
when he comes in. He puts the coffee down without drinking from it, which is how
the player knows this one is real, and he starts with the hardest part of the
sentence before the good part.*

**Hollis Grant:** I have no authority to give you that. I want to say that
first, before the title, so you do not spend a year finding out.

**Player:** Then who can.

**Hollis Grant:** Nobody. Salt Lake could veto it. Phoenix will. Albuquerque
will not return the paperwork. I am going to say the words anyway, and three of
those four are not going to do anything about it, and I am going to be able to
live with that, and so will you. That is the entire arrangement this section has
ever had.

**Player:** What do I do with it.

**Hollis Grant:** You show up. I am going to be blunt, because I do not have
the patience for a speech and you have got a hard hat on, which tells me you
already understand what I am for. There is a pass outside Pueblo that has been
closing in three different places since March, and a district outside Reno with
eleven weeks of water, and no authority in four states who can send a crew to
either. That is you. You are not a general. You are the man who gets a truck
there.

**Player:** And when Denver disagrees with me.

**Hollis Grant:** Then you will do the thing you are good at, which is be right
in a room full of governors. And I am going to be in that room, and I am going
to say almost nothing, and it is going to be the worst ninety seconds of your
career, and I will not rescue you. Sit down. Finish the coffee. Tell me about
the north wall.

*How the room reacts: the two governors stop arguing about the grain and watch,
because they have never seen him do this before, and one of them says "that is
four" quietly, meaning he is counting. It is not a hostile number. It is the
only applause a Mountain Alliance promotion gets.*

### 2.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*The scene is a table outside a trailer park on the Utah line, and the man in it
is not Hollis Grant. Grant does not do this. What Grant does is turn up, which
is the worst thing that can happen to you, because he is a decent man and he
came.*

**Corbin Ashby:** Sit down. Do not look at the door. There is nobody behind it
and I am not going to be clever about anything. You worked for the Alliance and
you are about to not work for the Alliance, and I have the only signed thing that
says so, and I am not going to pretend that is generosity.

**Player:** Grant is the one who has the authority.

**Corbin Ashby:** Grant has a moral authority and a hard hat and no legal
authority, and I have read every memo he has ever written about that, and every
one of them is correct, and every one of them has been ignored by men who own
tanks. You are not the first crew chief to come down a grade and I am not the
first man to hire one. I pay on the first of the month, and I am going to be
straight with you about the whole thing, because you can tell I would rather not
waste a morning.

**Player:** Straight about what.

**Corbin Ashby:** I do not need you to betray anybody. That is the pitch, so you
can stop waiting for it. I need your name on a work order for a shaft I am
reopening, and Grant's name off a letter he is going to write in about eleven
days. In eleven days a roof is coming down on somebody, and Grant writes to
everybody when a roof comes down, and he is right to, and his letter is going to
go to every owner in four states including me. If that letter does not go out,
the ore keeps moving.

**Player:** People could die under that roof.

**Corbin Ashby:** They will, and they will be my men, and I will put new bolts in
on my own dime, and I want that in writing. Put it in your contract. Put my name
on it. I am not lying to you about the danger. I am lying to you about nobody
knowing, and the difference matters to me, and it should matter to you, because
the day it stops mattering to you is the day you are Ashby instead of a man who
has made a mistake.

**Player:** I will not do it.

**Corbin Ashby:** Then here is the part I do not enjoy. I am putting you out in
writing before the shift, because I do not have time to be a disappointed man,
and a crew chief who walked out of a mine in front of thirty men is a story by
lunch. You worked a hundred and sixty days. I am paying you for every one of them
at your rate, and I am writing on this paper that the man who ends you is me and
not the mountain.

**Player:** That is worse than if you hated me.

**Corbin Ashby:** Yes. And you will be able to say that out loud to somebody in a
year, and that is the entire currency I deal in, and I would like you to have
it. I would like Grant to have it too, because he never comes down here and you
are the only honest man in his section who ever has.

## 3. GREAT LAKES UNION

The section is a promise about grain, so the oath is literally about counting. A
Union is the only place in this file where a new member is handed a number and a
rule about what may not be done, and the rule is the whole institution. The first
promotion is given by a county man who has never had a soldier. The second is
given by a rail head trader being kind in a way that is really an argument. The
top rank is given by Bernice at a table with a sack of corn and a napkin, and it
is the only promotion in the game where the reward is a fact about a specific
field.

### 3.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Thelma Ruediger |
| Age | 55 |
| Home | Cedar County, Iowa. Same farm her father sharecropped and did not own |
| Role | Elevator manager and grain committee chair for a county of four towns |
| Talks like | Weather, soil, prices, the year. She will not discuss a decision without the year |
| Wants | Somebody who can be believed standing in a field in October |

*She is at the elevator office with the door open and a fan running and a
revolving credit open on the desk behind her, which she does not close. She has
coffee from a thermos and she has already poured you a cup without asking, which
is a test and also hospitality.*

**Thelma Ruediger:** Sit, no, do not sit on that side, that side is where the
scale tickets go. Sit here. The coffee is terrible, I made it at five.

**Player:** Thank you.

**Thelma Ruediger:** I put your application at a table in the union hall and I
asked four men who is standing in a cornfield in October who can be believed, and
I got four names and one of them was already on my list, so that is progress.
That is what I do. I am a farm woman with a clipboard and I go find out who can
be believed.

**Player:** Believed by who.

**Thelma Ruediger:** That is the whole question and I will tell you what I think
the answer is. Not by me. I am not important. By a hundred people at a town hall
in a bad year, deciding whether to sell their corn early because a stranger with
a badge and a good coat is standing in the back. You are not selling anything in
that room. You are being a fact in it.

**Player:** I have never done that.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Then you will be terrible at it for a year, and then you
will be the man in three counties worth a third of his standing, and I have
watched that happen twice, and both times it was to a man nobody expected
anything from, which is why I like hiring them.

**Player:** What is the job right now.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Hauling. Elevator to elevator, four counties. It is the
least interesting work in this section and it is the work the section is made
of. Everything in the Union is a truck that went when it was supposed to. Your
entire value to me in the first six months is that a farmer in a bad September
can look out his window and see your rig go past, going the right way, on the
day you said.

**Player:** That is a low bar.

**Thelma Ruediger:** It is the only bar, and nobody clears it twice a week. The
fancy people can do all the talking they want. Somebody has to be there on
Wednesday.

**Player:** What does it pay.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Every month, cash at the co-op, and the co-op has never been
late, which I know does not sound like much until you have worked for somebody
who is always late. I am not going to sell you the section, and I am not going
to tell you what Bernice is like, because if I did that you would join the wrong
thing and it would be my fault.

**Player:** So what is the section.

**Thelma Ruediger:** The section is a promise. Oyelaran has never once sold a
member's grain out from under them without telling them first, and she has paid
for that promise every year since I started paying attention, and I want you to
know that when Nebraska leaves, which it will, it will be because of a number and
not a scandal.

**Player:** You are on her side.

**Thelma Ruediger:** I am on the side of the elevator, which is the only thing I
have ever been on the side of, and the elevator has been on her side for six
years because she has never asked it to lie.

### 3.2 The oath

*In the elevator office, with the credit open on the desk, because she refuses
to do this in a room where the sound carries differently. There is a sheet with a
number on it. Your name goes on a line, and above your line there is a rule
printed in the union's own hand.*

**Thelma Ruediger:** Read the line at the top. Out loud. I do not care, the
office is empty.

**Player:** No member's grain shall be sold out from under that member without
telling that member first.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Again, slower, because I want to be certain you heard which
word. Not without permission. Without telling them first. She is allowed to sell
it, if she tells you first. That is what makes this a promise instead of a
slogan, and there are men in Omaha who would sell every bushel in the state if
the section died tomorrow, and the only thing stopping them is that she is still
alive and still keeping the sheet.

**Player:** What happens if she breaks it.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Then it was never a promise, then it was a schedule, and I
do not know what this section looks like after that. I have a farm and eleven
people on that line, so I have thought about it more than I would enjoy
admitting in a room with a fan running.

**Player:** And if I break it.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Then you do not put your name back on that sheet, and I
will drive down there myself. Now. Your number. Take the number. It is not a
rank, it is a number, and the number is what the truck goes out under. If you
skip a run you do not skip a run, you skip a number, and somebody in Chicago
will notice the number and not you. That is how this works.

### 3.3 If you are turned away

**Thelma Ruediger:** I have four trucks and I have four drivers, and two of
those drivers are mine better than the other two, and I am not pretending that is
fair. It is just what happened. I am not taking a fifth to make somebody feel
better about a bad September.

**Thelma Ruediger:** You want a section with a leader to admire. This one has a
leader who is better at numbers than any of us and cannot tell a cornfield from
a parking lot. She is not a symbol. She is a farm woman from a county you have
never driven through. I do not know how to sell that, so I will not try.

**Thelma Ruediger:** If you want to be somebody, come back in October when there
is actually a shortage, because that is when a man who can be believed is worth
something. In August every fool in a clean shirt can be believed.

**Thelma Ruediger:** Go south. I mean it. The man down there will hand you a
rifle and a hot meal and tell you that you are a brother, and I am not going to
insult you by pretending the food is not better.

**Thelma Ruediger:** I liked you and I have no chair. That is the whole
conversation. Take your coffee, it is terrible.
### 3.4 Promotion to Local Captain

*Given by Dale Kaminski, who is a deputy sheriff and a militia captain in the way
that means he has a second job and a trucker cap. He gives it in a church
basement with eleven other men who did not know they were being ranked, and he
is uncomfortable the whole time, which is what makes it work. He hands you a
vest. There is no insignia. He apologizes for the vest.*

**Dale Kaminski:** Okay. I am going to be honest, I have never given one of
these out to a man I did not know, and I do not have a speech, and I have been
staring at this box for ten minutes. Here. It is a vest. That is all it is.

**Player:** That is fine.

**Dale Kaminski:** It is not fine, it is embarrassing, and I am doing it anyway
because the county did not give me a bullet vest and a man went out in a pickup
with a box of flares in a riot last year. So the vest is the badge. That is the
whole logic. Are you in.

**Player:** I am in.

**Dale Kaminski:** Good, because here is what a local captain is in this union.
You are the man the sheriff's office calls at four in the morning, and you are
the man the co-op calls when a load is short, and you do not have any more men
than you had this morning. That is the rank. The number of men you command is
not the rank, and I want you to hear me say that out loud now, because in about
two years somebody is going to offer you a rank with fifty men in it and it is
going to sound like an upgrade.

**Player:** Is it an upgrade.

**Dale Kaminski:** It is a job. It is also the fastest way for a man to stop
being trusted by a town and start being trusted by a payroll, and I have watched
men make that trade and never be able to go back. You will be the first captain
out of this basement who can still walk into a farm meeting and be believed, and
that is a thing you can do for about four years, and then somebody is going to
put a badge on you that says the section sent you, and that is the day the town
stops believing and the union starts.

**Player:** I will remember.

**Dale Kaminski:** You will not. Everybody says that. The vest is in the trunk,
there is no ceremony, and the co-op is doing a fish fry Friday and you are not
required to come.

*How the room reacts: eleven men who had not been told either look at each other,
because the whole rank arrived without a speech and none of them know what to do
with that. Kaminski leaves first.*

### 3.5 Promotion to Elevator Agent

*Given by Merle Duplantis, who works a rail head in eastern Nebraska, and who is
being kind in a way that is really an argument. He is unshaven, he pours two
coffees, puts one in front of you, and does not sit down until he has said the
part he came to say.*

**Merle Duplantis:** I am going to say a thing and I want it on the record that I
said it kindly. I like you. I voted for you. And I do not think you should be an
elevator agent in Nebraska.

**Player:** Say the rest of it.

**Merle Duplantis:** The agent is the man who decides what leaves this county
and in what order, and the reason the county needs one is that Oyelaran will
not open the gates, so the gate stays shut and the man sitting next to the shut
gate gets blamed by about nine hundred people at once. That is the job. I have
had it four years and I have not slept through a night.

**Player:** And you think the job is wrong.

**Merle Duplantis:** I think the job is right and it is in the wrong building.
There is exactly one honest argument for opening those gates, and the agent is
the only man in the section standing close enough to make it. I have been
standing here four years making a version of it that nobody believes, because I
am an agent, and every time I get close the section says no and the county says
you knew the gates were shut.

**Player:** You want me to take it and use it.

**Merle Duplantis:** I want you to take it and lose the first time on purpose.
Open a gate you should not open. Get caught. Be the section's man who admitted
it. Because the only thing that has ever beaten her promise is somebody inside
the union breaking it honestly and taking the punishment in public, and it has
to be somebody the county believes, and that is a very short list right now.

**Player:** That sounds like a trap.

**Merle Duplantis:** It is a job. I will put the terms in writing for you, which
is more than the last man got. Take the vest off when you go in there. Nobody
wants to talk to a uniform in October, and I have learned that in the worst way
possible.

**Player:** What happens if it works.

**Merle Duplantis:** Then Nebraska leaves, and I do not know what this section is
after that, and I have a daughter in Omaha. Ask Bernice about it. I have asked
her about it twice.

*How the room reacts: nobody else is in the room, which is the point. It is the
only promotion in the file that happens in a car park with two coffee cups, and
the man giving it is offering the player a promotion on the condition that the
player commit an act which is a betrayal from the inside, in public, wearing no
badge. Taking the offer is a choice with a real faction cost either way.*

### 3.6 Promotion to Union Steward

*Given by Bernice Oyelaran, in Chicago, in a room booked under a name that is
not hers. She is early, she has a sandwich, and she has brought a paper bag. The
promotion is a sack of corn. She puts it on the table, and the room reacts the
way a room reacts to a sack of corn.*

*Two people from the industrial states are in the room and one of them has a
factory district and no farmland, and he did not come to be talked to about
corn.*

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Stand up. Everybody stand up, we are doing this fast
because the light is good right now and I would like the picture to be the one
where you look like a person and not the one where you look like a briefing.

**Player:** I do not need a picture.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** You do not, and I am going to make you look at one anyway,
because in two years somebody is going to say the section handed you this and that
you do not look like a farmer, and then it is going to matter that there is a
picture of you in a field in October. Sign here. Say the number for the record.

**Player:** Eleven hundred and sixty.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** And the elevation and the date, because a number without
those is a number a man in a rail head could have made up in a bar. July
nineteenth. Thirty-one feet. Do not let anybody take the date out of it.

**Player:** What is it for.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Nothing. It is from my own farm, four hundred acres my
family worked and did not own, and I have brought it in here to a room with two
men whose districts are factories, because a man whose town is a factory is going
to ask what a Union steward is for, and I am not going to answer him with a
policy.

**Player:** You are answering with corn.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** I am answering with a field, so the question he is really
asking, which is whether this section has a heart, gets a real answer. The corn
is not the argument. The corn is the receipt. And I learned that from a man in
Des Moines who told me about a combine, so do not look at me like that.

*The factory man from Cleveland speaks, and he is not a villain, he is a man
with four hundred people and no food.*

**Cleveland Governor:** Bernice, I will vote for him. I want that understood. I
am not voting for him. I am voting because you have brought corn into a room that
has never once been asked what it is feeding.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Do not say that, you will make it into a speech. Just sign.

**Player:** What can I actually do as steward.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Here it is, and I want no argument today. You are the man
who talks to Prewitt. He is the best negotiator in this section, and he has never
once raised his voice at me, and I have been waiting six years for a man to put
across that table who has a farm in his history and not a committee. I cannot
outargue him. I can outlast him. You can do neither of those things, but you can
do the third, which is be in the room for four years until he is old enough to be
disrespected, and then he will be disrespected by his own people and not by me.

**Player:** And if he never leaves.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Then we do this the hard way, and you will lose somebody in
Omaha who has a daughter, and I have already had that conversation with Thelma
Ruediger twice, and both times she was right, and both times I did not change my
mind. That is what this rank is. If you want the version where you are
comfortable, take the field again. I will not be offended.

### 3.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*The scene is the Chicago room again, booked under the same name, and this time
she has the paper bag and it is full and she does not put it on the table. She has
a napkin in her hand and she is folding it again while she talks, and she was
folding it the first time you met her too.*

**Bernice Oyelaran:** I am going to start with the part that is in my favor and
then I am going to stop being in my own favor, so hold on. I do not think you
took the money for yourself. I checked. The money went where money goes when
somebody is being bought, which is a truck, a cousin, and a man named Renner who
has been in this business for eleven years.

**Player:** I took it for the trucks.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** You took it for the trucks and you told yourself the trucks
were the point. That is how it works on everybody, and I have watched it work on
two governors and one of them was me, in a year you do not need to hear about. A
truck is the most convincing story there is.

**Player:** Prewitt took the whole Nebraska book through me.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Say it slower.

**Player:** He took the whole book through me. Four counties. Thirty-one thousand
acres. I did not even know that was what I was opening.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** I know. I have been reading your messages since April and I
did not stop them, and that is the part of this I cannot say out loud in a room,
so I am going to say it here with two witnesses who are not in the room. I could
have closed this in April. I read it and I waited, because I wanted the book.

**Player:** You used me.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Yes. And I want you to sit with how little that hurts,
because you would have done the same thing to a county for less. You know it. I
have watched you decide three times this year that one particular county could go
first. You are not a monster. You are a man with a schedule and a favor in it, and
I am the woman who taught you that that is the same as being reasonable.

**Player:** What happens to me.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** The rail heads close to you in the morning. Not by
violence, do not make it dramatic. Four elevators, two co-ops, and a legitimate
notice about credit. By Friday your name is a question people ask in four
counties, and the answer is that you sold the book, and it is true, and you will
say it, because the alternative is the version where they say you were tricked,
and a man who was tricked does not get trusted again either.

**Player:** The promise.

**Bernice Oyelaran:** The promise is the only thing I have, and you did not break
it, and that is the part that is going to keep you awake. You told me first.
Ninety days ahead, in writing, exactly like Prewitt. I have been holding that
letter in a drawer since June, and I use it to argue with the Cleveland people,
and I hate it, and I would like you to know that you made my life easier in the
worst possible way, and that I will not forget it.

*How the room reacts: she does not send anybody out, and she does not raise her
voice, and she slides the paper bag across the table anyway. The player does not
know whether that is a kindness or the last thing she will ever be able to do.*

**Bernice Oyelaran:** Take the corn. You will not eat it. I have four hundred
acres and I cannot carry all of it home in one truck, and that is the truth, and
it is also a thing I am saying because I would like one thing in this room today
to not be a calculation.

## 4. SOUTHERN COMPACT

The section was founded by a man with a training room, so the oath is drill, and
the rank structure is the closest thing in the game to a military one, and
Cordell Jessup has spent six years pretending it is a hobby. The first promotion
is given on a range by a drill sergeant. The second is given by a governor who
has a use for you, which is the entire point. The top rank is given on a porch
with forty people there, and the room applauds, and Jessup is aware of exactly
what that means.

### 4.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Arlie Dugas |
| Age | 49 |
| Home | outside Baton Rouge. Fifteen years in and around camps, eleven of them teaching |
| Role | Drill sergeant. He can sign a man onto a muster roll and that is genuinely all |
| Talks like | Range cadence. Short instructions, long silences, and he hates waste of both |
| Wants | Somebody who shows up tired, because the first ninety days is the whole section |

*The meeting is a folding table under a shade structure at the edge of a firing
range, and there are forty men in the dirt behind you running drills. He does not
lower his voice when he talks to you, because in his experience people who need a
lowered voice are about to be disappointed.*

**Arlie Dugas:** Stand up. There. Everybody who comes through here stands up for
the first conversation, because the first conversation is where people decide
whether this is something they are doing or something that is happening to them,
and I have watched about four hundred men make that decision and I could tell you
which side it went in the first two minutes.

**Player:** I have fought before.

**Arlie Dugas:** Everybody has. Nobody has done this before. Fighting is a thing
that happens for about an hour and then you are a person again. This is a
Tuesday. This is a Tuesday and next Tuesday and the one after that, and the part
of you that is good at it is not the part that swings.

**Player:** What is the part.

**Arlie Dugas:** The part that puts a truck where I said at four ten, on a dirt
road, in the rain, with forty men who have all been up since three. And here is
the thing I need you to hear, because men do not believe it and then they are
disappointed and then they tell people the section lied to them. I cannot give
you any of the good parts. Not now. In the first ninety days the only good part
is being where you said, and I will be out in that dirt with you doing it,
because I have never asked a man for something I would not do in August heat
with forty men who also did not sign up for August heat.

**Player:** I have heard this section talks about ninety days.

**Arlie Dugas:** We all talk about the ninety days, and the smart money says that
means we are a ninety day section, and that is what a man with a computer thinks.
Here is what it actually means. In the first ninety days after a call, we can put
more men in a field than anybody on this map. Nobody can match it. Not the
Corridor with its money, not the mountains with their ground. It is not close, it
is not a matter of opinion, and it works because there are men in these counties
with a rifle in the truck and nobody to call them up.

**Player:** And after ninety days.

**Arlie Dugas:** After ninety days we are poorer and smaller than everybody, and
we are in a hole. I am not going to sell you the first part without the second,
because a man who only heard the first part is going to be a problem in month
four, and month four is where this section is actually decided.

**Player:** What is the pay.

**Arlie Dugas:** Cash at the end of the call, from a man named Dupree who counts
it out on a folding table and does not owe anybody a dollar. It is not much and
it is on time, and on time is the only thing anybody in this section actually
values, and I have watched rich sections lose to us for exactly that reason.

**Player:** And the promise. The big one. That we do not lose, we do not fold, we
do not run.

**Arlie Dugas:** Do not say that to me. That is his, and he has been working on
it for six years, and if a drill sergeant says it, then it is a thing men say, and
the day it is a thing men say, it is a thing men have said before and come home
from somewhere.

**Player:** Then what do I tell people I joined.

**Arlie Dugas:** Tell them you joined a camp. Tell them there is a range and a
schedule and a man on a folding table with a rifle count. Do not tell them you
joined a man, because in a year he will be gone, and I would like you to still
have a place to stand when that happens.

### 4.2 The oath

*There is an oath, and it is the only one in the game, and it happens on a dirt
range in the middle of a Thursday with two hundred other men at various stages of
the same afternoon. There is no ceremony. It is a form with four lines and a wait
for a pencil.*

**Arlie Dugas:** Line one, your name and where you are from. Line two, who can
find your family in under a day. That is not a threat. That is so somebody can
send somebody, and every man who has ever needed that has been glad somebody had
it.

**Player:** Line three.

**Arlie Dugas:** Line three is the one people skip, and it is the only one that
matters. Whatever you are told about this section by somebody who is not me is
wrong until you ask me. If a governor tells you there is a bonus, there is no
bonus. If a man in a bar tells you we hold the north, we do not hold the north
yet. Line four is the date. Sign.

**Player:** And the swearing part.

**Arlie Dugas:** There is no swearing part. I have asked four thousand men to
swear on things, and the ones who could not say it were all the ones I wanted. If
you want to say something, say it to yourself on the drive home, because in six
weeks you are going to be too tired to say anything to anybody, and I would
rather you were tired than eloquent.

*He takes the form and looks at it and does not sign it himself, which the player
notices. There is no officer signature on the muster form, and if there is one the
sergeant has not read it.*

### 4.3 If you are turned away

**Arlie Dugas:** The camp is full, and it is full of men I watched walk out of
this gate in June, and I do not have a second tent, and I am not going to
improvise a hungry one on your account.

**Arlie Dugas:** You are looking for a section with a flag, a speech, and a man
whose name you can tell your grandchildren. That is not us. We are a camp, a
range, and a schedule. We are the fastest thing on this map for ninety days and
then we are the smallest thing in the southeast. Both of those are true at the
same time and there is no version of the talk where only one of them is true.

**Arlie Dugas:** I liked you and I cannot use you. Take the northbound truck, get
to a section with a hundred year plan. I will not say a bad word about it in this
gate.

**Arlie Dugas:** You are too far along, and I mean your age and your history, and
it is a compliment and it is a problem. Men with your kind of experience think in
weeks. This is a Tuesday for a year, and then it is a Tuesday for nine years,
and the men who burn out in month four are the ones who thought it was a
campaign.

**Arlie Dugas:** The south will feed you, and I have seen men take a meal at a
woman's table in a parish and decide the whole section was worth it. I am not
judging. I am telling you that you will be hungry for a different kind of thing
out here than you are hungry for now, and I do not want to be the man who watched
you find that out.
### 4.4 Promotion to Line Captain

*Given by Arlie Dugas himself, on the range, in the last hour of light, with the
range card in his hand and forty men who just watched somebody else get sent in
as the movers. He is not proud of it. He is doing a job he has done eight times
and every time has thought about quitting afterward.*

**Arlie Dugas:** Card. Take it. You are a line captain, which means you take
forty men and a truck and you take them where I said, and the reason you are
getting it is that the man I would have picked cannot do the second Tuesday, and
I need somebody who can do all of the Tuesdays, and that is a lower bar than you
think and nobody ever clears it.

**Player:** Thank you, sergeant.

**Arlie Dugas:** Do not. And I want to be straight with you about what I just
did, because you will find out in a year and I would rather you heard it from a
man with a range card. The man I wanted is standing in the third row, and he is
better than you, and he has a bad back and a family in Chattanooga. I did not
tell him yet, and I am going to tell him tonight, and he will be fine, because
his family is the reason I did not put him on a truck for the rest of the summer.

**Player:** Would you have taken the job.

**Arlie Dugas:** In a minute. He has a bad back and I would not have said no.
You are getting it because a man with a family in Chattanooga told me truthfully
what he could not do. That is the whole of my opinion of you.

**Player:** What does a line captain actually decide.

**Arlie Dugas:** Almost nothing, and that is the discipline of it. You decide
where the truck stops and you decide who walks. Everything else is not yours, and
if you touch it somebody in Atlanta will have a reason. There is one decision
that is yours and it is the bad one. It is a Tuesday in October. Somebody is
going to be standing at the end of a road with a truck of men, he is going to want
to go forward, and there is going to be a reason to go forward. If you go forward
you are a man who decided. If you do not, you are a man who refused. Both of
those are going to be held against you and one of them is going to be right.

*How the room reacts: the man in the third row watches the whole thing without any
expression, which the player will see again later in a much worse context. The
file should make sure the player can recognize him.*

### 4.5 Promotion to Field Commander

*Given by Governor Dumas, out of Louisiana, in a room in Baton Rouge with the
windows open. He is the only leader in the game who hands you a rank without
pretending to have grown you. Dumas does not care about you. He wants a commander
in the Mississippi counties, he has three candidates, and one of them is a
problem he cannot fire. He tells you all of this.*

**Governor Dumas:** I have known you five weeks. I am going to hand you four
counties and a river, and I am not going to pretend to have watched you develop
into the man who can hold it, because I have not, and time is a thing I am short
of. I will tell you why you. The other two candidates I cannot use.

**Player:** Why can you not use them.

**Governor Dumas:** One is mine, in the sense that he would run the parishes for
me instead of for the state, and I have enough of those. One is Chambliss's, and
I would rather have a stranger with a boat than a man in Tennessee who is loyal
to a church.

**Player:** And if I fail.

**Governor Dumas:** Then the river is open in September and I have to explain to
four parishes why, and I will not blame you, because blaming you is cheap, and
because I am the one who put an unknown man in a boat in the first place. I want
that on the table at the start, so you do not spend four months trying to be safe.

**Player:** What do you want from me.

**Governor Dumas:** The river, in October, with maybe nine hundred men, and I
want to know every morning by six where those nine hundred are, because I do not
have an intelligence service and I am not going to grow one in a flood season.
That is the job. A chair and a wire and a habit.

**Player:** You are asking me to report to you and to Jessup.

**Governor Dumas:** Yes. And here is the part that will make your life
interesting, so I will say it out loud. I do not care what Jessup thinks about
that, and I have known him six years, and I have never once told him a lie that
could be checked, and he knows that is the only reason I am still in this section.
He also knows the reason I am still in this section is not loyalty, and we have
never once said that out loud either.

**Player:** What is the reason.

**Governor Dumas:** He is the only man in the south who can hold a room. I hate
it, I depend on it, and I would leave tomorrow if he asked me to.

*How the room reacts: two people at the table who are not Dumas, both Compact
men, both taking this in. One of them is a man the player will later have to
decide about. The room now knows Dumas said he would leave, and the room did not
expect that, and Dumas did not intend to say it.*

### 4.6 Promotion to Muster Officer

*Given by Cordell Jessup, on the porch in Atlanta, with forty people there,
because Jessup will not do a promotion in a room with four people in it. There
is a game on the table that nobody is playing. He has asked for this, and it cost
him something to ask for, and he does the whole thing warm and fast and perfect,
and the room loves it, and the player is now the second most useful man in the
south.*

*He comes out to the rail first, shakes the hand, holds it half a second longer
than a stranger requires, and that is the method and it is also the actual
reason.*

**Cordell Jessup:** Friend. You have got a muddy shirt and I have got a clean
one, and between the two of us that is the whole story of the next four months.
Come here. Everybody stop pretending to play this game.

**Player:** What is happening.

**Cordell Jessup:** You are a muster officer, which is the least romantic name in
the English language. I have renamed it twice and both times somebody in
Tennessee complained, so we are leaving it. It means you can call men, put them
in a field, and feed them, and you do not have to ask me. That is the entire
rank. There is no ceremony, there is no flag, there is no speech, and if you were
hoping for the speech I am genuinely sorry, because I have given that speech four
times and every single time a man went home and stopped calling anybody in.

**Player:** Why me.

**Cordell Jessup:** Because you have done the unglamorous version of this four
times and told nobody about it, and I found out from a sergeant, which means you
did it for the right reason.

**Player:** What is the wrong reason.

**Cordell Jessup:** Doing it so somebody would find out. I have watched forty men
do that, and every one of them burned out by the second summer, because a man who
is working to be seen is never finished. You have been doing it to be here. I
checked. It took me a while.

**Player:** What can I actually do with it.

**Cordell Jessup:** Here is the part I do not say on porches. I have got four
hundred thousand men who own a rifle and have been told their whole lives to keep
one in the truck, and I can call them in days. Ninety days after the call we are
better than anybody on this map, and after ninety days I have fewer cards than
everybody, and that is the deal I have been given, and I play it.

**Player:** Then what is a muster officer for.

**Cordell Jessup:** That is the whole game, friend. The first ninety days win
wars. After that you are slower and poorer than everyone, and what you have is
the section, and the section is ten states that have to decide they are one
section without me, and I am trying to make that happen before I go, and I am
going to be honest with you, which I am not often, and pay attention.

*How the room reacts: forty people applaud, and this is the only place in the
game where a promotion gets applause. Jessup hears it and does something with his
face that the player cannot read, and it is worth watching twice. Dumas is not
here. Nobody from Tennessee is here. The room is entirely people who already like
him, and he built it that way, and the player has just been given a rank inside
the most reliable man in America's gift.*

### 4.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*He comes to the porch. Alone. It is four in the afternoon and he is not
working, which is how the player knows before he says anything at all. He sits
down in the chair he has never once sat in.*

**Cordell Jessup:** Okay. I am going to do this the worst possible way, which is
sitting down, and I am asking you to let me, because I have got about four
minutes before somebody comes out here and it turns into a scene with an audience,
and I have watched a man's entire life get decided in a scene with an audience.

**Player:** Go ahead.

**Cordell Jessup:** I am not going to ask why. If I ask why, you will tell me, and
I will forgive you, because that is what I do, and then in six months somebody is
going to tell me a story about how I found out. I would rather spend the six
months. I know that is me being cowardly about it. I have thought about it and I
am doing it anyway.

**Player:** I would have told you.

**Cordell Jessup:** I know, and I have known since March, and I have watched
three of your messages come in that you did not have to send, which is the
strangest thing about this and the part I cannot get past. You are not a traitor,
friend. You are a man who decided the section had a plan and it did not, and I
did not have a plan, and I have been in Atlanta for six years pretending that a
schedule and a personality is a plan.

**Player:** It is not that.

**Cordell Jessup:** It is completely that. I have got a hundred and forty
thousand men who would come if I called, and I have never worked out what I am
calling them for, and you worked it out, and then you told somebody in Tennessee,
and I would have done the same thing, and it took you four months longer than it
should have, which is a compliment from me and you should hear it as one.

**Player:** What happens now.

**Cordell Jessup:** Here is what I am not going to do. I am not going to stand
up on that porch and make an announcement, because an announcement is a story,
and a story is a thing a man carries for the rest of his life, and you have done
ninety days of good work and I would like you to keep it. I am going to pull your
call. Tonight, quietly, through a sergeant. Your name comes off the muster roll
as a transfer. Dupree will have your money counted out, all of it, there is no
dispute, and I am paying it out of my own share rather than the section's.

**Player:** That is not legal.

**Cordell Jessup:** It is not entirely legal, and it is not important, and I
would take that as the last free thing I ever do for anybody. Get off the porch.
Take the four o'clock truck, not the six, the six goes to Dumas. And one more
thing, and then you are a man I do not know.

**Player:** Go ahead.

**Cordell Jessup:** Do not go to Texas for a while. I mean that as a kindness and
it is the only kind I have left. The man down there will make you an offer in one
afternoon that I could not match in a year, and it will be a real offer, and you
will take it, because you have just been inside a room where forty people loved
a man, and that is the most dangerous feeling in America. Go north. Learn a thing
you cannot do. Come back in a year and I will either put you in a room again or
I will not, and I will tell you which.

*How the room reacts: the room does not know, which is the point, and the player
has to carry it. The game should not let them talk to a Southern NPC about this
for two in-game days, because Jessup said forty minutes and he meant it.*

## 5. LONE STAR FRONTIER

The section runs on deals, so there is no oath and joining is a contract, and
the ceremony is that your price is read out loud in front of witnesses. The first
promotion is given at a gate by a ranch foreman. The second is given by a rancher
six hundred miles from anywhere who cannot leave her own land. The top rank is
given by Adair, who explains cheerfully that the player is now the only person
who can commit the section, and that this is a burden and not a prize, and he
does not apologize for it.

### 5.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Chet Iverson |
| Age | 55 |
| Home | Ranch country outside San Angelo. Thirty years, eleven of them for Adair |
| Role | Contract foreman. He signs, delivers, and explains, in that order |
| Talks like | Acres, fences, weather, and price. He states his price before you ask |
| Wants | Somebody who shows up on the fourth day of a nine day drive and is still useful |

*He is at a gate with a truck behind him that is not clean and is not going to be.
He does not shake your hand for about a second, which is a test, and he passes it
by not watching you pass it.*

**Chet Iverson:** You are the man from the Corridor job.

**Player:** I am.

**Chet Iverson:** Two hundred miles of dirt through country you do not control,
in nine days, with sixty men and forty of them walking. My people said sixteen.
My people have never run supply through that road. So either you are better than
they are or you are lucky, and I do not have time to find out which, so I am
going to put you on a route and see.

**Player:** What route.

**Chet Iverson:** Abilene to Amarillo, twice a month, with a load of whatever the
man in Abilene says is in it, and back with whatever the man in Amarillo says he
is buying. I am telling you both numbers in advance, because that is how the
trade works, and the people who cannot do that are the reason the ranch business
died.

**Player:** What do you pay.

**Chet Iverson:** Four hundred and eighty a run, fuel on the company card, and
eleven percent of anything you bring back that I did not know about. That last
one is not a bonus, it is a rule, and I would like to explain it, because nobody
explains it. If I want the information, I have to pay for it. If I take it off
your number, then the next man lies to me about the price, and then I am the man
who lied to a rancher, and there is no third thing.

**Player:** And the section.

**Chet Iverson:** What about it.

**Player:** I want to know what I am joining.

**Chet Iverson:** I will tell you what I tell everybody. You are joining a man
with more room than people, who has run out of people to fill it more times than
he has won a fight. There is a lot of nothing in here and a long way between any
two things. If you want your work seen, you will be seen in a month. If you want
to be stretched thin for nine years, that is also what you are buying. Out here
it is always both.

**Player:** Who is Adair.

**Chet Iverson:** The governor, in the way that a big rancher is a governor. He
will be direct with you, and it will feel like rudeness for about a year, and then
you will meet somebody else and figure out what you are actually being sold. Do
not shake on anything with him in front of anybody. He will make you do it in
front of anybody.

**Player:** Why.

**Chet Iverson:** Because he wants you to know exactly what you were bought
with. He has never met a man who could not hear that sentence and want to walk
away, and he would rather lose you here than find out about you in March.

### 5.2 The oath

*There is no oath. There is a truck, a scale ticket, and two witnesses, and Chet
reads the deal out loud including the number, and if the player says anything
that is not the number, Chet makes them say the number.*

*Adair is there. He stands behind the truck the entire time and does not
intervene, which is the whole of his participation in the ceremony.*

**Chet Iverson:** Read it back to me.

**Player:** Four hundred and eighty a run, fuel on the card, eleven percent of
anything new I bring you.

**Chet Iverson:** Now the part people skip. Say the other part.

**Player:** Any deal, loan, or offer I am offered, I bring to you first.

**Chet Iverson:** Say the rest.

**Player:** Any deal, loan, or offer I am offered, I bring to you first, and I do
not accept it.

**Chet Iverson:** Now say the number again and mean it.

**Player:** Four hundred and eighty.

**Royce Adair:** That is it. That is the whole ceremony. Most sections ask you to
swear loyalty to an idea. Chet made you say a number twice, which is harder, and
it took him forty seconds, and I have watched men give a day on that porch and
get less out of it.

**Player:** Why no flag.

**Royce Adair:** Because a flag is a thing you put on your land and call
partnership, and I have turned down six offers in six years and I said no on the
seventh too, and every one of them started with a flag and a very nice man from
the coast. You are on a truck. The truck is the flag. If the deal is good we both
know where we stand, and if it is not, we both know that too, and I would rather
have that than a ceremony that means I owe somebody something I cannot say out
loud.

*How the room reacts: Chet signs the ticket, the two witnesses sign, and Adair
is already walking back to his own truck. Nothing else happens. It is the least
ceremonious initiation in the file, and the only one where the player leaves
knowing what he is worth.*

### 5.3 If you are turned away

**Chet Iverson:** I have four rigs and four drivers and two of them are mine
better than the other two, and I am not adding a fifth to make a good impression.
Take the union scale work in Oklahoma City. It is a third less and it starts on
time.

**Chet Iverson:** You are going to hate it out here, and I would rather tell you
now than have you find out on the fourth day of a nine day drive with a truck
that is not making money. There is no flag, no ceremony, no lord, and no promised
future. There is a gate, a number, and a lot of miles. Men who come for the
other three things do not last a season.

**Chet Iverson:** I like you and I have no rig. Take I-35 south, the Compact
will put you on a run this week and pay you on time, and I have no hard feelings
about it at all.

**Chet Iverson:** You want a section that can feed you and a flag to hang on the
wall. This section cannot feed you every year. The section up north can, and it
will feed you by owning you a little at a time, and I have watched men take that
trade at fifty and still be paying at seventy.
### 5.4 Promotion to Trail Boss

*Given by Chet at the same gate eleven weeks later, in front of the same four
rigs. It takes about ninety seconds. It is a pay raise, a radio, and Chet saying
it in front of the other drivers, which is the Frontier version of a speech. They
are not gracious about it, because out here a raise means one of them is not
getting one.*

**Chet Iverson:** Eleven runs, no problems, and one of them you took at two in
the morning because the other truck was in a ditch, and I want everybody here to
notice that he did not get asked. He just went. So you are trail boss, which
means the schedule is yours, the second truck is yours to send, and if somebody
does not like working for you they can take it up with me and I will say
something unhelpful.

**Player:** The raise.

**Chet Iverson:** Six hundred and ten. And eleven percent is now twelve, and I
am going to be honest about why. You are the first driver in eleven weeks who has
brought me something I did not know about, and that was a diesel price in Rankin
that I had wrong by forty cents, and forty cents saved me about nine hundred
dollars, and you did not have to go looking for it, you just wrote it down. That
is the whole job.

**Driver:** Chet, that is fine, but I have been on that run since March.

**Chet Iverson:** And you will be at six hundred and ten in June if March man
here brings me one thing I did not know. Mack, do not look at me like that. I have
been doing this eleven years and the trail boss has been the best driver every
single time.

**Player:** What if the other drivers walk.

**Chet Iverson:** Then I will have a schedule problem in July and I will fix it in
July. That is a solvable problem. Do you know what is not a solvable problem? A
man who will not tell me a diesel price because he is thinking about a promotion.
That is how this section stops working, and that is how I find out six months
late that a piece of ground has been taken.

*How the room reacts: Mack does not take it well, and he will be a working
problem for the player for a year, and Chet knows that and picked him anyway. A
Frontier promotion makes somebody's friend an enemy, and the file should say so
plainly.*

### 5.5 Promotion to Land Partner

*Given by Birdie Lujan, who runs cattle out of Presidio, and who is the reason
the west half of the Frontier functions at all. She has driven four hours to
arrive and is not coming back tonight. She gives the rank in a kitchen, at a
table, with food that has been out a while, and she does it in front of her own
foreman, who is not happy about it.*

**Birdie Lujan:** I am going to say the hard thing first and then we will eat,
because I drove four hours and I am not doing this on an empty stomach and neither
are you. This is not a promotion you earned. This is land nobody has time for,
and I have four hundred head on ground I cannot get to, and a man I trust to go,
and I do not trust men, so you understand what that sentence costs me.

**Player:** I understand.

**Birdie Lujan:** Do not say that yet. Here is what I am actually giving you. Six
sections, and the water rights came with them, and there is a man in El Paso who
has been trying to get a signature on that water since March, and he is patient
and he is polite, and he has been polite at me for two years. You will sign what I
sign and refuse what I refuse. The first time somebody offers you a better deal
than I offered you, you will bring it to me before you accept it. If you do not,
I will take it out of your hide and I will not need a reason in front of a court.

**Player:** Why me over somebody local.

**Birdie Lujan:** Because a man from Presidio who works for me is a man from
Presidio, and the first offer he gets he is going to be tempted by, because it is
for his mother. You are from somewhere with no reason to love this. That is worth
more to me than skill right now. I am aware of how that sounds. I have a daughter.

**Foreman:** Birdie, you cannot do that. Four hundred head is not a lesson. He
will lose the herd in the first month and it will be my fault for letting it.

**Birdie Lujan:** Then it will be yours, and you will be the one who knows every
gate and every windbreak, and he will be the one who does not, and in a year one
of you will be right about the water and I want to find out which.

**Player:** What is on that land.

**Birdie Lujan:** Grass, water, a road that floods twice a year, and a house with
a well and a windmill that works. I have not sold it because I have not met
anybody I wanted to sell it to. Now eat. You have a hundred and forty miles and a
road that is worse than the map says.

*How the room reacts: her foreman argues for ten minutes and then stops, and the
stopping is the promotion. She says nothing sentimental, and the last thing she
says about the house is that the well is ninety feet and the windmill is not
yours to sell, which is the most romantic thing anybody in this file says.*

### 5.6 Promotion to Frontier Signatory

*Given by Royce Adair, at a gate outside San Antonio, not Houston, next to the
same truck with the rifle leaning against the fender. There is a second man
there who has come a long way and is not introduced. The whole conversation is
about what a signature can do, and both of them are entirely serious, and neither
of them says the word honor once.*

**Royce Adair:** Straight to it, because you have been driving two days and the
light is going. A Frontier signatory can commit the section. Not me, not Chet,
not the governor of anywhere. The signatory. You can put a promise in writing that
a rancher six hundred miles from a paved road will accept as the word of this
section, and if you do it wrong, that word will hold your name and the section
will carry it. I have done exactly one of these in six years, and it was the best
decision I ever made, and it is also the reason two governors in Phoenix will not
return my calls.

**Player:** Who is he.

**Royce Adair:** Sit down. That is Hollis Grant, and he drove from Denver to
watch a signing, which is not a thing men from Denver do, and he would like to
say a few words, and I would like to let him, and I will tell you why I am
letting him. In about a year I am going to be asked to sign something that
decides whether a piece of desert stays mine or goes to the mountains, and I
would like a man from the mountains in the room when that happens.

**Hollis Grant:** I am not here to negotiate and I have no authority, which you
will be told anyway. I am here because I have heard that Adair signs on time, and
I have spent six years telling people the Mountain Alliance does not have to be
bought, and I would like to see a man from the east write that down.

**Player:** What am I signing.

**Royce Adair:** That is the question, and it is the right one, and I will give
you a straight answer instead of the good one. When I sign, I am saying this
section will honor a thing, and I can say that because I am rich, independent,
and unbuyable, and I have spent six years being all three. You are not any of
those things yet. You are going to be, in about two years, and the first
signature you take is going to be a lie, because you will be signing before you
have earned it, and every signatory I have ever seen has had to lie once.

**Player:** And then.

**Royce Adair:** Then somebody will come to you with a better offer, because
somebody always does, and they will be very nice about it, and they will not ask
you for anything, and that is how the Atlantic Corridor works. I refinanced them
twice and turned them down twice and that is the whole story and it is very
boring. When it comes, bring it to me. That is the only thing I want from a
signatory, and I have never asked a man for anything else in his life.

*How the room reacts: Grant asks one question, which is whether the Frontier has
ever signed a deal it could not deliver, and Adair says yes, once, and does not
elaborate, and Grant writes it down. That is the only comment Hollis Grant makes
in this file, and it should stay that way, because a second sentence from him
here would be a speech.*

### 5.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*It is not Adair who does it, which is the Frontier's whole advantage. It is
Sheridan Cobb, in a courthouse he keeps in good repair, and the man who sends for
you is Chet, who will not meet your eye. The scene is short and it is entirely a
transaction, and that is what makes it brutal.*

**Sheridan Cobb:** I have three counties, eleven miles of private right of way,
and a man in Phoenix who has been buying signatures in this state for two years.
Your name was on four of them in the last six weeks. I am not going to ask you
whether you took money, because you did not, and I have known that since Tuesday.

**Player:** Then why am I here.

**Sheridan Cobb:** Because you signed four things you were not authorized to
sign, and you did it for free, and you did it because you believed the Frontier
would honor them, and I need to know whose word you were carrying, because that
tells me whose word is going to be in my county in October.

**Player:** Adair's.

**Sheridan Cobb:** Adair has not signed a thing a Phoenix man could use in six
years, and the reason is that he is a rich man who can afford to lose. You are
not, yet, and you signed anyway, and I am going to be honest with you, which is
that I could have used you for the next two years instead of having this
conversation.

**Player:** Why not.

**Sheridan Cobb:** Because I sell the wire everybody fences with, and I am not
going to be a man who put a fence up on a lie. That is the only sentence I have,
and I am aware it is not much of a reason.

**Chet:** I have your gear in the truck. The account is closed out, all of it,
and fuel is on the card until Friday.

**Player:** Chet, look at me.

**Chet:** No, I am not going to, and I will tell you why, and then I will go load
the truck. I put you on that road. If I had put somebody else on it, Cobb would
have got a stranger with a signature, and a stranger can be frightened, and you
could not be, and that is how I would have slept. I do not think about that at
two in the morning. I have not slept at two in the morning since March and I
have not mentioned it to a single person, and I am mentioning it to you because
you are the last one I get to say it to.

**Player:** You are putting me out.

**Chet:** Adair is putting you out, and he asked me to, and he is going to do it
in the open, and you should let him. A man who was put out by a foreman he liked
is a man who gets hired back in a year. A man who was put out by the section is a
story in every gate from Amarillo to Laredo. I hate that this is true.

**Player:** Does he know what I signed.

**Sheridan Cobb:** He knows. He knew Tuesday. He is not putting you out for the
signing, he is putting you out because you signed it without him, and he has been

*How the room reacts: nobody raises a voice, nobody threatens him, and the whole
scene is over in about six minutes. The player walks out of a courthouse with a

## 6. ATLANTIC CORRIDOR

The section is an administration, so the oath is a signature card and the rank
structure is a job grade. Nobody in the Corridor has ever been given a title that
did not come with a filing requirement. The first promotion is given by a woman
with a card catalog in her head who apologizes for it and does not stop. The
second is given by a district supervisor who is openly grooming the player for a
successor he has already decided about. The top rank is given by Yvonne Castille,
who explains that the player is now the switch, and that she is trying to stop
being the switch, and that she needs to be honest about what that makes him.

### 6.1 The recruiter

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Harriet Lowndes |
| Age | 44 |
| Home | Born in Buffalo. Twelve years in the Corridor, eight of them in a loan office |
| Role | Loan officer, grade two, which is the level that can approve small facilities |
| Talks like | Precise and conditional. She says if, and she means if, and it reads as kindness |
| Wants | Somebody who is comfortable being dull for two years |

*The meeting is a room with a window, a working radiator, and a printer going,
booked under a freight company that does not exist, which she will explain and
then decline to finish explaining. There are two paper cups and a carafe. She
pours without asking and does not sit until the player does, which is the
opposite of every other recruiter in this file.*

**Harriet Lowndes:** Thank you for coming in. I am sorry about the building. It
is not that kind of building. Sit anywhere. The water is from a tap I have
tested, which I will not bore you about.

**Player:** Where are we.

**Harriet Lowndes:** A freight office that does not exist, on the fourth floor,
and I am not going to tell you which freight company, because that is the kind
of thing that is written on a sign somewhere, and I have looked. I have looked
carefully, because I am not a person who enjoys being surprised.

**Player:** What is this.

**Harriet Lowndes:** If it is a loan, it is a loan, and I will show you the
schedule before you leave, which nobody in this building has ever done, and I
say that as a woman who has watched what happens when they do not. If it is a
job, it is a job, and I can be honest with you about the ceiling, which is lower
than the money, and the money is the best on this map.

**Player:** What kind of job.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Unglamorous. You would read. You would carry. You would
build a relationship with a man in a state you have never visited who currently
despises the person you work for, and you would do it without ever telling him
what you are doing, and in two years you would be the reason this section still
eats.

**Player:** Who am I working for.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Ms. Castille, and I would like to be careful here, because
people hear that name and they decide something about the room. She is the most
competent person in the eastern half of this country and the least liked person
in it. Both of those are facts. I have worked beside her for four years and I
have never once seen her lie about a number.

**Player:** Is she liked.

**Harriet Lowndes:** No. And she has tried twice to be liked, and it read as a
tactic both times, and she has decided the problem is her, and she has stopped.
That is not my opinion. That is what she told the whole loan office in a meeting,
in those words, and it was the strangest thing I have heard a senior person say
in a professional context.

**Player:** What is in it for me.

**Harriet Lowndes:** The best rates in North America. I will not dress that up.
You will be able to buy a house this year. If you come in with a number I can
usually meet it, and I have met numbers this month that no other section on this
map would have looked at.

**Player:** And the other part.

**Harriet Lowndes:** The other part is that you will also get a loan, and the loan
is the thing to think about, because the wage is a wage and the loan is a
relationship, and relationships have terms, and the terms will be in front of you,
in writing, on this desk, and I will leave the room while you read them if you
want.

**Player:** Why would you leave the room.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Because a man reading a schedule with somebody standing
over him is not reading it. Ms. Castille has done that to people for six years,
and it is why half of them are still in this section and the other half are gone.
Sit. Read. I am going to get the second file.

*How the room reacts: the printer runs the whole time. It is the first recruiter
in this file whose room has a sound that keeps going after everybody stops
talking.*

### 6.2 The oath

*In the same room, with a signature card, a pen, and a line that has to be read
back. There is no ceremony, but the reading back is treated like one, and the
reason is given out loud.*

**Harriet Lowndes:** This is a signature card. It is not a symbol. It is the
piece of paper that means you can move a specific amount of money in a specific
direction, and the only thing in the world standing between the person you are
helping and a bad afternoon is the discipline of somebody who read the schedule
twice.

**Player:** There is no oath.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Correct, and I will tell you why, because people find it
strange and I have stopped being able to say it without explaining. A section
that makes you swear a promise is asking for a feeling. This one gives you a
document, and the document is better, because a feeling can be worn down by
October and a document is still sitting there in October.

**Player:** Read the line.

**Harriet Lowndes:** "The undersigned is authorized to execute on behalf of the
section only the terms set out in the schedule attached, and is not authorized to
vary, extend, or forgive any of them without the countersignature of the office."
Now the part I actually care about. Say the last five words.

**Player:** Without the countersignature of the office.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Good. Now say this, and I will initial it. I have watched
nine men in this chair learn that sentence, and it is the only piece of knowledge
in this building that protects you, and I want it in your hand and not in a
drawer. If somebody comes to you, and somebody will, and they ask you to forgive
a term, you say those five words, and then you say, gently, that it is not your
decision and you are sorry.

**Player:** What if it is mine.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Then I will be very interested, and I will not help you, and
I will hand it to Ms. Castille, and she will be extremely fair, and it will take
fourteen months. Sign. There is a date on there, and the date is the part that
matters.

### 6.3 If you are turned away

**Harriet Lowndes:** I am not able to approve your file, and I want to give you
the actual reason rather than a warm one, because you will get a warm one from
whoever is holding your application and it will not be true. You have no
references from a state that owes us money, which means the office cannot assess
your exposure, and I am not going to guess at it.

**Harriet Lowndes:** You have applied to four offices in this building. I know,
because they all report to me. I would like to know what you were told at each
one, because if they told you we are short handed I would like to correct it in
writing, since you are going to repeat it to somebody.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Nobody in this section is going to tell you that you matter.
Not because we do not think you do, and not because this is a trick, but because
the office that is generous with praise is the office that is short on what you
can do. If you need a section that says it, take the Pacific Compact, where they
hand you a manifest your first morning and tell you that you are the reason a
valley gets fed.

**Harriet Lowndes:** You want a job with a ceiling you can see. This one has no
ceiling I can describe and no floor, and I have watched people with better
starting positions than yours leave in two years because nothing happened to them.
That is not a warning about you. That is a warning about the work.

**Harriet Lowndes:** Take the money in Texas if it is on the table. I have
watched that section hire six men on a Monday, and it pays on the first of the
month, and if you are at the point where you need a certain answer, a certain
answer is worth more than an excellent one.
### 6.4 Promotion to District Agent

*Given by Howard Lasker, district supervisor for six years, in a room in Albany
with a view of a parking lot. He is apologetic in a way that is clearly practice.
The folder is open and the recommendation is already written, which he admits.*

**Howard Lasker:** I want to start by saying that I wrote this recommendation in
March, and I have been carrying it since March, and I have not sent it because
of what I am about to ask you.

**Player:** Ask.

**Howard Lasker:** Two years. I want two years out of you before the next one. Not
for the office. For you. I have been a district agent for six years and I will be
honest about the rest of it. The job is learning how money moves, and that takes
two years of boring, and then four more of carrying it, and then somebody
retires and you are standing there with all six years and no personality. I am the
man who does that now.

**Player:** Why not promote me now.

**Howard Lasker:** Because I promoted a man in March and he is good, and I have
watched him for two years since, and he is excellent at the work and cannot be in
a room with a governor without a script. I made a mistake, and I would like to
spend the two years of your life preventing it from happening to you.

**Player:** What do I do with the two years.

**Howard Lasker:** Everything nobody sees. You will read. You will carry. You
will build a relationship with somebody in Omaha or Providence or Albany who
despises Ms. Castille and would never admit it to her face, and the job is to
make them say it to you, and the trick, the actual trick, is that you never ask
them to.

**Player:** How do I do that.

**Howard Lasker:** You bring a message that is not important and you make it feel
important. And once a year you do them a favor that costs you money and that
nobody ever hears about, and the second time you do it you will get a phone call
in February from a man who has not called you since June.

**Player:** And Ms. Castille is going to leave.

**Howard Lasker:** Ms. Castille is fifty-seven and she is the only person in the
eastern half of this country who can write a check, and the day she is not, this
section loses every negotiation it is in within a month. She knows it. She has a
plan to distribute her function, the people attached to the plan are serious, and
none of them is you yet.

**Player:** Is she leaving.

**Howard Lasker:** I have no idea, and I would like you to notice that I just
told a stranger a great deal about the most private strategic problem in the
Corridor. I have done that four times now, and three of those times the person
turned out to be a disaster. I am telling you because you will find it out in two
years, and I would like you to find it out with a warning attached.

*How the room reacts: nobody applauds and nobody objects. He signs the
recommendation, initials the date, and the promotion is a two year sentence
written on a form, which is the most Corridor thing in the entire file and should
read that way.*

### 6.5 Promotion to Portfolio Manager

*Given by a rival, Leland Shay, which the player does not see coming, and this is
the only promotion in the file given by somebody who is not in the section. He is
technically a former official of the Commonwealth and still says it that way, and
he invites the player to his house, and his house has four counties of quiet in
it.*

**Leland Shay:** I am going to be frank with you, because the woman who signs
your promotion is going to be frank with you in about forty minutes, and I would
rather you had this first.

**Player:** Go on.

**Leland Shay:** I run four counties in Virginia that have never missed a court
date and have never had a homeless family, under a jurisdiction that does not
technically exist, and every serious person in the Corridor knows it, and
Castille would like to move on me, and she has been trying for two years and has
not been able to invent a reason better than governing four counties well.

**Player:** And you want me to go with you.

**Leland Shay:** I want you to know what you are being promoted into, because
nobody in that building is going to tell you, and I would rather be the man who
told you. A portfolio manager can forgive a term. That is the first thing you
learn and nobody says it out loud. Ms. Castille said it to you herself, I would
put money on it, and she said it as a warning and meant it exactly that way.

**Player:** I am not going with you.

**Leland Shay:** I know, and I am not asking. I am asking you to hold a door
open. Because in about two years one of those four counties is going to lose its
clinic, and the Corridor is going to have a reason it can write down, and if there
is a man in that building who has met me and has not been frightened off, then
there is a person who can say, in a meeting, provided that, and the room goes
quiet.

**Player:** You want a spy.

**Leland Shay:** I want a witness. I am sixty-six and I am running out of the kind
of energy that builds a relationship, and I have spent six years being extremely
reasonable, and reasonableness is a thing that works until somebody needs a person
to be unreasonable. Keep the card. You will not have to use it for a long time,
and if you never use it, I will have wasted a very pleasant afternoon.

**Player:** Why are you telling me any of this.

**Leland Shay:** Because you are the second man this year who has turned this
down on principle, and I would like there to be a third, and I would like the
Corridor to have to keep being careful. That is not charity, it is a hedge. I am
not a sentimental man. I have a cardigan and a court, and both of them are
working.

*How the room reacts: the player is in a stranger's kitchen, not a room of
officials, and the offer is the friendliest thing anybody has said in this file.
It is also a plot to put a sympathetic man inside the most powerful
administration in the country.*

### 6.6 Promotion to Authorizing Officer

*Given by Yvonne Castille, in the borrowed room above a shipping office in
Philadelphia, booked under a freight company that does not exist. She is already
seated and she is not reading anything. There is a carafe and two cups. Nobody
else is in the room, which is the first time in this file that a promotion
happens with no audience, and the player should notice the absence.*

**Yvonne Castille:** Thank you for coming. I have put a letter in front of you
and I am not going to summarize it, because a summary is a version, and I would
rather you had the document.

**Player:** I have read it.

**Yvonne Castille:** Then you know that an authorizing officer can forgive a term.
I did that in an interview years ago, to a man who is no longer in this building,

I meant it, and that the letter in front of you does not give you that power. It

**Player:** What is the one that matters.

**Yvonne Castille:** That you are the switch. I am the switch now. The office is

month of the day I am not here.

**Player:** And this fixes it.

**Yvonne Castille:** This does not fix it. I want to be extremely clear about

then believe they had solved a structural problem by being competent at it, and

thing collapses on me. What it does is make the switch a person who can be
replaced, which is the only improvement available in my time.

**Player:** What happens if I refuse.

**Yvonne Castille:** Then you will be extremely good at a job I can describe in
one sentence, and you will be here in nine years, and I will have found somebody
else, and you will be right that you could have done more. I would like you to go
home and think about it for a week, and I would like you to know that a week is
not a test. It is the only thing in this building that is not a test.

**Player:** And if I accept.

**Yvonne Castille:** Then you will spend the next year with a governor in
Providence who currently despises me and would never admit it to my face, and
you will carry a message that is not important and make it feel important, and
you will come back and tell me it is important, and I will let you, because the
work needs the belief more than it needs the accuracy.

**Player:** You said two years.

**Yvonne Castille:** I said two years before you could be useful. I did not say

this section, and I want you to have that information at the same time as the
letter, and not three years into the job.

**Player:** If the Corridor ran out of money tomorrow, and you were gone, and the
imports stopped, what would you do. Would you hold the city, open the
warehouses, or leave.

*She asked the player the same question in the first meeting. She waits, and she
does not help, and this time the player knows she is checking whether she told
them the truth about the job.*

**Yvonne Castille:** Take your time. I have never asked you a question I did not
already know the answer to, and if I have, I would like to be told, and I would
take that as a compliment.

### 6.7 Betrayal and expulsion

*She does it in the same room in Philadelphia, booked under a freight company
that does not exist, with the letter that gave the player the job and a second
letter. There is no audience. The carafe is not refilled. It is the quietest
violent scene in this file and it is done entirely in conditional sentences.*

**Yvonne Castille:** I am going to read clause nine, and then I am going to give
you a copy, and I would like you to know before I start that I have practised
this in my head about four times, and that the version I practised was kinder
than the one I am about to do, and I have removed the kinder parts.

**Player:** Read it.

**Yvonne Castille:** "Any authorizing officer who varies, extends, or forgives a
term of schedule without the countersignature of the office forfeits the office,
the schedule authority, and the indemnity, and shall deliver the records of the
account within five business days." That is clause nine. You varied a term in
March. You did it in writing, you did it for a family in Brockton, and you told
me about it afterward, and I want to record that you told me afterward, because
in six years that has happened twice, and both times the man gave me the letter
and the explanation in the same envelope.

**Player:** What is the penalty.

**Yvonne Castille:** You keep the wage. That is the humane part, and I have
removed it from the script in my head, but it is true, so you should have it. You
do not keep the authority, the schedule authority, or the indemnity, and without
the indemnity you are personally exposed for the balance, which is a number I
will give you in writing. The number is not small. I have already arranged for a
firm to handle it, and I have paid the first installment myself, out of the
office, and if you ask me why I will tell you it is because I would like there to
be exactly one person in this country who treats you decently today, and it is
going to be a person you no longer work for.

**Player:** It is not your fault.

**Yvonne Castille:** It is entirely my fault, and I want to correct you, because
this is the part I have actually been dreading, and it is the part nobody ever
believes. I gave you the power to refuse a payment that would ruin a family. I
told you it was a worse job in every way except the one that matters. And I did
not build a single mechanism into this office for a person to use that power
without the corridor above them finding out within nine months, because the
corridor above them is me, and I did not want a second me, and I have been the
only one for six years because I decided I did not want one.

**Player:** So Brockton is on you.

**Yvonne Castille:** Brockton is on both of us, and you carried it, and you
carried it well, and the reason it is on me is that I built a system with one
switch in it, and then I held the switch myself for six years to keep it from
being abused, and I was too tired to notice that holding it was the abuse.

**Player:** What happens to the section.

**Yvonne Castille:** I will find somebody by Thursday. It will take six weeks and
it will be worse than you, because you knew the governors and you knew me, and
the person after you will know a spreadsheet and four people in Providence. That
is the cost, and I have put it in writing in the second letter, and the second
letter is dated today, and it does not name you as the cause. I would like you to
notice that I have protected you in a document nobody else will ever read, which
is a thing I learned from a man who taught me administration in nineteen
seventy-nine, and he is dead.

**Player:** And your version.

**Yvonne Castille:** My version is that I recruited well, I trained an officer
who exceeded his instructions, and I failed to build a system that could survive
his conscience. That is true, it is publishable, and it is going in the annual
record. I have written it, and it is in the drawer with the letter from March.

*How the room reacts: the room does not react. There is no room. That is the
Corridor's version of this, and the player should be left with the specific
discomfort of having been the only audience.*

**Yvonne Castille:** One more thing, and it is a professional courtesy rather
than a kindness. Do not take a position with Leland Shay's people, and I am not
telling you that because I suspect it. I am telling you because he came to see me
in January, and he did it politely, and he is a courteous man, and I would like
you to know the decision has been made for reasons, so that you do not spend two
years feeling you were clever.

**Player:** Was I.

**Yvonne Castille:** No. You were three weeks from a decision I had already made
in January about a different matter entirely. That is the honest version, and I
would like you to have it, because the version where it was about you is a better
story, and I have watched better stories keep this section running for six years.

## THE WANDERER PATH

A Wanderer has no section, so the player gets no ladder, no oath, and none of the
promotions in this file. The only thing the Wanderer gets is a number from
nobody, and every section above will offer them something within the first month.
What a Wanderer can have instead:

1. **A rate card.** Every run, every job, every contract, priced in the open by
   the section, the notable, or the merchant. A Wanderer is the only player in
   the game who can always find out what something pays.
2. **No loyalty penalties anywhere.** The player can work for all six, in

### What a promotion writes

Every first and second promotion should write at least two things beyond the
player's own rank: one to the section, one to a neighbouring section. The
neighbouring write is the point. A Great Lakes Union local captain is a fact in
Omaha and a fact in Cleveland and a fact in Omaha's biggest competitor at the
same time. The rival reactions above are the surface text for that write.

### Rules for anything added later

1. Plain American English. Contractions. Fragments are fine and often better.
2. One thought per line. If a speaker has two ideas, it is two lines.
3. No em dashes, no en dashes, no ellipsis characters, no smart quotes.
4. A recruiter never promises the player a rank. Six recruiters, six reasons, no
   promises, because the promise is what the leader gives.
5. The leader who gives a top rank and the leader who expels the player are
   different people wherever the lore allows it. Only the Corridor does both
   in the same room, and that is the Corridor's flaw.
6. Every rejection line has to be true on the faction's terms. A rejection
   because a recruiter does not like the player is a bug. A rejection because
   there is no hole in the schedule, no crew, no truck, no budget, no reference
   from a debtor state, and no chair is content.
7. A betrayal scene is never a fight. Every one of these is somebody deciding to
   end it, and every one of them costs the player a relationship they had
   earned. The leaders who turn up in person are the ones who could not live
   with not turning up.
8. No real people, companies, or events. Real places, fictional everything else.
