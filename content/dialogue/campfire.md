# CAMPFIRE

Rest scenes. The hours between two fights, when the party has picked a place off
the road, put the trucks in a ring, and somebody has started a fire that is
mostly for light. These are the only scenes in the game where nobody is asking
the player for anything.

Read content/characters/companions.md first. Every voice in this file is already
published there, with its traits, its flaw, and its wage. Nothing here upgrades
a companion, resolves a flaw, or gives anybody a plan they do not have. These are
eight working adults sitting down.

Rules for anything added here later:

- Real geography, fictional people. No real person, company, or event appears
  here. Per CONSTITUTION.md section 6.
- Plain ASCII throughout. No em dashes, no en dashes, no curly quotes, no
  ellipsis characters, no smart punctuation of any kind.
- Plain spoken American English. Contractions. Fragments. Regional rhythm.
  Nobody talks like a briefing document.
- One thought per line in dialogue. Nobody delivers a paragraph at a fire.
- Stage directions go in asterisks on their own line and are never mixed into
  somebody's speech.
- No speeches. A campfire line that could be said standing up should be said
  sitting down.
- Nobody is brave tonight. Tired people at a fire argue about small things
  because the large thing is too big to hold, and that is the entire texture of
  this file.
- No marketing language anywhere. Nobody is battle tested. Nobody is hardened.
  Nobody calls this war anything other than the thing they are in.

## 1. HOW THIS FILE IS USED

Three kinds of scene, all fired from a rest event.

**Conversations.** Two or three companions talking with nobody else around. Pick
by which companions are alive and in the party, then by what happened in the
last battle, then by whether anybody is hurt. A conversation does not require the
player to be present. Several of them are better if the player is not there,
because that is the entire point of a campfire.

**Night watch.** One character alone, thinking out loud, with nobody to hear it.
These fire on watch rotation. They are not addressed to the player and they are
not addressed to anybody. They exist so that a companion who is in the party for
forty hours is still a person at hour thirty.

**Questions.** A companion asks the player something, the player answers, and
the companion has a different reply for each answer. Four of these in this file.
They are the only place in this file where the player speaks.

**What a campfire writes to.** Companion morale, and one companion's opinion of
the player. Nothing else. A campfire does not change a quest, does not unlock a
recruit, and does not hand out a favor. If a campfire scene ever starts doing
those things it has stopped being this file.

**Cast index.** Every companion appears at least twice, and no companion appears
in more than six conversations, so the file never becomes one loud person.

## 2. CONVERSATIONS

### 2.1 WHAT IS LEFT TO EAT

**Who is here.** Cody Bravo, Odessa Pryor, Wendell Bragg. The fire is four hours
old and down to the ring of white ash it always ends at. Nobody has said the word
rations yet.

**Why it fires.** Rest after a battle. Party food at four days or under. No
casualties in the party.

**What it is about.** Food, and the fact that nobody wants to be the one who
counts it out loud.

**Odessa:** All right. Four days. I want everybody to let me finish before anybody
solves it for me.

**Cody:** There it is.

**Odessa:** Four days of what we call provisions, which is a word that got put
in a report by somebody who did not have to eat them.

**Wendell:** I called them provisions.

**Odessa:** You called them provisions and then you ate a can of peaches in
front of me in the second week.

**Wendell:** That was a can of peaches. That was not a provision. That was a
man having an event.

**Cody:** He had an event and it was loud.

**Odessa:** Nobody had to ask you. You were sitting in the middle of eight people
and a fire, and that is a stage.

**Cody:** Okay but can we talk about the part where you have not eaten either.

**Odessa:** I have a good appetite.

**Cody:** Odessa.

**Odessa:** I ate this morning. I ate a full breakfast, I have eaten a full
breakfast every day of this campaign, and there is nothing further to discuss.

**Cody:** What was the breakfast.

**Odessa:** Half a biscuit.

**Cody:** Half.

**Odessa:** Half is a real quantity. Half of a thing you are used to is a
normal serving.

**Wendell:** I counted the biscuit. It came out of a package that holds four.
The rest of it is in the truck under the seats.

**Odessa:** Then there is no problem.

**Wendell:** That is not where I was going with it.

**Cody:** I have got jerky. I have had jerky in this coat since noon and I have
been carrying it around like a man I am supposed to be meeting.

**Odessa:** How long has it been in the coat.

**Cody:** Since noon.

**Odessa:** Give it to me.

**Cody:** It has been on my person since noon, Odessa, it is not going to be
ruined in four hours.

**Odessa:** It is going to be warm and it is going to be terrible and you are
going to eat it and be glad.

*Nobody argues. He eats it. He looks like somebody who has made a mistake he
intends to make again.*

**Cody:** That is the worst thing I have ever eaten and I want everybody here to
know that I did it on purpose.

**Wendell:** I put that in the notebook. Jerky, noon to dusk, eaten after
objection.

**Cody:** Do not put that in the notebook.

**Wendell:** Everything goes in the notebook.

### 2.2 THE RADIO

**Who is here.** Delphine Morrow, Lorna Quinn, Cody Bravo. The truck radio works
about one time in four and Lorna has been trying to make it work for two hours.

**Why it fires.** Any rest. A working or half working radio in the party's
vehicles.

**What it is about.** The argument about music, which is never really about
music.

**Lorna:** That is a six tube set and it has been since before any of us had a
driver.

**Delphine:** It is a five tube.

**Lorna:** It is a six tube with the sixth one stolen, which means somebody in
this party knows enough to have taken it out and not enough to use it.

**Cody:** I did not take a tube.

**Lorna:** Nobody said you. I said somebody in this party and there are eight
of us.

**Delphine:** Turn it. Turn the dial. You are talking to the radio and the radio
has never once cared about your opinion of it.

**Lorna:** I am not talking to the radio, I am trying to get her something.

**Delphine:** For who.

**Lorna:** For whoever wants it. That is the entire campaign. That is my whole
method, and you are making it sound like a trick.

**Delphine:** It is not a trick, it is just the thing you do out loud.

**Cody:** Can I put on something. Not a station, I do not care what station. I
will take anything that is not talking.

**Lorna:** That is not a station, that is a complaint.

**Cody:** It is a request and it is the only request I have made in nine days.

**Lorna:** There is a station out of Bakersfield on the dial that plays about
three songs an hour and spends the rest of it telling you the weather. In the
morning it plays one of them.

**Cody:** I will take the weather.

**Lorna:** Everybody always takes the weather and then in twenty minutes
somebody asks why we did not do something else.

**Delphine:** Let me tell you what the camps play, since neither of you has been
in a camp. The camps play six songs. There are six because somebody in every
camp has a truck with a radio in it and that person has a taste, and it is
always a bad one, and it is always the same bad one.

**Cody:** What are the six.

**Delphine:** I am not telling you. You will tell them.

**Cody:** Who, there are eight of us.

**Delphine:** There is your answer. Somebody always tells them.

*Lorna is not laughing. She turns the dial a little more anyway, and for about
four seconds you get a woman's voice and a piano and then it goes to static, and
none of the three of them says anything about it.*

### 2.3 THE MACHINE THAT WORKED

**Who is here.** Teodora Nakashima, August Ramey. They are both still awake
because there is a pump on the trailer that is making a noise and neither of them
has been able to stop talking about it.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Any minor mechanical fault in the party's vehicles.

**What it is about.** The oldest argument in the party, which nobody has stated
in this form before.

**Teodora:** It has been running two hours and it has not stopped once. I want
you to understand that I do not like it.

**August:** It runs.

**Teodora:** It runs like a thing that is hiding something.

**August:** It is a pump. It pumps. That is the whole feature set.

**Teodora:** A pump that pumps smoothly has a reason. A pump that pumps smoothly
at two in the morning after nine days of mud has a reason and I cannot hear it
and that is going to keep me up.

**August:** You cannot hear it.

**Teodora:** I cannot account for it.

**August:** Those are not the same and I would like to get that on the record
before sunrise.

**Teodora:** You have never accounted for anything in your life that you did not
eventually have to shoot, and I say that as a professional opinion and not as a
dig.

**August:** It has been running since before you got here.

**Teodora:** It has been running since 1968 and it has never once failed, and
that is the part I want to take apart and look at.

**August:** Now you are talking about the other thing.

**Teodora:** I know what I am talking about.

**August:** You do not, you are about to say the sentence about the gun and I
would rather you got through it.

**Teodora:** You have a weapon that works and you cannot tell me why. I have a
pump that works and I cannot tell me why. Those are the same sentence and you
know it is the same sentence, because you do not sleep either.

**August:** I do not sleep. That is a different conversation with a doctor in
it and I have had that conversation and I did not enjoy it.

**Teodora:** Then let us have this one instead.

**August:** All right. Let us have this one. Tell me what the pump is doing.

**Teodora:** It is doing a thing I have never heard, and I have heard four
hundred pumps, and it is going to fail in a place where failing costs somebody
water.

**August:** Then leave it alone and watch it.

**Teodora:** That is what everybody says.

**August:** I did not say it like everybody else. Everybody says it because it
is easier. I am saying it because I would rather find out at a standstill than
at a bridge.

*Teodora does not touch the pump. She stays awake with it anyway, and neither of
them mentions that, and at some point in the night they both stop talking and
just listen to the pump together, which is the closest thing to agreement the
party has seen in six weeks.*

### 2.4 WHAT I DID BEFORE

**Who is here.** Cody Bravo, Delphine Morrow, August Ramey. Fire is high and
somebody found a guitar case in a house and nobody has opened it yet.

**Why it fires.** Rest, morale above a threshold, no casualties recent.

**What it is about.** Funny past jobs, told badly and in the wrong order, which
is how people tell them.

**Cody:** Okay, so, the funniest one, and it is not funny, but it is the
funniest.

**August:** Those two things are not in opposition.

**Cody:** My uncle put me on a crew that hauled survey gear across west Texas
for two years. Forty trucks, a hundred men, and a schedule so tight that being
late got you fired at the roadside.

**Delphine:** At the roadside.

**Cody:** At the roadside. In front of everybody.

**August:** They fired you on the shoulder of a highway.

**Cody:** They fired you at the roadside, Gus, and they made you walk, and then
somebody's cousin offered you a ride in a pickup and it was the best day of my
life.

**Delphine:** What did you do before that.

**Cody:** Welded gates. Rode fence. Ran a water truck for a man named Virgil
who is dead now and who never once said thank you.

**Delphine:** I sold watches.

**Cody:** You what.

**Delphine:** Out of the trunk of a car in a parking lot outside an airport when
I was nineteen. That is my funny one. You had a nice one and I am going to take
yours, because mine is funnier.

**Cody:** How is that funnier.

**Delphine:** Because it worked. That is the joke. That is the whole joke, it is
a nineteen year old girl with a tray of watches and it worked.

**August:** I had a press.

**Cody:** Everybody had a machine.

**August:** Everybody in this party had a machine. That is what we are. I stood
too close to a press in 1961 and I lost most of the hearing in this ear, and I
have told people I was reckless about four or five times, and it is not that,
and I would rather somebody here heard the true one.

**Delphine:** What is the true one.

**August:** I was standing next to a man who needed me there.

*Nobody makes a joke. Cody starts to say something about the guitar case and
Delphine beats him to it by asking Ramey what he did on the other side of the
room, which is a thing she does, and which is how everybody knows she heard it.*

**Delphine:** What did you do on the other side of the room.

**August:** Watched you not open the case.

**Delphine:** There is nothing in the case.

**August:** You have been sitting on it for an hour.

**Delphine:** There is nothing in the case and I am going to keep it that way
for another hour, and if you three want to go to sleep you can go to sleep
without me explaining anything.

### 2.5 FOUR HOURS OF SLEEP

**Who is here.** Cody Bravo, Odessa Pryor, Royce Calloway. Second watch, thin
blankets, everybody else asleep at the ring of ash.

**Why it fires.** Rest after a hard engagement. Casualties present.

**What it is about.** Fear, described as logistics, by three people who are all
lying in different directions.

**Cody:** Do either of you sleep before a day like that one.

**Odessa:** No.

**Cody:** That was not a no, that was a laugh with words in it.

**Odessa:** I sleep when there is nothing to do at four in the morning, and last
night there were eleven stitches and one man who was asking about his leg.

**Royce:** What was his leg.

**Odessa:** A hole. It is clean and he will keep it.

**Cody:** You always say that. You said that about the guy in Newport and he
could not hold a cup of coffee on Tuesday.

**Odessa:** He could hold it on Wednesday.

**Cody:** I did not know that.

**Odessa:** You were not there on Wednesday.

**Royce:** You can go four days without sleep.

**Cody:** You can go four days without sleep if somebody else is watching, and
if nobody is watching then it is not four days, it is about eleven hours.

**Odessa:** That is not in a book anywhere.

**Royce:** No. It is what happens.

**Cody:** All right. Here is my honest one and then I am going to sleep, and I
am putting it in the middle of the night because that is where the true things
live.

**Odessa:** Go ahead.

**Cody:** I am not afraid of getting shot. Everybody assumes the loud one is
brave and I have been in three things I should not have been in and I know the
math on those better than anybody alive. What I am afraid of is being the reason
something went wrong because I did not ask the second question.

**Royce:** The stupid second question.

**Cody:** The stupid second question. I ask the first one and everybody pats me
on the shoulder, and the one that mattered was the one under it, and I do not
ask it because I am already embarrassed.

**Odessa:** Ask it.

**Cody:** It is not going to be the one you want me to ask.

**Odessa:** I do not care what it is, I have been asked worse at four in the
morning and I have sewn the answers.

**Cody:** All right. Ask it tomorrow when the coffee is good and I will ask it
tomorrow.

**Royce:** You will not.

**Cody:** That is unfair, Royce, that is a real thing about me and you said it in
nine words.

**Royce:** I said it in nine words because it is a true thing and you did not
need it dressed up.

### 2.6 SOMEWHERE TO PUT ROOTS

**Who is here.** Teodora Nakashima, Odessa Pryor. Two women who both had a trade
and no room for it.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Party morale under a threshold, or a companion has been
uninjured for a while.

**What it is about.** Home, and the specific insult of not being able to picture
it.

**Teodora:** I cannot do it any more.

**Odessa:** Do what.

**Teodora:** The picture. I used to be able to do the picture. My grandfather's
shop, the counter, where the light came in, the specific shade of the wall behind
the drill press. I can do the shape of it. I cannot do the color.

**Odessa:** When did that go.

**Teodora:** About two years. And before you say it, no, it is not because of
the war. It is because every time I checked it, it was wrong, so now I have two
pictures, and the wrong one is louder.

**Odessa:** I know about the two pictures.

**Teodora:** You said that too quickly.

**Odessa:** Everybody who has been away long enough has two pictures. The
house is real and the version you carry is not, and the version you carry has
better weather in it.

**Teodora:** Why does yours have better weather.

**Odessa:** Because in the version I carry, the light in the ward was better,
and the halls did not echo, and there were more of us than there were.

**Teodora:** That is not a place. That is a wish with a building on it.

**Odessa:** It is what I have.

**Teodora:** All right. Then here is mine. In the version I carry, my father is
in the shop and he has not worked nineteen hours.

**Odessa:** He did not work nineteen hours in the real one either.

**Teodora:** He worked twenty six years and I do not think he ever had a day off
that was not a Sunday, and Sunday was partly work, and he would have told you
that was the same as a day off.

**Odessa:** That is not a place either.

**Teodora:** No. It is a man in a room, and the room is the only part I can
still see.

**Odessa:** Give me one thing about the town you are from that is not the shop.

**Teodora:** There was a woman on the corner who sold tamales out of a cooler
on Saturdays, and she had a radio, and it was never on the same station twice,
and I do not know her name.

**Odessa:** You do not know her name.

**Teodora:** I do not know her name.

**Odessa:** That is a better picture than the shop and you should put it in
first next time.

**Teodora:** It is not the shop.

**Odessa:** No. It is the thing on the corner, and that is where the people
actually were.

### 2.7 THE FIRST THING

**Who is here.** Teodora Nakashima, Cody Bravo, Royce Calloway. Fires late in a
run, when somebody in the party says something like that out loud by accident.

**Why it fires.** Any rest. No trigger condition, because a character having
this thought is the trigger.

**What it is about.** What they will do when the war ends. Nobody in this scene
has a good answer and one of them has no answer at all.

**Cody:** Okay, I have a question and it is a stupid one so I want to get it out
before everybody gets clever.

**Teodora:** Ask it.

**Cody:** When this is over, and I mean all of it, not the part after the next
fight. What is the first thing you do.

**Royce:** That is not a stupid question.

**Cody:** It is a very stupid question. It is the only one I have.

**Teodora:** I have one. Ask it next time and I will answer it, and I will
answer it the way I answer everything, which is badly and about machines.

**Cody:** Fine. Teodora, first thing.

**Teodora:** Build one thing that outlives me.

**Cody:** That is not a first thing, that is a whole life.

**Teodora:** It is a water plant with a bracket in it that I designed, and if it
is still running in forty years then I did one real thing with my hands.

**Cody:** And you will not put a plaque up.

**Teodora:** If somebody puts a plaque up I will take it down. I will do it
politely and in daylight.

**Cody:** Mine is the ocean.

**Royce:** Which one.

**Cody:** Does it matter.

**Royce:** It matters to about four people alive and none of them are in this
party, but if you say the Pacific I will give you a route and if you say the
Atlantic I will give you a different one and both of them are about a day too
long.

**Cody:** Pacific.

**Royce:** There is a man in Barstow who has driven it eleven times and does not
like it. He will take you for the price of gas and he will not talk for the first
hour, and that is the correct arrangement.

**Cody:** That is the nicest thing anybody has offered me in a year.

**Royce:** Now you.

**Cody:** Me?

**Royce:** I asked you a question in the fire last week and you turned it into a
joke. That is the answer. You are going to see the ocean and you are going to
have nothing else, and that is fine, and it will be about four hours of your life
that you cannot plan.

**Cody:** That is the nicest and the meanest thing anybody has said to me and I
am going to think about it for the rest of the night.

**Royce:** Good. Think about it out loud. I am not going to be here at three.

### 2.8 NAMES

**Who is here.** Lorna Quinn, Delphine Morrow. They do not get along and both of
them know exactly why and neither of them will say the other one.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Both companions present. Fires more often at higher
relation with either of them.

**What it is about.** How the two of them use people, told by each other, in
their own words, which are not the same accusation.

**Lorna:** I want to say something about you and I want to say it once.

**Delphine:** Go ahead.

**Lorna:** You do not organize anybody. You move them.

**Delphine:** That is not a word anybody has ever used.

**Lorna:** It is the word I use. You find out what a place is short of and you
get it there before anybody decides it is a shortage. That is movement. I have
spent my life getting people into a room, and you do not even want the room. You
want the truck to leave.

**Delphine:** I want the truck to arrive.

**Lorna:** Same thing, and you know it is the same thing, and you did it in a
county where the people who arrived were not from there and did not stay.

**Delphine:** That is a fair sentence and I would like to know which part of it
you learned from me.

**Lorna:** I did not learn it from you.

**Delphine:** You learned it from watching me buy a bad night at a bar for four
dollars, and you have been carrying it around for two years like it is a
person.

**Lorna:** You perform.

**Delphine:** Say that again and mean it in the same breath.

**Lorna:** You perform. I organize. There is a difference and it is not a small
one. When I walk into a factory floor, two hundred people know what happens to
them this year. When you walk into a warehouse, four people know.

**Delphine:** Four people get paid.

**Lorna:** That is not the same.

**Delphine:** It is the same to the four.

*Lorna opens her mouth, and then does not say it, and it is very clear from about
four feet away that she is not going to. Delphine lets her have it, which she does
not often, and neither of them marks the moment, and they sit with it until the
fire needs another log.*

### 2.9 THE LEDGER

**Who is here.** Wendell Bragg, Delphine Morrow. They have been arguing about a
number for two days and neither of them is going to win.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Party food below six days.

**What it is about.** Food, and money, which turn out to be the same argument.

**Wendell:** Four days, and I have been wrong about it twice, and I would like it
understood that I was wrong in a specific way.

**Delphine:** You have never been wrong about anything and it makes people
unpleasant.

**Wendell:** Tuesday I counted eleven sacks and a half. Wednesday I counted ten.
Nothing left the camp. Nobody took anything. I went back with a different method
and got ten, and the ten is right, and I cannot tell you where the half went,
and that is the part that keeps me up.

**Delphine:** Because you counted by weight the first time and by hand the
second.

**Wendell:** I do not know why it came out different.

**Delphine:** Then you did not count twice, you counted two different things.

**Wendell:** I would like to be angry about that and I am not, because you are
right and it took you nine seconds.

**Delphine:** That is the whole problem with you, you are extremely difficult to
cheat and I make my living off people I can.

**Wendell:** You are going to buy them cheaper.

**Delphine:** Somebody is going to buy them cheaper. If it is not me it is a man
who will sell them wet, and I would like it to be me, and I would like it to be
recorded that I said so out loud in front of a quartermaster.

**Wendell:** It is recorded.

**Delphine:** Write it down.

**Wendell:** It is already written down. That is what a ledger is.

**Delphine:** Then say the rest of it. What happens on day five.

**Wendell:** On day five I tell a town that the grain is not coming.

**Delphine:** There. That is the sentence.

**Wendell:** I am aware it is the sentence.

**Delphine:** Say it again, out loud, to me. You have never said it out loud to
another person and I want to hear what your voice does.

**Wendell:** No.

**Delphine:** All right. Then I will say it for you, and it will be wrong, and
you will correct me, which is how I know what it costs. A town runs out in
November. There was not enough grain.

**Wendell:** There was enough grain. I had the grain. I did not have the cars.

*That is the first time he has said it. Delphine does not push it, which she
would have pushed anything else, and neither of them says anything for a while,
and then Wendell asks her to check the count tomorrow morning and she says yes
without making a joke about it.*

### 2.10 SOMEBODY TO WRITE TO

**Who is here.** Royce Calloway, Wendell Bragg, Lorna Quinn. All three of them
are the kind of man who counts other people, and they have worked each other out
within about four minutes.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Fires more often after a battle with heavy losses.

**What it is about.** Home, and the people who are not there.

**Lorna:** Royce. I am going to say your name a certain way and you are going to
hate it.

**Lorna:** Royce Calloway.

**Royce:** That was worse than I expected.

**Lorna:** I have been in a fight with you in my head since Tuesday over a bridge
and you lost. Say something in your own words instead of my name.

**Royce:** I do not have a name.

**Lorna:** Everybody has a name. You have a girl in Ohio.

*Royce does not say anything for long enough that it becomes an answer.*

**Wendell:** How old.

**Royce:** You do not ask me that.

**Wendell:** I am not asking, I am guessing out loud, because I have been
counting all night and it is the only kind of talking I know how to do. I would
say sixteen.

**Royce:** She is twenty two.

**Wendell:** Older than you said, and you said nothing. You said somewhere. You
have said somewhere four times in nine days and never once a place, and I have
been keeping track, and I would like to stop keeping track.

**Royce:** All right. Ohio. She is in Ohio.

**Lorna:** What does she write about.

**Royce:** School. She is in a program. She writes about the program and about
the weather and about a boy in her class who is not smart and is very funny.

**Royce:** I have all of them.

**Wendell:** In what.

**Royce:** The glovebox.

**Royce:** Every one. Eleven years of them in a truck that has a bad heater and
a bench seat I have been meaning to redo since Casper.

**Lorna:** You are afraid she will ask you to come.

**Royce:** Yes.

**Lorna:** And she will.

**Royce:** Yes.

**Lorna:** Then you have already lost that one and you are just waiting to find
out the date.

**Royce:** I know the date. I have known the date for about four years. That is
not the problem.

**Wendell:** Then say what the problem is, since we are all here and one of us
has already said the hard one out loud.

**Royce:** I keep thinking about the drive. I get in and I do not know what I
am going to be, and I am about forty minutes out and I have already decided I am
going to stay four months.

**Lorna:** Royce, that is not a problem. That is a habit.

**Royce:** I know what it is.

### 2.11 SINGING

**Who is here.** August Ramey, Wendell Bragg, Cody Bravo. Somebody has been
whistling for four days and it has finally become an event.

**Why it fires.** Rest. Party morale above a threshold.

**What it is about.** The second music argument, which is worse, because this
time nobody is defending anything.

**Cody:** I would like to say something and I want to say it all at once so
nobody can stop me.

**August:** Say it.

**Cody:** You whistle.

**August:** I do not.

**Cody:** You whistle. Badly. In key. For four days.

**August:** I do not whistle, I breathe out, and I am not going to apologize
for a thing I do not know I am doing.

**Cody:** It is the same song every time.

**August:** There is a correct version of a song. There is one correct version.
If I have it wrong you would hear it.

**Wendell:** It is the same song every time and you are half a beat behind.

**August:** The half beat is the interesting part.

**Wendell:** The half beat is a mistake. You are a man who makes machines that
work every time and you cannot hold a tune through eight bars.

**August:** I can hold a number to six figures. The number does not wander.

**Cody:** Everybody is talking about me and I would like somebody to talk about
me.

**Wendell:** You are not in this.

**Cody:** I brought the song.

**Wendell:** You did not bring anything. You have been here four days.

**August:** He brought it four days ago.

**Wendell:** Then that is the other problem. He did not bring it once, he brought
it for four days, and now we all know it, and that is the worst way for a thing
to become a habit.

**Cody:** I like it.

**August:** Good. That is the only part of this that was ever about music.

**Cody:** No, I mean it. I like it when it is there. I have had four days of
marching and two hundred people telling me what to do and somebody comes by
about every hour and does the same eight bars wrong, and it is the only thing
that has happened to me that was not scheduled.

*Nobody has an answer to that, and Wendell Bragg, who has an answer to
everything and gets up at five, sits back down.*

**Wendell:** All right.

**Cody:** All right?

**Wendell:** I am not going to fix it. I am going to hear it four more days. I
would like it noted that I am doing a thing I do not agree with.

**Cody:** Who is keeping track?

**Wendell:** Somebody is. There is always somebody.

### 2.12 THE HOUR BETWEEN

**Who is here.** Odessa Pryor, Delphine Morrow. Third watch. One of them has said
a name out loud and the other one has heard it and neither of them is going to
pretend about it.

**Why it fires.** Rest, deep in a campaign, high relation with either companion.
This scene has a relation gate and it is the highest gate in the file.

**What it is about.** Fear, and one loyalty that is not a reveal yet and must not
be one at this stage of a campaign.

**Odessa:** You are on watch.

**Delphine:** You are on watch.

**Odessa:** You were on watch at four and again at eight.

**Delphine:** I like the third shift. Everybody is asleep by then and I can walk
to the truck without anybody asking me a question about the manifest.

**Odessa:** That is a reason.

**Delphine:** That is a reason.

**Odessa:** All right. Here is mine. I am going to say a thing and then I am
going to stop talking and you are going to let me, because you are not the kind
of person who will follow a person somewhere.

**Delphine:** Go.

**Odessa:** When this one came in, the men on the second row. They were gone by
the time we got them and everybody in the room had seen them come in that
morning. And what I did, what I actually did, was count the men on the first row
and see if I could add the ones on the second row to the number I had, and if
the math worked, then maybe they had not been shot yet, and I could stop thinking
about them and start working.

**Delphine:** Did the math work.

**Odessa:** No. That is the part I have never said. It did not work.

**Delphine:** What did you do then.

**Odessa:** I did it again the next night. That is the answer to that question.

**Delphine:** Odessa.

**Odessa:** I am not finished. I do it every night and I have done it for a year
and a half and it has never once worked, and if I stop doing it then I have to
be a person who knows what happened, so I do it.

**Delphine:** That is not crazy.

**Odessa:** I know it is not crazy. That is the trouble with it. It would be
easier if it were crazy.

**Delphine:** It is a system. You are running a system and it is running on
nothing and it works every time so far.

**Odessa:** I want you to say one thing for me and then I am going to stop.

**Delphine:** I am not going to guess what it is.

**Odessa:** I do not need you to guess. I need to know that if there is ever a
morning where it works, and one where the number is exactly right, you will be
in the second row of the truck with the other people's people.

**Delphine:** Ask me that in a year.

**Odessa:** I asked you now.

**Delphine:** I said ask me in a year. That is a no with a date on it, and it is
the only way I know to say it, and you know that, and you asked anyway, which
means you already knew.

*Odessa looks at her for a while. Then she says goodnight, which is the end of
it, and Delphine walks out to the truck and stands in the dark with her hand on
the door, which is where the reader is meant to end.*

## 3. NIGHT WATCH

One character alone, thinking out loud, with nobody to hear it. These fire on
watch rotation and are not addressed to the player. They are for the companion
who has been in the party for thirty hours and is still a person at hour thirty.
Each is between one hundred and two hundred words and each is written in that
character's published voice.

Five of the six fire on watch rotation alone. Lorna Quinn at 3.6 also needs
weeks elapsed and a relation floor, because it is the only monologue in the file
that says something the player has not been told yet.

### 3.1 TEODORA NAKASHIMA

*A water plant in the dark. She is not thinking about anything.*

I keep rebuilding it in my head. The line, the rollers, the belt, where the
grease goes and where it does not go. Nine months since I last did this and I am
doing it now, at three in the morning, with no tools, which means the version in
my head is the good one, which means it is not true.

Forty thousand people ate through that line. Three years. The check did not clear
and I went anyway. When I think about that, the picture is not the line. It is a
bearing. The one that sang on the outfeed in February of the second year, and I
never replaced it.

I have been offered work in three towns this year. Every one had a machine that
was fine, and a machine that is fine is a machine where I have nothing to do. I
would rather have one that is not fine. Then I am useful, and I do not have to
think about the other thing.

That is not a good reason to be anywhere. I have known it for a year and I have
not found the better one.

### 3.2 ROYCE CALLOWAY

*Eleven hundred yards of nothing in every direction. He has done this before.*

The dark out here is not dark. That is what people get wrong about the mountains.
It is not black, it is blue. Stand still long enough and your eyes go, and then
you can see the ridgeline and the rocks and your own hands, and stand still long
enough after that and you can see a man doing this a mile away.

So I do not stand still. I walk. Four hundred yards out, come back, sit, go
again. That is the job. Eleven years of it was the same job in a truck with a bad
heater.

Hour three is for checking my own reasons. You sit with a thing long enough and
you go looking for the bad one, and the bad one is usually the one you have been
telling people first.

Mine is that I do not want to be the man who is there. Not the man who stays.
The man who is there. In a camp like this somebody is that, and it is the best
thing I do, and it is why I have not gone anywhere.

That is the bad reason. I am sleeping on it anyway.

### 3.3 ODESSA PRYOR

*The generator is off. It is the first quiet hour in eleven days.*

I do not have to be anywhere for an hour and a half. That is what I am going to
spend it on. Not sleep. I decided not to sleep, every night for two years, and
it has never once worked, and here I am again.

Here is what I do. I go down the row. Not the men. The ones I have already let go.
And I say the thing I did for them, out loud, one at a time, in order. The
dressing. The amount. The time. Sometimes the time is the whole answer, and
sometimes it is the one where I could have gone faster and did not know it.

Then I stop. Past that one is the arithmetic, and I have watched a person do that
about their own work at three in the morning and it takes nobody anywhere. I have
been that person.

There is a name from a Tuesday. I am not saying it here. I do that when nobody
is to hear, and that is worse, not better.

The shoulder is fine. I am not writing that in the book. I am saying it here
where nobody counts.

### 3.4 CODY BRAVO

*He has been doing this since he was a boy and it has never once worked.*

Okay. So.

I am not going down there again tonight, and not tomorrow either. I have been
telling myself that for two hours. It is going great.

I do not want to think about it. Everybody in this party has a word for that and
they are all right, and I have used three of them on other people with my whole
chest.

The thing is not even the fire. The thing is that I do not know what I would
have done differently. Six weeks on it and every time I get close the answer is
that I would have been faster. Bad answer. Being faster is not a thing I get to
decide about myself in a field. That is wanting to be somebody else for the
length of a fire.

Here is what I actually did. I got four people out of a truck I did not think
would burn, because I was closest to the door. Forty feet over and it would have
been somebody else, and they would have got out too, because they were good at
their jobs and so was I.

Smaller story than I wanted. But it is the true one.

### 3.5 AUGUST RAMEY

*He has the launcher stripped to four parts on a blanket and he is doing this
anyway.*

This is the third and it is not right either. I want to be clear about the word I
am using, because I use the wrong one on purpose in front of people.

I want it right. Not reliable. Right. I have known that since I was nineteen and
it is the only thing I am good at. All three are machines that work. Fires, hits
the paper, resets, fires.

The gap is a twentieth of a second. You would never see it. I measured it four
times with a stopwatch like a man in a story.

A thing that works and you cannot explain is worse than a thing that stops,
because a thing that stops is telling you the truth. And a man telling people his
weapon works is telling the truth the way a thermometer tells the temperature.
True, and not enough.

I am fifty something, two and a half ears, one of them for engines. Every time I
think about the stopwatch I get a little stupid. There is no shoulder in this,
just a spring and a sear.

I will get it. Or I will not, and I will have written down why.

### 3.6 LORNA QUINN

*She is sitting on the tailgate with a list she is not going to read.*

Two hundred names. I could still do it. Not being proud, being honest. I could
stand in front of those two hundred people tonight and get every one of them onto
that factory floor, and eleven of them can shoot.

That is the whole asset. No factory, no hall, no grievance procedure, no money.
What I have is a woman in a room who can make a hundred frightened people believe
a thing is possible, and I have never once been wrong about whether a room can be
moved.

That is also the problem. Not because it is a lie. Because I can do it to
anybody, including people who should not be moved. That is how you get a sentence
said in a hall that somebody repeats later in a bad week.

I keep a list. Not the two hundred. The other one. People who said a thing to me
in a bad year. I have never shown it to anybody, and if this camp burned down
tomorrow it is the only page I would go back for.

There is one name on it that I have taken out and written back four times.

## 4. QUESTIONS

Four scenes. A companion asks the player something, the player answers, and the
companion has a different reply for each answer. These are the only places in
this file where the player speaks.

No option here is the good one. Each one costs something, and the game does not
tell the player which cost it was. Options are not gated on anything except the
companion being present and alive, with the exception noted in the fourth scene.

### 4.1 WENDELL BRAGG, ON THE NUMBER

**Fires.** Rest, party food at four days or under. Wendell is present. This is
the first time he has asked.

*He has the notebook closed on his knee. He has had it closed for about a minute,
which for him is a speech.*

**Wendell:** Captain. I am going to ask you something and I want you to answer
fast, because I have already asked it in my own head four times and I got four
different answers and all four of them were me.

**Player:** Ask it.

**Wendell:** On day five I have to tell a town that the grain is not coming. It
is not going to be enough. I have been counting it three ways and it is not
going to be enough and I would like somebody else to have seen the number before
I say it out loud in a room.

**Option A. Tell him now.** Tell him to say it tonight, on the road, before he
gets there. He will not be alone in it.

**Wendell:** No. No, I would not. I would do it anyway, I would just do it
twice, once to you and once to them, and I would have gotten used to it, and then
I would get to that room and it would be the first time I had said it to a
person with a face on it. That is worse. I would rather say it once, to somebody
who is owed a delivery, and hear myself do it.

**Option B. Tell him to wait until you are in the room.**

**Wendell:** You would want to be there. That is a kind thing and it is going to
be worse than the kind thing, and I am saying so now so you do not feel good
about it later. If you are standing in that room, then it is a thing that
happened with a witness, and if you are not in the room then it is a thing that
happened to a town. I would rather it was the second one. Somebody is going to
have to be able to point at me afterward and say that man said it to me and I
heard him. It cannot have an audience.

**Option C. Tell him to give the grain anyway.**

**Wendell:** Then I move it, and somebody upstream does not eat in February, and
I find that out in a letter or I do not find it out at all, which is the same
thing. And then I get to be a man who did not say it. Do you know how many men in
this war are running on that? Everybody who is asleep in a bed somewhere is
running on somebody downstream not hearing about it.

**Wendell:** Which is also why I asked. You are the only person in this party I
can say that to without it becoming an order, and if you turn out to be the kind
of man who wants it clean, we are going to find that out now and not in April.

**Effect.** All three write the same morale result and different opinions of the
player. Option C costs food elsewhere on the map and writes a cause log row.

### 4.2 ODESSA PRYOR, ON THE SHOULDER

**Fires.** Odessa present, at least one companion wounded in the last battle,
rest. It fires once and then she does not ask again.

*She is cleaning instruments that are already clean. She does this when she wants
to be holding something.*

**Odessa:** You are going to ask me how it is going. Everybody asks me how it is
going, and the question is always about the shoulder, and I am going to give you
the true answer and it is going to be the one about the shoulder, and then I am
going to ask you something back and I want you to answer it without thinking,
because the ones you think about first are the ones you have already decided on.

**Player:** Ask.

**Odessa:** Who is the person you would call.

**Option A. Name somebody who would come.**

**Odessa:** Good. Then you are in a bad position and you should think about it
tonight rather than in the road. I am not being kind, I am being practical. A man
who has one person to call has a hole in him and he cannot fill it at three in
the morning with a leg like that. I would find you a second one. Ask around. Ask
Lorna, she knows four hundred names and about forty of them owe somebody.

**Option B. Say there is nobody.**

**Odessa:** All right. That is allowed and I am not going to make it a thing. I
will say one thing and then I will not say it again. Somebody in this party is
going to be the person you call, and it is going to be an accident, and it is
going to be in the middle of something with no time in it. Decide now whether you
are going to be embarrassed about it or grateful.

**Option C. Ask her who she would call.**

*She stops. She puts the instrument down, which she does not do.*

**Odessa:** No.

**Player:** I asked.

**Odessa:** You asked. And the answer is that there is one person in this party I
would call and she is not a surgeon and she does not know that I have asked you
the question, and if you ever repeat any part of this to her I will sew you up
wrong on purpose. That is a joke. Half of that is a joke.

**Effect.** Option C is worth more relation with Odessa than the other two by a
wide margin, and it opens nothing. That is the reward. The scene does not unlock
a fact about her and must never be used to.

### 4.3 CODY BRAVO, ON THE SECOND QUESTION

**Fires.** Rest, no casualties recent, party morale above a threshold. Cody is
present. This is the cheerful one and it is the easiest to get wrong.

*He is sitting cross legged and has clearly been waiting for somebody to sit
down.*

**Cody:** Okay, I have been working on a question all day and I have got it down
to two sentences, and I want you to know that I worked on it, because I am not
usually a two sentence guy.

**Player:** Go.

**Cody:** When I ask the second question and nobody answers it and we go anyway.
What do I do. Not tomorrow. Tomorrow I am fine, I am always fine tomorrow. The
night we are on the ground and it is done and everybody is looking at the
ground.

**Option A. Tell him to ask it anyway, every time.**

**Cody:** Okay. Every time. That is the answer I wanted and it is the reason I
asked you instead of Gus, because Gus would have said every time and then talked
me out of it in the morning with something about ammunition.

**Cody:** I am going to be really annoying about this. You understand that. I am
not going to ask it in a mean way, I am going to ask it in the dumb loud way
where everybody rolls their eyes, and if I do it for four months it stops being
annoying and starts being how you find things out. That is the actual plan. There
is no plan past next week, so that is the whole plan, and it is yours now too.

**Option B. Tell him to pick the one he thinks matters and ask that one.**

**Cody:** That is worse than you think it is, because then it is not a habit, it
is a judgment, and a judgment is a thing I would be bad at, because I would pick
the stupid one and then defend it.

**Cody:** But I can do it. I want you to know I heard you and I can do it and I
am not going to. I am going to ask the dumb one. I am telling you now so you
cannot say I hid it.

**Option C. Tell him to ask it of Ramey.**

**Cody:** Oh. Oh, that is mean. That is mean and it is correct and it is the only
one of the three that got a real answer out of me.

**Cody:** Because he does the numbers and I do the door. He is going to hear
something I am not hearing, because he counts things and I am the guy who walks
in without counting. Do that. Put him on it. And do not tell him I sent you,
because if he thinks it was your idea he will do it better and if he thinks it
was mine he will do it as a favor and both of those are worse than just doing it.

**Effect.** Option A raises Cody's morale the most and Option C raises his opinion
of the player the most. All three are good and the game does not rank them.

### 4.4 ROYCE CALLOWAY, ON THE FALL

**Fires.** Rest, deep in a campaign, at least eight weeks in, no casualties in
the last two engagements. It fires once. After it has fired it never fires again
in that run, whatever the player does.

*He is the last one up either way. He does not make a speech about it and he does
not sit closer than he has to.*

**Royce:** I am going to ask you something and then I am going to go to bed,
which is how you know I mean it.

**Player:** Go.

**Royce:** Are you going to be here in the fall.

*That is all. He does not explain it and he does not follow it up, and there is
about four seconds where the only sound is the fire, and he is not going to fill
them.*

**Option A. Tell him you do not know.**

**Royce:** That is the correct answer and I would have said it myself, and I am
glad you did not lie to me about it, because you would have known I was going to
find out.

**Royce:** I will say the rest so you have it and then I will not bring it up.
I have been at this four months now, which is the longest I have gone anywhere
since Ohio, and I have started doing a thing where I learn the names of the men
in the supply section. Not because I need them. There is nothing down there that
I would be doing.

**Option B. Tell him yes.**

**Royce:** All right.

**Royce:** Do not say that to make a man feel better at midnight. I have watched
somebody do that to somebody else on a campaign and it does not survive a road.

**Royce:** If you are going to do it then do it out loud where people can hear
you say it, and not to me at a fire, because I will hold you to the one you said
in front of people. That is the only version I can use.

**Option C. Tell him you would rather he stayed anyway.**

**Royce:** That is a different question than the one I asked.

**Royce:** I asked whether you would be here. You have answered whether I should
be here. Those are two questions and they do not have the same answer, and I would
appreciate it if you would let me ask mine.

**Royce:** I am going to tell you what mine is, once, and then that is the end of
it. It is that I have a daughter in Ohio and every letter she has ever sent me is
in the glovebox of a truck, and I know exactly what I am going to do in the fall,
and I have known it since about the third year, and it has not once been a
question of whether a captain wanted me around.

**Effect.** All three options lower his morale slightly and write a cause log row,
because they all cost him something. Option B is the one that shows up in the
four months later system. This is the only scene in the campaign where the player
hears about the glovebox from him as a reply to something they said, rather than
overhearing it in conversation 2.10.

## 5. NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION

1. **Counting an exchange.** One exchange is one back-and-forth: a line and the
   line that answers it. Every conversation in section 2 runs between eight and
   fourteen exchanges, which is two to four lines per speaker. If a conversation
   needs a fifteenth exchange it has stopped being a campfire and has become a
   negotiation, and it belongs in quests.md.

2. **Nobody is upgraded.** A campfire writes companion morale and one companion's
   opinion of the player. It does not write skill, wages, health, or loyalty. The
   only file-wide exception is the night watch, which is read-only and writes
   nothing at all, and which exists because a companion with forty hours in a
   campaign stops being a person somewhere around hour twenty.

3. **Presence, not permission.** Conversations fire on who is in the party and
   alive. They do not check whether the player liked the companion, and there is
   no dialogue tree and no affinity gate on section 2, because a fire is not a
   reward. The two gated scenes are 4.4 and conversation 2.12, both of which are
   late campaign and both of which gate on weeks elapsed as well as relation,
   because a loyalty is not earned in week two.

4. **Delphine and Odessa.** Conversation 2.12 must not confirm the loyalty. It is
   gated at the highest relation in the file and it ends on Delphine walking out
   to the truck. The reveal is a late campaign beat in a different file and this
   scene exists to make it land when it arrives. If a player reads 2.12 as
   confirmation, the reveal has already happened too early and the scene is too
   generous.

5. **Question scenes write nothing but opinions.** Options 4.1 through 4.4 do not
   unlock quests, do not change companion goals, and do not pay out. The only
   downstream effects are a morale value, an opinion value, and in 4.1 Option C a
   cause log row for grain that moved downstream instead. The scene is worth
   playing because of how the player feels at the fire, and the game should not
   have a reason to make the player pick carefully.

6. **Gus and the fifth person.** August Ramey's hearing penalty is real data in
   CHARACTER.md section 10 and it is not waived at a fire. Conversation 2.11
   uses the wrong way round and he asks for it to be repeated as a statement
   rather than as a question, which is the established behavior and is the whole
   joke of the scene.

7. **Lorna's son is not in this file.** Per the restraint note at the end of her
   entry in companions.md, the son is a late campaign reveal and there is no
   version of this content where the player gets a decision prompt about him.
   Campfire is exactly the kind of place a reveal would leak out of, so he is
   absent from all twelve conversations, from her night watch, and from all four
   question scenes. He exists nowhere in this file on purpose, and that is a
   decision rather than an oversight.

8. **Voice check.** Every line above was written against the published banter and
   personality blocks in content/characters/companions.md. Wendell counts, Teodora
   explains machines and is funnier about them than about people, Royce says nine
   words that matter, Odessa says the symptom before the feeling, Delphine is
   fast and does not apologize for money, Cody is warm and has no plan, Lorna uses
   names as a weapon, and August talks about the weapon because it is the only
   time he is relaxed.

9. **Decisions taken without asking.** Three things were unresolved while writing
   this and were settled against the established lore. First, the party travels
   between two and three companions for campfires because that is what the
   duplicated party roles imply; a party with the full roster aboard uses the same
   scenes with fewer cast members and no unlocked variation, and that is a content
   decision for whoever owns the roster screen. Second, August's deafness is
   written as published, including the overlap, so nothing here requires the
   hearing penalty to be modeled before the file can ship. Third, the night watch
   is left ungated on relation for everyone except Lorna, whose monologue is the
   closest the file comes to a private reveal and is held to a weeks elapsed and
   relation gate for that reason.
