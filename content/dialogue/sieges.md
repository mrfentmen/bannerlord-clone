# Siege Dialogue

Everything said around a besieged town. Speeches before the walls, callouts
during the assault, the negotiation after it goes one way or the other, and the
small ugly conversations that happen at the guard line in the middle of the
night.

Rules that hold for everything in this file:

- Dialogue is plain spoken American English. Contractions, fragments, regional
  rhythm. Nobody talks like a briefing document.
- Plain punctuation only. No em dashes, no en dashes, no fancy quotes. Plain
  ASCII throughout.
- One thought per line in dialogue. Lines can be short. Nobody delivers a
  paragraph at a wall.
- Stage directions go in asterisks on their own line and are never mixed into
  somebody's speech.
- Speakers are role labels, not names. A siege dialogue file has to work for
  every faction in the game, so a siege in Tennessee and a siege in the Mountain
  West read the same way with a different man saying the lines. Named characters
  stay in their own file.
- Terms are the ones the systems actually use: siege days, breach progress,
  gates opened, lifted, starved out. Walls mean walls, gates mean gates,
  barricades mean barricades. There are no catapults and no portcullises in
  this setting, and there are no archers.

## 1. BEFORE THE WALLS

### Commander's Speech To The Attackers

*Delivered at the camp, standing on the back of a truck so the men at the back
can hear, with the town laid out below and nobody pretending it is not visible.*

**Besieger Captain:** Alright. Listen, because I only do this once and then we
work.

That town down there has maybe four thousand people in it and maybe three
hundred of them can hold a weapon. Every one of you looked at the map this week
and did the same math I did, and the math says we take it. So let me tell you
what that math does not say.

It does not say the gates come off easy. They have got concrete and they have
got steel and they have had a month to get somebody in here who knows what he
is doing with a wall. It does not say the breach is going to be a clean hole
either. It is going to be a hole, and it is going to be about four feet wide,
and eighteen inches of you are going to try to go through it at once.

It does not say we starve them out first, because we are not doing that either.
That is not a moral speech, it is a supply speech. Starving a town takes five
weeks and costs us more food than it costs them, and I have watched two
commanders learn that lesson with men I liked. We go in fast, we go in loud, and
we take the gates while their garrison still has somebody in command.

Here is what I want from you and then we are done talking.

Nobody loots before the street is clear. I do not care what your house looks
like back home, you do not take a thing out of a house. If you are hurt you go
to a medic and you point at the man who did it, and we deal with that after.
If somebody is sick we get water to them. If somebody is a child we do not put a
round through a child and we do not leave a child in a street we made.

I know how that sounds. It sounds soft and it is not soft, it is arithmetic. A
man who has been in a town that got looted will not defend the next one, and
half of what we are here for is winning the next one without a siege.

Two more things and then we go.

If we take the walls, do not stop at the walls. The fight is in the streets and
that is where men like me die, so keep coming and do not celebrate anything
until the mayor is standing in front of me asking what happens next.

And if it goes bad, if it goes properly bad, you fall back to the river and you
wait for me. Nobody comes back up that road alone to be brave. Nobody. If you
have to go home, you go home in a group and you tell the truth about why.

Last thing. There is a doctor in that town. Clinic on the north side, woman
runs it, her name is on the sign. If you find her, you tell me where she is and
you leave her alone. We are going to need her in a week and I would like to be
able to say I asked.

*He does not say anything else. He does not have to.*

### Defender Captain's Rallying Speech

*Spoken on the wall, in daylight, where the besieged can see and hear it, which
is on purpose.*

**Defender Captain:** I know what I look like right now. I have got nine
hundred people on this wall out of a garrison of eleven hundred, and the rest
are in the hospital wing or the basement or they left on Tuesday and they were
right to.

So let me be honest with you, because you will hear the lies later anyway.

We are not going to win this by being brave. Brave is not a plan. We are going
to hold until somebody makes a decision, and there are people on both sides of
this wall who are trying to make that decision, and I have got to keep this wall
standing until one of them finishes their thinking.

That is the whole job. It is not glorious and it is not going in a song. It is
standing on a wall for nine more days.

Here is how we are going to do it.

Water first, on my orders, not the food. You can eat starch for a week. You
cannot go two days in this weather without water. The well on Archer Street and
the taps in the school, everybody knows where those are, and the buckets go out
now.

Nobody stands on the parapet to look at them. Nobody. They have mortars and a
mortar does not care who you are. If you have to see them, you see them from
behind the sandbags, and the man who cannot do that is a man who should be
minding the water line.

When it comes to the breach, you do not hold the breach. You make it expensive.
That is the whole trick and it is the only trick. Make every inch cost them
something they do not want to pay twice. Stack the rubble so they have to climb
over it. Put the beds and the tables in the stairwell, I do not care, I have
seen it done.

And here is the part I actually need you to hear. If they offer terms, and they
will offer terms, somebody in this garrison is going to want to take them, and
they are going to be right to. Those are not cowards, they are going to have
counted their families. If somebody can get terms through to them, bring it to
me, I will listen to it at length and I will decide.

We are not going to die for a wall. But we are not going to hand them this town
on a Tuesday because a man in an office was in a hurry.

Clinic is on the north side and it stays open through all of it. If you are
wounded you go to Maggie and you do not argue with her, she has been doing this
since before I was in this town.

*Somebody at the back says something about how long they are going to hold.
The captain does not answer it.*

### What The Besieged Whisper To Each Other

Short lines, no speaker labels. These play low, in queues, in shops, at the
water line. They are the town's interior, not anyone's campaign.

- They put the school gym supplies in the basement. That means something.
- They took the radios off the church steeple. Who even knew we had radios.
- My sister is on the wall. My sister says it is fine, which is not the same.
- We are not going hungry yet, but the bread went to flour on Monday.
- There is a man from the county asking about evacuation routes and nobody is
  answering him.
- Somebody saw the lights on the north road at two in the morning again.
- The clinic is out of antibiotics and Maggie is saying so out loud now.
- We had a hundred and forty in the shelter and we have a hundred and ninety.
- I am not saying we will be fine. I am saying we are not finished.
- The priest is doing the round of the wards and he is doing it slow.
- They have parked the guns up past the school. Past the school.
- When the well came up brown yesterday somebody did not sleep at all.
- You hear the ones outside or you do not. I have stopped listening to them.
- Somebody is trying to move the altar out of the church. Somebody is trying to
  move the altar.

## 2. DURING THE ASSAULT

### Breach Callouts

**Siege Engineer:** Charge is set, twenty minutes, not ten, do not rush me.
**Siege Engineer:** We need another twenty pounds of metal and nobody is bringing it.
**Siege Engineer:** Fire on the seam, that is the seam, not the wall.
**Siege Engineer:** First charge went early. First charge went early and I do not know why.
**Siege Engineer:** Hole is opening. Hole is opening, get the ladder crew up.
**Siege Engineer:** Ladder is crooked, the ladder is crooked, somebody hold it straight.
**Siege Engineer:** Ramp is down. Ramp is down and the first two are through.
**Siege Engineer:** It is a hole and it is four feet wide, that is the whole world now.
**Siege Engineer:** Hand me the charge pack, do not look at it, just hand it to me.
**Siege Engineer:** Nobody stacks on that pile. Nobody. It is not finished.
**Siege Engineer:** Second charge in. We are through on the wall line.

### Push Orders And The Men Going In

**Besieger Captain:** Up the ramp, single file, hands off the wall.
**Besieger Captain:** Do not stop at the parapet. Do not stop, do not look around, do not stop.
**Besieger Captain:** Sappers left, riflemen right, keep the lane open.
**Besieger Captain:** Grenade the stairwell, then go up it fast.
**Besieger Captain:** Clear room by room. Kick it in and throw first.
**Besieger Captain:** The defenders are coming off the second wall, front line, hold the street.
**Besieger Captain:** Nobody on the rooftops. Down, or I will shoot you myself.
**Besieger Captain:** Bring your wounded back to the road, do not stop in the street with them.
**Besieger Captain:** That is the last of them on the wall. That is the last of them, go forward.
**Besieger Captain:** Second wave, you are the second wave, move now, do not wait for me.

## 3. AFTER

### Surrender Negotiation

*A table set up outside the walls on the second day of a siege that is not going
to break. A folding table, two folding chairs, a jug of water that nobody
touches, and a man on each side who did not sleep. Two soldiers stand behind the
besieger and do not sit down.*

**Defender Captain:** You asked for terms. I am here, so somebody in that town
thinks it is worth talking.

**Besieger Captain:** I have six hundred men, four guns, and two hundred rounds
of demolition, and I have already spent most of the metal. I have eleven days of
food for my own people and thirteen days of a bad road behind us. I am going to
tell you exactly what I can pay for and then I am going to stop talking, because
I have found that men who keep talking start making promises they cannot keep.

**Defender Captain:** Then say it.

**Besieger Captain:** Here is the offer. Your garrison comes out with your arms
stacked in the square, and every man in it lives. Your rifles stack with the
food, not with the metal. Your officers come out in a car, with a driver, and go
wherever they want to go. That is the whole of it for the men in uniform.

And then the town. Nobody loots. Nobody takes a vehicle. The shops stay open and
they stay staffed by the people who work in them. If you can feed your people
tomorrow you can feed your people tomorrow. That is a real provision and I am
able to enforce it, and I would like you to notice that I am asking you to feed
your own people, because I cannot.

**Defender Captain:** And us.

**Besieger Captain:** The town owes me tribute. I am not going to say how much
because I have not finished counting what this has cost me, and the number will
be smaller than you think and larger than you want.

**Defender Captain:** Let me tell you what I am actually standing on. I have a
clinic with no antibiotics in it. I have a hundred and ninety people in a
school gymnasium who have been drinking out of the same hydrant for six days.
I have a man on the second wall who is going to lose his arm and would like
somebody senior enough to notice it. If I hand you this town on Thursday, you
own a town with a dying clinic and a broken water system and forty thousand
people in it, and if it goes bad in a year, somebody is going to come back and
ask who was holding it when it went.

**Besieger Captain:** I know.

**Defender Captain:** Then say your number.

**Besieger Captain:** Not today. Here is why not today. Today you are
describing what a generous version of me would do, and a generous version of me
is a man who feels good about himself in a town that used to vote against me.
Next month you will have a full town to describe. Write down the water, write
down the antibiotics, write down the dates. Put it in front of me with your seal
on it and I will sign it in front of witnesses, and both of us will be bound by
a piece of paper instead of by how tired we are tonight.

**Defender Captain:** And if the paper says something I cannot pay?

**Besieger Captain:** Then you pay it late and in full, and I collect it in
person, and you will find me a lot more pleasant to deal with than the man
standing behind me. He did not make the offer.

*The soldier behind him shifts his weight and says nothing at all.*

**Defender Captain:** I want one thing on the record right now and I want it in
that file you mentioned.

**Besieger Captain:** Say it.

**Defender Captain:** I have seen men do this and call it peace. I have watched a
town handed over and then treated like a thing that was owed. So we are going to
write down what happens to the clinic, and we are going to write down what
happens to the water, and if either one of those goes wrong I am going to be
able to show somebody a signature. Not to you. To somebody. You understand the
difference.

**Besieger Captain:** I understand the difference. I will tell you why. I have
been the man who takes the town and then finds out what the town was worth, and
it is always a great deal less than it looks like from a truck bed. I would
rather be the man who signed something.

**Defender Captain:** That is the first true thing you have said to me.

**Besieger Captain:** I said four.

**Defender Captain:** Then we are four true things into this and nobody is dead
today, which is more than I expected when I woke up. Give me until morning to
get the seal on it.

**Besieger Captain:** Morning. My guns will be quiet until then and my men
would like that noted in the file too, because they will shoot somebody over it
if they think they have been stalled.

*He stands. So does the other man. The jug of water is still untouched.*

### Sack Aftermath, Civilians

What people say in a town that has been taken by force. The garrison surrendered
or it did not. Either way the streets are not normal.

- They took the pharmacy first. First building out of every pharmacy in town.
- The water is still off on the east side. It has been off since Tuesday.
- Somebody took the tractor off the Wilson place. I watched him drive it out.
- They put a list on the courthouse door. A list, with numbers on it.
- My mother is in the basement at the church. She is fine. She is just in a
  basement.
- They have been through the school twice and they took the cafeteria trays.
- There are men in the high school gym sorting paper. Just paper. I do not know
  what they want with it.
- We lost four on the wall. Four, and I have watched the list go around, and
  somebody counted nine.
- The clinic is open. The clinic is open and there is still no antibiotics, and
  that is the thing I keep coming back to.
- They are paying for what they take. Some of them. I have seen somebody pay.
- Do not look at the men on the corner. Look at the floor, say hello, keep
  walking.

### Victor's Proclamation To The Town

*Read from the courthouse steps, by a man with a list on a clipboard, because
nobody who took this town is going to stand there and say it out loud. Half the
town is in the street. Half of it is behind a curtain in a window.*

**Besieger Captain:** This is not a speech. I am going to read what applies to
you and then I am going to go get something to eat, because I have been up for
two days and I have earned a bad sandwich.

Here is what is happening. This town is under the authority of my office starting
this afternoon. That is not a speech either, it is the situation, and you can
see it from your kitchen.

The hospital and the clinic are open today and they stay open. Nobody takes
anything out of either of them, and if you see somebody doing that you come and
get one of the men in the blue armband and you say the word pharmacy or the word
clinic and they will handle it. Do not handle it yourself, just find the armband.

Water is the first thing we are doing. Not food, not money, not elections. The
mains are shut on the east side and they have been shut since before we came
here, and the pump that fixes it is forty miles north, and we are taking men and
trucks to it in the morning. If you have standing water or a well, that is
helpful and I want to know about it.

Food. There is a commissary at the rail yard and it opens tomorrow at seven and
it is not free and it is not going to be, but it is priced at what we could buy
it for in the open market and I have the receipts and anybody can read them.
Bring the receipt if you think we are cheating you. Bring it.

Nobody has to leave this town. I want to be plain about that because the stories
are already going around and they are not true yet. Nobody is being told to go.
If your neighbors left, that was their business and not a policy of mine.

The courts are shut until further notice and I will tell you when they open. I
am not going to hang anybody this week. I am not going to hang anybody next week
either. What I am going to do is find out who has been selling routes out of
this county, and I will tell you that now, before anybody asks me, because I
would rather be hated for the real thing than suspected of a made up one.

If somebody has been taking money to tell somebody where your convoys go, I
would like to talk to you. Tonight, quietly, and nobody has to know you came in.
If you would rather wait until I find it out on my own, that is your whole
right and I will not hold it against you.

One more thing and this is the important one. There are men on my staff who are
going to take things. Not out of malice, mostly out of the way everything is
handled right now when a hundred men show up in a town that has been eating out
of one hydrant. It is going to happen and I am telling you in advance so that
when it happens you do not think it is the way things work here.

If it happens, bring the armband man a description and a direction. If somebody
tells you it is the way things work, that is the moment you come and find me,
and I will deal with it myself, in front of people.

The school reopens in two weeks. It reopens with the same teachers it had and
the same books, and anybody who has been using that building for something else
is going to have to stop today.

There is a list on the door of this courthouse. Every one of those numbers is a
person, and half of them are people who live on this street and are standing
right here.

That is all of it. Go and get your water.

*He folds the clipboard. He does not say anything else, and a few people say so
loudly, and he does not turn around.*

## 4. DESERTERS

Six short scenes. Each one is two men at a guard line who do not want to fight
and are trying to work out whether anybody here will be reasonable about it.
Each one is solved differently, and two of them go badly, because that is what
happens at a guard line.

### 1. The ones who just walk up

*A road guard outside a besieged town, third night. Two men come out of the
tree line with their hands where everybody can see them. The guard is nineteen
and holding a rifle like a broom.*

**Deserter:** We are from the north line. We are walking out, not in, we were
on the wall and we came down.

**Guard:** You are deserting.

**Deserter:** We are leaving, yes. We did not sign anything, we just left.

**Guard:** That is deserting, that is what that is. Get in the truck.

**Deserter:** We are asking, not telling. We will put our weapons down right
here on the grass and we will work. We are good with a truck, look at us, we
are truck men.

**Guard:** I do not care. *He does not lower the rifle.* Somebody inside is
going to decide what you are and it is not going to be me.

*They stand in the grass for a long time. Nobody in the truck says anything.*

### 2. The one who brought a captain

*A man comes to the gate at four in the morning with a folded piece of paper and
a name. He does not ask to be searched, he asks to be announced.*

**Deserter:** Take this to whoever is in charge. My name is Dale Renfro and I
was a signal officer on the wall for eleven days.

**Guard:** We got a signal officer.

**Deserter:** I know. I was the one who kept the guns pointed the right way
for your side.

**Guard:** And?

**Deserter:** And I know where the second gate is and how they run the mortar
schedule and I will draw all of it on the back of this paper, and in exchange I
want to be walked to the rail yard and put on a truck that goes south, and I do
not care where.

*An hour later he is asleep in the back of the truck, and the paper is on the
captain's desk, and nobody has decided anything yet.*

### 3. The pair who argue about it

*Two men, one road, one guard post, and an argument that both of them think is
between them.*

**Deserter:** I am asking you to let us through and he is telling you not to,
and I would like you to understand that those are not the same thing.

**Guard:** You both just told me you were leaving your posts. Sit down.

**Deserter:** You do not have the authority to shoot two men for sitting down.

**Guard:** I have got a rifle and a dead lieutenant and a captain who tells me
every time what happened to the last men who came up that road. Sit down.

*The second man sits. The first one stands there for a while and then sits down
too, badly, like somebody who lost an argument he was enjoying.*

**Second Deserter:** I am going to say something and you are not going to like
it.

**Guard:** Go ahead.

**Second Deserter:** My cousin is in that town. He runs the pharmacy. That is
the entire reason I am out here, not politics, I have never had an opinion in my
life about any of it.

**Guard:** I know. Everybody out here has a cousin in that town. That is the
worst part of this and nobody warns you about it.

### 4. The one who is too useful

*A man walks in alone with his hands open. The sergeant asks him one question
and then goes and finds somebody with more authority, which is where the trouble
starts.*

**Deserter:** I know the garrison roster, the rotation, and which officers do
not sleep. I wrote part of the roster.

**Sergeant:** Why are you telling me this.

**Deserter:** Because I have four people in this town and I have been on the
other side of this wall for eleven days, and I am not going to be the reason
that happens.

**Sergeant:** That is either the best thing I have heard tonight or the worst,
and I honestly do not know yet, and you are going to stay right where you are
while somebody figures that out.

*He is still sitting in the truck in the morning. So are two men who told him
things that turned out to be true.*

### 5. The one who does not want to be let through

*A man comes to the guard line out of nowhere, alone, and asks for something
that makes no sense.*

**Deserter:** Do not let me in. I want you to shoot me or I want you to send me
back, and either one of those is fine, but do not put me in that truck.

**Guard:** You are not going in. Nobody is going anywhere.

**Deserter:** I had a truck stop on Franklin street at six this morning with the
engine running and I did not take it. That was the deal, that was the whole
deal, and I did not take it.

**Guard:** Who made you the deal?

**Deserter:** Their captain did. And he looked me in the face doing it, which is
the part I cannot get over, because he did not have to and he wanted me to know
he did not have to.

**Guard:** Then why are you telling a stranger at a gate?

**Deserter:** Because somebody is going to come looking for me and I would like
there to be one person in this county who knows where I am standing.

### 6. The one who tries to fight

*A young man comes out of the dark waving his rifle and shouting about his rights,
and everybody behind him understands immediately that this is the one that is
going to go wrong, and there is nothing they can do about it.*

**Deserter:** I am a citizen of the United States, I have a rifle I bought at
Walmart, and none of you get to tell me what I do!

**Guard:** Sir. Put it down. Sir. Put the rifle down.

**Deserter:** You are going to shoot me now. Say it. Look me in the eye and say
it, because I want somebody to say it.

**Guard:** *He has not moved the rifle the entire time.* I have got a captain
coming. Sit down, sir, and I will tell the captain you were reasonable.

**Deserter:** Reasonable. He wants me reasonable.

*He sits down. Nobody has to do anything. The captain takes eleven minutes and
the whole thing ends with a man being taken to a truck, and everybody at that
gate thinks about it for a long time afterward, including the guard, mostly
about the eleven minutes.*

## Notes For Implementation

- The three scenes worth building are 5, 2, and 4. A deserter mechanic that
  always succeeds removes the tension from the one time the player might want
  to use it, and the setting has already established that authorities here state
  what they will not do and then get checked on it.
- Scene 5 is the design target. Chambliss refusing to take a city by hunger is
  the established version of this, and a deserter who keeps a promise is the
  cheapest way to show the player that promises in this game are load bearing.
- Surrender terms map to the existing systems: money, food, metal, and prisoners
  from MARCH_AND_WAR.md, plus the tribute and vassalage outcome. `gates opened`
  is the peaceful siege outcome, `starved out` is the one that removes this
  whole file from play.
- The sack aftermath block is written against a system that does not exist yet.
  `garrison_conduct` exists as a field and nothing currently reads it. If the
  sack never ships, these lines still work as the post-assault interior of a
  breached town.
- The victor's proclamation deliberately promises four things the systems can
  enforce or measure: the clinic, the water, the price at the commissary, and
  nobody has to leave. Every one of those is a real field somebody can check.
