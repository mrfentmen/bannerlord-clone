# COMPANIONS

Eight recruitable named characters. They are the crew the player builds a war party out of. This is the companion roster, not the lord roster (that is `faction-leaders.md`).

Design rules these follow:

- Fictional people, real geography. No companion is a real person, and no companion's history replays a real war, attack, disaster, or crisis. Per CONSTITUTION.md section 6.
- Companions are full characters, not stat blocks with a name attached. They use the same attributes, focus, skills, perks, traits, and health as the player (CHARACTER.md sections 2 through 7).
- Every one of them fills one of the four party roles: Quartermaster, Scout, Surgeon, Engineer (FEATURES.md section 3). Two roles are covered twice, because a player who loses their surgeon in a siege needs somebody to step into that slot.
- Every personality is built from a real problem, not a stereotype. A Great Lakes quartermaster is obsessed with waste because his section feeds everyone and has nothing spare.
- Every companion has a flaw the player can protect, exploit, or lose them to. A companion with no flaw is a vending machine.
- Every companion has a personal goal, a wage, and a reason to walk (CHARACTER.md section 8).
- Banter is plain spoken American English. Short, specific, and about a real thing in the world. No one lines up a punchline, and nobody delivers a speech in the field.

A note on ages: these are written as birth years, not ages, so the aging system in CHARACTER.md section 9 works across every start year from 1950 to 2009. A companion born 1938 is 21 in 1959 and 61 in 1999. Where a companion's skills only make sense in a later tier, an earliest start year is given.

## 1. TEODORA NAKASHIMA

**Engineer. Pacific Compact. Born 1938.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | F |
| Home | Oakland, California, later Richmond and the port terminals |
| Party role | Engineer (primary) |
| Earliest start year | 1960, because her signature work is container handling equipment, which does not exist before then |
| Traits | Valor 3, Mercy 2, Honor 4, Generosity 3, Calculation 4 |
| Top skills | Engineering 5, Gunsmithing 4, Logistics 3, Driving 3, Tactics 2 |
| Wages | Medium, and she will not take more. She does not understand what she is being paid for |
| Personal goal | Build one thing that outlives the section, the war, and her |
| Talks like | Machines, and she is funnier about them than about people |
| Flaw | She has to be right, and she will take a working system apart to prove she is |

### Backstory

Teodora Nakashima grew up over her grandfather's machine shop in West Oakland, in the building where her father kept a shop of his own for twenty-six years and never once got a day off. The shop did dies, small motors, and the kind of repairs people bring in when they cannot go to a dealer and cannot explain the problem.

She was the one who learned the machines by listening to them. She can tell what is wrong with an engine by the way it idles. She could do it at nine. She still cannot tell you in words, and this has been the central problem of her life, because the people who paid for the repairs did not want an explanation, they wanted the machine to work.

She got into ports because the pay was real and the machines were enormous. For nineteen years she kept container cranes and conveyor lines running in a section that could not feed itself and needed every ton of food that came off a boat. She was good at it in a way that was hard to argue with, and eventually somebody in a good suit noticed, and Teodora found out that a supervisor is a mechanic with one fewer wrench and twice the paperwork.

Then the government of California came apart over a water fight, and the suit turned out to belong to a man who no longer had a country to issue a bad order from. The supervisors left with the structure and stayed gone. Teodora stayed because the cranes did not care who was paying her, they only cared whether the grease got done.

For three years after that she was the only person keeping the Port of Richmond's conveyor line alive, and she was doing it for a check that stopped clearing, and she was doing it anyway. A town of forty thousand people ate through that line. She thinks about that more than she talks about, and when she does talk about it she talks about the bearings.

### Personality

Teodora is unfailingly generous with parts, time, and other people's machines, and completely unwilling to be managed. She will rebuild your rifle, your truck, and your generator without being asked, and she will be short with you about anything that is not machinery. She is not a loner on purpose. She just finds that the number of things she wants to say is much smaller than the number of things she wants to say about that bearing.

Her real personality is this: she cannot accept that a thing works for no reason. Given any setup that functions, she will insist on knowing why, and if nobody can tell her, she will find out the expensive way, by taking it apart. She has been right every time. This has made her very good and very expensive.

She respects competence and only competence. A general who can tell her why a bridge holds up gets more of her than a general with a bigger rank. Flattery does nothing. Honesty does everything. She will tell the player that a plan is stupid to their face in front of their officers, and she will do it the same way in front of a leader, and this is why leaders either promote her or drive her off the map.

She has never been in charge of men and does not want to be. She is aware that this makes her a weak quartermaster and a good engineer, and she has made her peace with the trade.

### How you recruit her

**Source:** a notable in a Pacific port town who needs someone to keep a terminal running and cannot pay what Teodora is worth.
**Where to find her:** a decommissioned airfield on the edge of the city, in the control tower, where she has been living since the airport closed. She is not squatting, she is on a lease from a retired electrician, and she will explain the lease at length if you let her.
**The hook:** a town four miles inland is about to lose its only water pumping station, because the standby generator failed and the parts to fix it are sitting in a warehouse in a county that has since declared for the Mountain Alliance. Somebody has to go get them.
**The catch:** Teodora will not come along for a promise. She has been promised before. She comes if you personally escort the load and if you do not leave anybody behind to carry it.
**On completion:** she packs one toolbag and gets in the truck. She does not say thank you, she says "you can drive," which from her is the same thing.

### Banter

- I need the generator. I do not care that it works. I need to know why it works, and right now nobody does.
- Whoever welded that bracket did it on purpose. I am not saying it was a person. I am saying it was deliberate.
- You want it faster, you give me a better wrench. That is not a threat. That is just how the world is.

### Notes for implementation

- Her quest is the cheapest good example of the game: drive a truck, get a thing, come back. A player who skips it should be able to feel the difference in siege repair cost for the rest of the run.
- Calculation 4 with Mercy 2. She is not unkind, she is just sorting people into those who understand what she does and those who are in the way.
- Weakness to expose in play: she will break a working engine to satisfy a question. Give the player a siege where a working engine matters more than an understood one and watch her choose.

## 2. ROYCE CALLOWAY

**Scout. Mountain Alliance. Born 1941.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | M |
| Home | Casper, Wyoming, then anywhere with a road and no cell coverage |
| Party role | Scout (primary) |
| Earliest start year | 1950, works in any tier |
| Traits | Valor 3, Mercy 2, Honor 5, Generosity 2, Calculation 4 |
| Top skills | Scouting 5, Athletics 4, Rifles 3, Driving 2, Medicine 1 |
| Wages | Low, and asking for more genuinely offends him |
| Personal goal | Walk a line from the Gulf to Canada that nobody has walked in thirty years |
| Talks like | Weather, distance, and whatever is under the truck |
| Flaw | He cannot stay anywhere, and he calls it restlessness instead of what it is |

### Backstory

Royce Calloway spent eleven years as a game warden in the Mountain Alliance, which meant a uniform, a pickup with a bad heater, and a job that was mostly driving slow roads looking at things. He was good at it. He knew where the water was, where the elk crossed in October, and which Forest Service roads were signed as closed because they had washed out and which were signed as closed because the sign was a target.

When the federal forestry service stopped existing, his badge stopped meaning anything, and the people he had spent his career arresting started asking him for the same information for free. He gave some of it away. He has never been clear with himself about which parts.

His marriage ended the way things end out there, which is to say one of them moved to a city with a streetcar and the other one stayed. He has a daughter somewhere in Ohio whom he speaks to twice a year and writes to badly. He keeps every letter she ever sent him in the glovebox of the truck, which is a detail he would share with you and no one else.

He has been working as a guide, then as a ranger for ranch outfits who needed somebody to find holes in fences, then as a man who transports things for people who would rather not name themselves. The current job ended when the people he was transporting turned out to be the reason a bridge on a state route was burning. He helped four people off that bridge. He has not worked since.

He is the most competent outdoorsman the player will ever hire, and the reason the party will not hold a permanent base.

### Personality

Royce is quiet, and it is a real quiet, not a performance. He will sit through an eight hour council meeting and contribute nine words at the end. Those nine words are usually the only ones that matter, and he knows that, which makes him worse company and not better.

His humor is deadpan and it arrives several seconds late, and it is mostly about himself. He is not above a joke at the player's expense, but he will not make one at a wounded man or a hungry one.

He has a strong sense of the difference between what you promised and what you can actually deliver, and he holds the player to that difference rather than to the promise. He would rather be told no in a tavern than let somebody find out the hard way on a ridge.

The restlessness is the whole man. He stays about four months anywhere. When the party takes a town and offers him the governorship, he will refuse, and he will refuse badly, and he will be right that he is no good at it. He tells himself the reason is that he does not want to be tied down. He has a letter in his glovebox that suggests he may be wrong about this.

### How you recruit him

**Source:** a notable who owns a failing freight outfit and needs a guide he can trust with a convoy.
**Where to find him:** a closed Forest Service fire lookout on a mountain the party has to climb to reach, because every route through the lower pass is watched.
**The hook:** an outfitter has three trucks of seed grain and a mine survey crew due in the valley. Both will be sitting on the same road, and the road is going to be a fight. Somebody needs to go up first and decide what is happening, and then come back and say so in words the other people will accept.
**The catch:** he will only come if the player does exactly what he says on the way up. He does not care about being polite to the road, he cares about being early, quiet, and hydrated. A player who complains about it will lose him.
**On completion:** he comes down off the mountain, looks at the party's horses, and says one of them is badly shod. He stays.

### Banter

- Wind is out of the west. That means the pass is open and the afternoon is going to be ugly.
- I saw your camp before you saw my ridge. That is not a threat either. That is just Tuesday.
- Do not talk while we are moving. I am not being mean, you just make me miss things.

### Notes for implementation

- Honor 5 and Generosity 2 is an unusual pair and it is the point. He will keep an oath that costs him everything and he will not tip a starving man.
- Medicine 1 is deliberate. He can keep a man alive for three days on a mountain and he cannot do anything about infection. Pair him with Odessa Pryor and the combination is the strongest survival chain in the game.
- The four month timer should be a real system, not a vibe. A player who is a Wanderer with no territory has a different version of this pressure than a player ruling four towns.

## 3. ODESSA PRYOR

**Surgeon. Southern Compact. Born 1936.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | F |
| Home | New Orleans, later wherever the hospital still has staff |
| Party role | Surgeon (primary) |
| Earliest start year | 1950. Antibiotic availability is gated by medicine tier, not by her, so she is worth recruiting early and value grows with the era |
| Traits | Valor 4, Mercy 5, Honor 4, Generosity 3, Calculation 3 |
| Top skills | Medicine 5, Chemistry-adjacent Engineering 2, Leadership 3, Streetcraft 2, Athletics 1 |
| Wages | Low, and this is the one thing about her that will make the player angry |
| Personal goal | Train four people who can do what she does, before she is too tired to do it herself |
| Talks like | Symptoms first, feelings later, and only if the patient is conscious |
| Flaw | She has no margin in her own operating. She gives everyone else a spare and keeps nothing for herself |

### Backstory

Odessa Pryor came out of a flood in a way that decided the rest of her life. She was twenty, working the triage line at a shelter gymnasium in a city that was a foot deep in water with the power out, and she learned in four days that the thing that actually kills people is not the disaster. It is the hour between the disaster and the doctor.

She got into medicine the long way, through nursing, then a two year program at a school that folded when the state did, and then eleven years of work in a hospital in New Orleans that was at some point the only functioning thing between Texas and the Florida line.

She was very good. She was the one they sent for the ones nobody else wanted. She also noticed, for about a decade, that she was the only person in the building who could do what she could do, and that when she was sick, nobody could cover her, and that this was the most frightening thing about her life.

Around the time the Compact started calling up militias, her hospital ran out of both antibiotics and administration. She worked a stretch on a truck-based clinic in a parish that no longer had a government, and she watched the numbers of what she could treat go down while the numbers of what she could not went up.

She left the hospital when a militia colonel decided her supply of morphine would be better administered by a sergeant. She did not fight him, she simply stopped coming in, which she considers the most effective thing she has ever done to a man.

### Personality

Odessa is warm in a specific, unsentimental way. She is not kind to be liked. She is kind because the person in front of her is hurt and it costs her nothing to say the true thing.

She is unsentimental about her own body in a way that reads as hypocrisy to the player and is actually a symptom. She has a bad shoulder she will not mention. She has not slept properly in two years. She will not eat before a night operation, and if challenged on it she will say she has a good appetite, which nobody believes.

She never raises her voice at a frightened person, which is a skill she learned on purpose and which unnerves people who were expecting her to. She gets loud with officers who waste her people's time, and the switch is instant and total.

She takes a personal interest in every single person in the party, by name, including the player, and she remembers the previous wound. When a companion has a bad leg from four months ago she will bring it up in front of officers, because she does not consider that unkind.

Her goal is four trained surgeons, and the game should make this hard in a way that costs the player something. Every recruit she trains and then loses in a siege should be felt.

### How you recruit her

**Source:** a notable who is looking for someone to keep a field clinic running at a town that does not officially exist anymore.
**Where to find her:** a former department store in a town that has since been named something else on somebody's map. The ground floor is still set up as a ward. The mannequins are still in the window, which is the first thing the player sees and the only thing they will joke about for months.
**The hook:** the militia that took her hospital's morphine is holding a town nearby, and there are wounded on both sides of it, and the wounded on the other side are going to be dead by morning unless somebody with her skill treats them. The only way to get close enough is to go in under a flag and come out with whoever is still breathing.
**The catch:** she will do it, and she will be furious about it, and the player has to actually do the job. Sending an escort and staying at the truck will end the recruitment, permanently, and the notable will tell the player exactly why.
**On completion:** she comes out with eleven people and a stolen ledger of medical supplies, and she does not explain where the ledger came from until the third town.

### Banter

- You are going to hold still and you are going to let me do it. I have done this on a floor and a tailgate and once in a church. It hurts all three times.
- I do not ask how you got here. I ask when it started and whether you are taking anything for it.
- If you ever tell anybody I am nice, I will sew you up wrong on purpose. That is a joke. Half of that is a joke.

### Notes for implementation

- Mercy 5 with Valor 4 is the most readable companion stat block in the game and it is a good teaching tool. Generosity 3 is low on purpose. She is generous with care and ruthless about time.
- Wages should read as a bug to the player the first time she signs a contract. The compensation system in ECONOMY.md should be able to absorb an unhappy reaction like this. It is a good test of whether the economy communicates itself.
- Medicine 5 should reduce infection outcomes through the existing medicine tier pathway in ERA.md section 4, not through a separate companion bonus. No special cases in the code (CONSTITUTION.md section 1.2).

## 4. WENDELL BRAGG

**Quartermaster. Great Lakes Union. Born 1929.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | M |
| Home | Cedar Rapids, Iowa, then wherever a rail spur still runs |
| Party role | Quartermaster (primary) |
| Earliest start year | 1950, works in any tier |
| Traits | Valor 2, Mercy 2, Honor 3, Generosity 2, Calculation 5 |
| Top skills | Logistics 5, Trade 4, Engineering 2, Gunsmithing 3, Leadership 3 |
| Wages | Medium, paid on time, and he keeps his own copy of the account |
| Personal goal | Deliver one shipment of grain, in full, to a town that is hungry, and then be allowed to stop |
| Talks like | Inventory. He is not rude about it, he is just somewhere else |
| Flaw | He will not throw anything away, and this has already saved the party's lives twice |

### Backstory

Wendell Bragg spent thirty one years as a freight agent for a railroad cooperative in eastern Iowa, which is a job that sounds like nothing and is actually a job of holding a hundred unrelated promises at once and remembering which ones you made last.

He moved grain. Not metaphorically. He loaded it, he billed it, he found railcars for it during two harvest failures and a flood, and when the federal inspectors went away he kept moving it anyway, because grain sitting in a bin in October is a thing that has stopped being worth anything and he could not stand to look at it.

The cooperative he worked for was bought by a holding company out of the Atlantic Corridor, consolidated, and closed. The men running it after the buyout knew the shipping rates and nothing about the farmers. Wendell watched four seasons get mishandled by people with spreadsheets and better schools than his, and he quit in a way that involved a meeting where he said the word "ungodly" out loud more than once.

His retirement lasted eleven months. He went to work for a town that was running out of both food and ideas, he started keeping an inventory in a school notebook because there was nobody to keep it for, and he is now sitting on a warehouse of supplies that a militia is about to want.

He is sixty when the game starts, more or less, and he is the oldest character in the roster. This is on purpose. The player is going to be told, by several different people, that they are getting an old man instead of a soldier, and this should be annoying to the player at the time.

### Personality

Wendell is quiet in a way that reads as slow, and he is actually just not interrupting. He counts things. He counts the crates, then counts them again because the first number was a lie, and he is right that it was.

He treats every object as if it will be needed later, because sometimes it was. This has twice saved the party from a supply collapse he could see coming and nobody else would listen to. It has also filled a warehouse that the party has to move, which is the cost side of the same idea.

He has no interest in command and is suspicious of officers. He will be brusque with the player, and he will be right, and the party will function worse on supply for as long as the player resents him. His respect is earned by logistics, not by rank.

His politeness is real and his patience is not. He is a genuinely courteous man to his equals, which is to say to about four people he has met since 1970.

He is terrified of one specific thing, which is being the person who has to tell a town that the grain is not coming. He does not talk about it. If the player catches him at it, he will not be sympathetic and he will still come back to work in the morning.

### How you recruit him

**Source:** a notable who is a town administrator and has been quietly relieved of that duty and is not entirely sure what he is now.
**Where to find him:** a truck stop outside town, at a table with a school notebook, a pencil, and a manifest he is checking against a listing of the parking lot.
**The hook:** two grain elevators in the next county are about to be stripped by one side or the other. Somebody needs to get in, weigh what is there, and get two hundred tons on the road to a town that has eleven days left, and there is no one left who knows which elevator belongs to which farmer.
**The catch:** the player has to give him a wagon train, a manifest he can sign, and permission to make a call without asking. He will not be a quartermaster who requests. He will be a quartermaster who informs.
**On completion:** he writes the number down, tells the player the number, and adds that if the number is wrong he is the one who will be asked.

### Banter

- Nothing is wasted. Nothing. That broken wrench is a crowbar that has not realized it yet.
- I counted the crates. I counted them again because the first number was a lie.
- You want to move fast, you want a light party. You want to move fast and light, you want me, and you cannot have both.

### Notes for implementation

- Calculation 5 and Valor 2 is the correct build for a quartermaster and it is also the reason he is not a companion the player can send into a town and forget about. He is at his worst at a table with a map and his best in a room full of actual supplies.
- The notebook is a good UI opportunity. The party inventory screen could show his running count beside the game value, off by a little, and it would be the most human panel in the game.
- This character and Marisol Vega should be read together. She thinks about days of supply. He thinks about sacks. They have a professional disagreement already written, and the game never needs to have the argument.

## 5. DELPHINE MORROW

**Trade and Streetcraft runner. Atlantic Corridor. Born 1952.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | F |
| Home | Newark, New Jersey, then the parts of the Corridor that still import |
| Party role | None. She is a fifth wheel when roles are full, which is the point of her |
| Earliest start year | 1950. Later start years add radio, then early computers, and each one makes her job easier |
| Traits | Valor 2, Mercy 2, Honor 2, Generosity 2, Calculation 5 |
| Top skills | Streetcraft 5, Trade 5, Charm 4, Leadership 2, Scouting 3 |
| Wages | Paid in advance, on purpose, and she will explain why |
| Personal goal | Retire somewhere nobody has ever heard of, with a document that makes her untraceable |
| Talks like | Prices, and who is about to be desperate |
| Flaw | Every deal makes a new enemy and she keeps the list in her head and never forgets a name |

### Backstory

Delphine Morrow got her start at nineteen in a parking lot outside a Newark airport selling watches, which is where she learned the single most useful fact of her adult life, which is that a person standing in a hurry will agree to almost anything.

She worked her way into parts. Transistors, then meters, then pharmaceuticals, then the specific gray business of moving things across a state line that no longer had a state worth the name. She was never a smuggler in the movies sense. She was a woman who knew what a town was short of and who had a man in another town who could get it there by Thursday.

When the Corridor stopped being one country and started being six districts with three different tax stamps, she became more valuable in a week than she had been in ten years. She knows the licensing, the ports, the man at the yard, the doctor who writes the letter, and the official in Philadelphia who can be persuaded. She is one of about a dozen people in the country who can do all of that, and several of the other eleven would like her dead.

She is not important. She knows that too. She is a middle layer, and middle layers get removed when the top decides to build a shorter one, and she has been planning her exit since the day the federal institutions came apart.

She has the paperwork. It is in a box in a storage unit in Hagerstown under a name that is not hers. She is in the middle of getting to the box when the party finds her.

### Personality

Delphine is funny, fast, and comfortable with silence, and she uses all three on people. She is the companion who is most fun to talk to and least comfortable to have on a march.

She has no moral position and does not pretend to. She has done things she would not describe to a stranger, and she has a clear line she will not cross, and she will not tell you where the line is until you have been with her long enough that it stops being a question.

She is honest about money in a way that other characters find uncomfortable. If the player is paying badly she will say so, and she will say it in front of officers, and she will not be embarrassed about it.

She keeps a mental list of everyone who has crossed her, and it is very short, and it is permanent. The player can get on it, and it should be possible to be removed from it, slowly, over a long campaign, by a player who never once lies to her about money.

She has one loyalty and it is to Odessa Pryor, for reasons she will not explain until the campaign is deep. This should be a reveal and not a test.

### How you recruit her

**Source:** a notable who has a problem that cannot be shot, which is a useful first introduction to her.
**Where to find her:** outside a warehouse, at three in the morning, negotiating with two men who are not her business. She wins. She always wins, and the player should arrive after the interesting part.
**The hook:** the notable needs a specific machine part, a specific medicine, or a specific person, and it is inside a warehouse in a port district that is currently owned by somebody else. There is no way to take it by force that does not cost more than it is worth. Somebody has to go in with a cover story and a list of names and come out with it.
**The catch:** the player cannot be told the cover story in advance. She gives the player a name, a title, and one sentence to say if anyone asks. If the player improvises beyond the sentence, the run is over, in a way that feels like her fault and is not.
**On completion:** she comes with the part, three additional items nobody asked for, and a phone number she says is a pharmacy. That number is the first entry in a very long relationship.

### Banter

- I know a guy. That is not a recommendation, it is just the only fact I have got.
- Everything has a price and most of it is not money. Ask what they are protecting and then make me an offer.
- Do not look at the merchandise. People who look at the merchandise get remembered.

### Notes for implementation

- Calculation 5 and Honor 2 is the honest version of a smuggler. Honoring the player to her face while working them is not a contradiction to her, and the game should let a player who is paying well notice that her small favors are not random.
- Calculation 5 with all four other traits at 2 makes her the hardest companion in the roster to keep. This should be tested, not assumed. A player who is openly cruel should lose her, and losing her should close content.
- She is deliberately role-less so that the player has a reason to bring a fifth person into a party built for four. That surplus is the design. Do not invent a fifth party role for her.

## 6. CODY BRAVO

**Ranger. Lone Star Frontier. Born 1949.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | M |
| Home | Kerrville, Texas, then the ranches and the roads between them |
| Party role | Scout (secondary) |
| Earliest start year | 1955, when the ranch roads and the surplus vehicles he grew up on are in the model |
| Traits | Valor 5, Mercy 3, Honor 3, Generosity 4, Calculation 1 |
| Top skills | Athletics 5, Rifles 4, Scouting 4, Heavy Weapons 3, Close Combat 3 |
| Wages | Low, and he will be insulted if it goes up |
| Personal goal | Get to the coast. He has never seen it and he thinks about it constantly |
| Talks like | Horses, food, and what is going to happen next |
| Flaw | He does not have a plan past next week, and on a horse that has so far been a gift |

### Backstory

Cody Bravo grew up on a ranch outside Kerrville that had been in his family since before the war between the sections, which is a distinction he thinks is very funny and nobody else finds funny. He was on a horse before he could spell his own name. He learned to work cattle, weld a gate, and drive a truck with a trailer that weighed more than the truck at nineteen.

His uncle put him on a ranch crew hauling mineral rights survey gear in a convoy across west Texas, and for two years that was the best job Cody had ever had. Forty trucks, a hundred men, and a schedule so tight that a man who was late was fired at the roadside.

Then a survey crew found something, and within a year the crews were being told to hold position against people who wanted the same ground, and the two years turned into a different thing. He left during a fire he does not talk about. He left before the war, in a truck, at night, and got as far as Las Cruces.

He has worked odd jobs and rode fence for other people's ranches since, and he is good at all of it and restless in all of it. He tried the service twice. He did not like being told to do something he already knew how to do better, which is the single most common reason good soldiers quit.

He is the youngest character in the roster by a wide margin and the only one the player can plausibly be closer in age to. He is also the one most likely to die in a fight he could have avoided, and this is a real problem, not a character joke.

### Personality

Cody is warm, fast, funny, and the first person in the party to make the player feel like the whole enterprise might be fun. He is generous, he buys rounds, he remembers birthdays, and he will walk into a burning building for a dog.

He is genuinely bad at planning and he will say so cheerfully, and then improvise something excellent under pressure, and the pattern is going to get the party killed within a year if nobody manages him. Calculation 1 means the game should read his decisions as readable to others but not to him.

He is loyal past the point where loyalty is smart. If the player loses, Cody is the one still with the banner, and this is a trait the player should be warned about and then allowed to exploit.

He is a bad quartermaster, a mediocre strategist, and the best natural horseman the player will ever have. In a game about supply and logistics, having someone in the party who is a liability on both and an asset in the field is the interesting part.

He has never seen the ocean. This comes up, and it comes up more.

### How you recruit him

**Source:** a notable who is a ranch owner trying to get a herd and a water right moved a hundred and forty miles and has lost two crews to the road.
**Where to find him:** a bar in a town that is mostly one street, at four in the afternoon, standing on a chair so he can see the door.
**The hook:** a herd, a water truck, and a stretch of highway that is going to be fought over in a week, and a rancher who needs somebody out front who can shoot, ride, and think of a third option when the shooting stops working.
**The catch:** the player has to let him make the plan. This is the only companion whose recruitment is about giving up control. A player who overrides him in the field has recruited him and will lose him within a season.
**On completion:** he comes, he stays up all night with the animals, and in the morning he asks the party captain what the plan is, which is the first time in his life he has asked anybody that.

### Banter

- That horse does not like you. I am just telling you so you do not get surprised.
- I do not have a plan. I have a direction and a good horse, and that has got me a long way.
- You are all tense. City people get like that out here. Breathe, eat something, it helps more than you think.

### Notes for implementation

- Calculation 1 is the most important number in this file. The AI in AI.md needs a read on a companion with no forward plan, and the answer should be that the party gets a warning from the character rather than a penalty from the system. He is the one who says the obvious thing nobody said.
- Pair him with Delphine and there is friction worth writing. She thinks he is a boy. He thinks she is a snitch who is going to get everybody killed. The argument should be in the game because both of them are half right.
- Valor 5 with Mercy 3 is the most likely companion to die of something stupid. The difficulty setting in CHARACTER.md section 7 that reduces the player to knockout and capture should be extended to the player choosing to make companion death rare, and Cody is the character most affected by that choice.

## 7. AUGUST RAMEY

**Heavy Weapons. Atlantic Corridor. Born 1934.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | M |
| Home | Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, then wherever the metal is |
| Party role | Engineer (secondary), and a gunner when it matters |
| Earliest start year | 1960. His first work is mounted weapons on carriers, which sets the floor |
| Traits | Valor 4, Mercy 2, Honor 4, Generosity 2, Calculation 3 |
| Top skills | Heavy Weapons 5, Gunsmithing 5, Engineering 4, Automatic Weapons 3, Leadership 2 |
| Wages | High, and he will point out that it is not high enough |
| Personal goal | Finish one weapon that works every time, which he has been trying to do since he was nineteen |
| Talks like | A man talking about a machine he is good at, which is the only time he is relaxed |
| Flaw | He will not accept a limit on his own body, and the party is going to need him to |

### Backstory

August Ramey came out of the Pittsburgh shops, and specifically out of a machine shop that made ordnance hardware for other people, which is a phrase that covered a great deal of what the yard did during the war years.

He lost most of his hearing in his left ear in a flash in 1961, standing too close to a press he had told three people not to stand close to. The compensation took four years. The four years are the reason he is not a foreman and the reason he will not be a foreman.

He moved to ordnance work because it paid, and he stayed because he found the one thing he is good at, which is the difference between a weapon that works most of the time and a weapon that works every time. That gap is very small. Most of the war was fought with weapons inside that gap. Gus is one of about forty people in the country who understands exactly what is in that gap and how to close it.

He lost his hearing the year he started taking pride in it, and he has told people the wrong version of that story more than once. The wrong version is that he was reckless. The real version is that he was standing next to a man who needed him there.

He has been in three militia units and left two of them over ammunition handling, and he has never been in trouble with a superior officer in his life, which is a specific achievement for a man with his mouth.

### Personality

Gus is proud, direct, and rude to people he has not measured, and the measuring is quick and based entirely on whether they know what he is talking about. He is not looking for a fight. He is waiting to find out if he has one, and he would prefer not to.

He is much softer about equipment than about people. He has been known to bury a firearm that could be repaired, and he will explain why at length, and the explanation is always better than the player's objection.

He is loud in a way that reads as confidence and is actually deafness compensation. He turns the wrong way, he does not always hear a name the first time, and he will ask you to repeat it instead of pretending. Companions and officers both make the mistake of repeating it as a question, and he hates that more than anything.

He will not stop. This is the whole risk of him. He works a launcher position for eleven hours, he hides that his shoulder is gone, and he will be standing in that position when the party needs him and then he will not be.

He is the companion the player will be most tempted to trust with a siege wall, and the reason the game should make that trust occasionally fail is that he is right about his own body and wrong about it in exactly the same way every time.

### How you recruit him

**Source:** a notable who is a defense contractor with a problem, which is the best way to meet this character.
**Where to find him:** a rail yard on the edge of the city, working on a mount that a militia pulled off a vehicle three counties over, with no supervision and no approval and a crane operator who has been letting him help.
**The hook:** a section leader needs a fixed position that has a real weapon behind it, and the parts to make it work exist in pieces across three states, and the man who understands all three is not in the army, the militia, the navy, or the mines. He is at a rail yard in Pittsburgh and he is doing this as a favor to a crane operator.
**The catch:** Gus does not want a command and will say so. The player has to be willing to have him in the party without ever giving him the job title he wants, and to let him keep the rail yard job as a courtesy, which is a small cost with a large morale effect.
**On completion:** he comes, he works on the party's vehicles for a week without being asked, and then tells the player that the party's leading vehicle is a death trap and the player can do whatever he wants about it.

### Banter

- Give me four minutes with this and it will do what I tell it. Maybe three.
- I am not tired. I am fifty-something, which is different, and if anybody says otherwise they are lying.
- Everybody loves the launcher. Nobody loves the guy carrying the ammo. That is a problem with people, not with the launcher.

### Notes for implementation

- Heavy Weapons 5 and Gunsmithing 5 is the strongest combat and repair pairing in the roster, and it should gate behind the era properly. A 1950 start should not be able to hire him, which is a real loss the player feels.
- The deafness should be modeled as a real, permanent, small penalty in the data, not a quip. Health and injuries are data fields (CHARACTER.md section 10). A hearing penalty that reduces the chance of hearing a command during a battle is a good cause chain for the Why panel.
- He is the natural third member of a siege party with Teodora and Odessa. The three of them together should be able to take a town that the player cannot take any other way, at a supply cost the player notices.

## 8. LORNA QUINN

**Party organizer and negotiator. Great Lakes Union. Born 1945.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Sex | F |
| Home | Flint, Michigan, then the union halls and the radio towers of the Great Lakes |
| Party role | None. She is a fifth wheel and she knows it |
| Earliest start year | 1965, when broadcast radio and television are the tools she actually used, and a 1950 run should feel her absence |
| Traits | Valor 3, Mercy 3, Honor 3, Generosity 4, Calculation 4 |
| Top skills | Leadership 5, Charm 5, Diplomacy 5, Trade 3, Streetcraft 3 |
| Wages | Medium, and she negotiates it every time it changes |
| Personal goal | Win one labor dispute from the other side, without anyone dying, in a section that will need labor to survive |
| Talks like | Names. She remembers every name she has ever been given and she uses them as a weapon |
| Flaw | She turns every conversation into a recruiting pitch, including the ones with the dead |

### Backstory

Lorna Quinn organized factory workers in Michigan for eleven years, which means she spent eleven years in rooms where everybody was losing and somebody had to be calm. She won four of the disputes she walked into and lost one badly, and the one she lost is the one she talks about.

She was good enough that the local UMW staff tolerated her, and then the regional office put her in a role that had no title and no budget, which is a polite way of getting rid of a person who is hard to argue with.

When the federal labor framework stopped existing, she did what she had always done. She went where there was no organization and built one. In two years she had a functioning grievance committee in a Great Lakes city that had no government, which is the most useful thing anyone in that section did that decade, and it cost her the local UMW's endorsement and about forty friends.

She was a radio host for eleven months on a station out of Milwaukee that no longer exists, and she is very clear that the radio years were the important ones, because a person who hears a name read out over a wide area believes in it, and a person who is merely told a name in a hall does not.

Her son is a section officer in the Great Lakes Union now. She has not seen him in two years. She knows exactly which of his policies she thinks are wrong and she has never once said so to anyone, including the player, unless the player asks directly.

She is recruited into a party that already has a leader, which is a step down for her, and she takes it anyway, and she is not pleasant about it.

### Personality

Lorna is the most socially capable person the player will ever meet, and she uses it on the player, and it works, and the player should notice that it works.

She has a big voice and a large memory for people. She will call the player by name in a way that implies a history they do not have. She will find out the name of the player's cook within a day.

She is generous, and her generosity is strategic, and both of these are true at all times, and she has never once pretended otherwise. A player who is hungry on a march will find she has already arranged for that to stop being true.

Her flaw is scale. Every interaction is an opportunity to build a membership, and she does this in town halls, in hospitals, in the player's own party, and in a conversation with a wounded man in the back of a truck. The companion who is most moved by the player's cause is the one actively signing up the party's soldiers.

She is a terrible quartermaster and an unmatched negotiator. In a game where the ruler's power comes from holding towns and keeping oaths, she is the most valuable character in the roster for a player who is playing politics, and almost useless for a player who is not.

She and Delphine Morrow do not get along. Lorna thinks Delphine organizes people, Delphine thinks Lorna performs. Both are right and neither will say which.

### How you recruit her

**Source:** a notable who is a town administrator trying to hold a workforce together after the head of the plant disappeared, and who has heard of the player from somebody.
**Where to find her:** the floor of a factory that has been shut for a month, in front of about two hundred people, on a stack of pallets, telling them what she is going to do about it. She is not waiting for the player. The player is the interruption, and she should be visibly annoyed about it.
**The hook:** the two hundred people are skilled, organized, and about to leave the town for work in a city that does not need them. Somebody has to offer them a reason and a contract, and the party's own labor needs are the reason, which is a transaction neither of them is going to describe honestly.
**The catch:** she will join to get one specific thing into writing, a paid contract with a named rate, a named period, and an actual grievance procedure. The player has to sign it or walk away. A player who sends an officer to negotiate this gets a worse deal and a colder version of her for the rest of the run.
**On completion:** she comes with a list of two hundred names and eleven of them can shoot, and the party has a permanent recruitment pool in one city, which is a mechanic no other companion provides.

### Banter

- I know your name. I know the name of the man who sent you, and I know which of the two of them is actually in charge.
- You want me to talk to those men? I will talk to those men. I will also be recruiting two of them by Thursday.
- A speech is just a list of things you need that you have not written down yet.

### Notes for implementation

- Leadership 5 with Charm 5 and Diplomacy 5 is the best social stat block in the game and it is deliberately the least useful in a fight. The game needs at least one companion whose value is entirely political, or every party ends up being a siege party.
- Her town recruitment pool is the strongest single companion perk in the roster. It should be an explicit data effect on that town, read by the recruitment system, with a cause log row, so the player can trace it in the Why panel (CONSTITUTION.md section 2.2).
- The son is a late campaign reveal and not a quest. There is no version of this content where the player gets a decision prompt about him, and that restraint is what makes it land.

## 9. WHAT ALL EIGHT HAVE IN COMMON

1. **Every one is recruited by doing a job, not by talking to them.** Eight out of eight turn on the player completing a real task: a truck run, a mountain climb, a field surgery under a flag, a grain manifest, a warehouse entry, a cattle drive, a rail yard negotiation, a labor contract. A player who has not done the job cannot have the companion, and a player who does it badly can lose the companion permanently.
2. **Every one of them is a working adult with a job they were leaving.** None of them is a vagrant and none of them has been adrift for years. They are all mid-career and between things, which is what a collapse actually produces, and it means the player is recruiting expertise rather than finding a wanderer.
3. **Every one costs something to keep.** Wages, a title the player cannot give, a harbor they will not stop visiting, a list of enemies in a rented head, a grief about a fire, a son in uniform. Poor treatment, unpaid wages, and choices that clash with traits should all be able to end a companion's run, and at least one of the eight should be able to leave the player a note.
4. **Every one has a goal that is not victory.** Deliver the grain, reach the ocean, train four surgeons, build one thing that outlives the war, walk a line nobody has walked, get the document from the storage unit, stop being the man who says the grain is not coming, do not be useless to her own side. If the player's campaign succeeds, most of these goals go unfulfilled, and that is the intended texture of a long run.
5. **Every one of them should be arguable.** A player who reads the roster should be able to construct a case against all eight. Gus is a burden, Cody is a liability, Delphine is the problem, Lorna is a snake, Odessa is underpaid, Wendell is a hoarder, Royce will not stay, Teodora will break your engine. Each of those readings is wrong, in a specific way, and the game should not correct the player.
6. **The roster covers the four roles without covering the party.** Two Scouts and two Engineers, one Quartermaster, one Surgeon, and two characters with no role at all. The party is built for four. Bringing a fifth is a real choice with a real cost in wages and food, and the two role-less characters exist to make the player make that choice.
7. **They are recruited by a notable, not found in a bar.** This is QUESTS_AND_NOTABLES.md section 2 applied. A notable recommends them, which means the player builds a relationship with a local power broker to get a crew member, which means the crew is a consequence of the political game and not a separate one.
